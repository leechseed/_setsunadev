# MCDP 8 — Information (Knowledgebase Summary)

## Abstract
MCDP 8, *Information* (21 June 2022), establishes the Marine Corps' doctrinal foundation for information as a warfighting function and as an instrument of maneuver warfare. It argues that information is central to every activity Marines undertake, that the modern information environment is global, instant, and persistent, and that competitors treat information as central to their way of war, so that no information advantage can be assumed without competing and fighting for it. The publication defines information in its several doctrinal senses, describes the information environment and competitor approaches to it, sets out a theory of information built on informational power and the attributes of war, and defines the information warfighting function as the means of generating, preserving, denying, and projecting information to create and exploit three information advantages: systems overmatch, prevailing narrative, and force resiliency. It demands that commanders treat information as their business, that every Marine recognize their role in it, and that the function be integrated with all other warfighting functions across the competition continuum.

## TL;DR
- Information is a Marine Corps warfighting function whose purpose is to create and exploit information advantages in pursuit of mission objectives.
- Marines must approach information with a maneuver warfare mindset; no information advantage exists without competing and fighting for it.
- The information environment is global, instant, and persistent; it compresses the levels of warfare so that local actions can have strategic effect.
- Competitors blur the divide between peace and war and employ unrestricted and irregular methods, systems confrontation, and destruction to gain information advantages.
- Informational power is the use of information, narratives, and technical means to advance interests and achieve objectives.
- The four functions of information are generation, preservation, denial, and projection, and they must be planned and used in combination.
- The three information advantages Marines seek are systems overmatch, prevailing narrative, and force resiliency.
- Every military objective has a cognitive (human) and a functional (machine) component, each exploitable by direct or indirect approaches.
- Deception is an information activity in three categories: tactical deception, joint military deception, and deception in support of operations security.
- Information is the commander's business, and all Marines have a role in it.
- Institutionalizing information requires integration with the other warfighting functions, information in planning, command narratives, and doctrine, training, education, and force design that keep pace with the environment.

## Section-by-Section Breakdown

### Foreword / Preface
- Information is the foundation of all human interaction and, in a contest of hostile wills, is as powerful a tool as any weapon system; competitors use it as both target and weapon.
- The publication introduces a conceptual framework for understanding and employing the information warfighting function across the competition continuum and all warfighting domains, using vignettes to illustrate enduring theory and principles.
- It is not a checklist; it is to be read cover to cover as a baseline for all Marines, must be updated frequently as the information environment changes, and holds that every Marine has a role in information.

### Chapter 1 — The Nature of Information
This chapter frames information as both a source of exploitable advantage and a source of disadvantage to be overcome, describes the information environment, and explains how competitors approach information.

#### Information Explained
- Information as an instrument of national power operates alongside the diplomatic, military, and economic instruments; the Marine Corps plays a supporting role in advancing US interests through it.
- Command and control and intelligence use the word differently: MCDP 6 treats information as a range from raw data to knowledge and understanding; MCDP 2 treats it as unevaluated material from which intelligence is derived.
- The information environment is the global competitive space spanning the warfighting domains where all operations depend on information, including all social, cultural, psychological, technical, and physical factors bearing on decision making.
- An information advantage is an exploitable condition resulting from one actor's ability to generate, preserve, deny, and project information more effectively than another; three types are sought: systems overmatch, prevailing narrative, and force resiliency.
- Information is a warfighting function distinguished by its purpose: to create and exploit information advantages in pursuit of mission objectives, integrated with the other functions for unity of effort.

#### How Important is Information?
- Information was important in the industrial age but is central to life in the information age because of societal dependence on digital information and networks.
- Dependence creates vulnerability, and vulnerability creates opportunity to seize information advantages; competitors can expose institutions from the sanctuary of their borders.
- Competitors use digital media to spread disinformation and intensify divisions, a form of continuous hostile social manipulation that will grow with artificial intelligence and synthetic media.
- Because information dependency is shared by all information-age militaries, the United States must never assume an inherent information superiority.

#### Characteristics of the Information Environment
- Instant and persistent global visibility: every Marine's words and actions can become globally visible within hours, with implications for operations security, readiness, narrative, and resiliency.
- Compressed levels of warfare and battlespace: local tactical actions can become regional or global incidents; commanders must consider strategic implications and train Marines to promote a credible narrative.
- Truth, trust, and belief: reliance on technology makes trust in sources the basis for belief; misinformation is false information not intended to cause harm, disinformation is false information deliberately intended to cause harm.
- Information volume, velocity, and value: the side faster at acquiring, processing, and exploiting information makes faster and better decisions and drives tempo; value depends on timing, accuracy, relevance, cultural context, and trust.
- Thinking and non-thinking processes: human cognition and decision making versus algorithmic or machine processes; both take information as input and provide two avenues of approach for creating effects.

#### How Competitors Approach Information
- Competitors value information as central to their way of war and see themselves in a constant state of struggle, blurring the divide between peace and war that US thinking tends to preserve.
- The competition continuum is the US term for constant interaction spanning every action below and above the threshold of armed conflict; "opponent" denotes any competitor, adversary, or enemy on that continuum.
- Competitors seek to win without fighting through unrestricted and irregular methods: China's Three Warfares (public opinion and media, psychological, and legal warfare) and Russia's undeclared irregular warfare and reflexive control.
- Competitors pursue information systems confrontation and destruction with technologies of decision advantage, technologies of disruption, and technologies of destruction targeting command and control, intelligence, logistics, and weapons systems.
- Chapter conclusion: information is fundamental to all human interaction and to every Marine activity; competitors will strive to put the United States at information disadvantage; this is why the Marine Corps adopted the information warfighting function and why Marines must approach information with a maneuver warfare mindset.

### Chapter 2 — The Theory of Information
This chapter develops a theory of information grounded in informational power, relates information to the attributes of war, defines the information advantages and the four functions of information, and explains the cognitive and functional components of objectives and the role of deception.

#### Informational Power
- War, like all competition, is about the distribution of power through a contest of wills; information is a form of power.
- Informational power is the use of information, narratives, and technical means to advance interests and achieve the Nation's objectives, employed in concert with diplomatic, military, and economic power.
- Information has a special relationship with combat power: the side that can manipulate, deny, or destroy the information an opposing military system needs, while preventing the opponent from doing the same, gains significant advantages.
- When combat power is not favorable to the mission, Marines still harness information by influencing perceptions, decisions, and behaviors.

#### Information and the Attributes of War
- Ambiguity and uncertainty cause friction; opponents use ambiguity as a weapon to cause hesitation, and Marines must create it in opponents while defending against it.
- Complexity, fluidity, and disorder degrade situational understanding and demand flexibility of thought.
- The human dimension is vital because war is a contest of wills and a social phenomenon; the ultimate aim of an information advantage is to influence an opponent's will.
- All military activities are physically detectable and communicate a message, intentional or not, affecting observers' moral and mental factors; moral and mental factors are targets for enemies and can be defended with tangible means of information.

#### What is an Information Advantage?
- At the strategic level, the United States gains information advantages by defending vital information and infrastructure while persuading or compelling favorable perceptions and behaviors; the Marine Corps contributes to the narrative that the United States is the strategic partner of choice.
- At the operational and tactical levels, an information advantage is an exploitable condition resulting from generating, preserving, denying, and projecting information more effectively than another actor.
- Systems overmatch is the technical advantage of one side over another yielding fires, intelligence, maneuver, logistics, force protection, or command and control advantages.
- The prevailing narrative is the credible narrative that resonates most with the intended audience, yielding a public opinion or perception advantage.
- Force resiliency is the ability to resist, counter, and prevail against reconnaissance, technical disruption, and malign activity such as misinformation, disinformation, and propaganda; commanders instill an "assault through the ambush" mentality.
- These three can lead to other information-based advantages such as decision speed, surprise, faster targeting, tempo, or psychological advantage.

#### Functions of Information
- The functions are mutually supporting and overlapping and must always be planned and used in combination.
- Information generation gains and maintains access to the information environment, builds awareness of threats, vulnerabilities, and opportunities, holds systems at risk, and creates the information needed to plan and conduct operations.
- Information preservation protects and defends friendly information, systems, and networks; builds and maintains the historical record; and recognizes and dispels misinformation and disinformation.
- Information denial disrupts or destroys the information an opponent needs to understand, decide, or act, by offensive means or by passive signature management, operations security, and communication discipline.
- Information projection communicates, transmits, or delivers information to inform, influence, or deceive an observer or system, including by observable physical actions such as demonstrations and exercises.

#### The Cognitive and Functional Components of Military Objectives
- Every objective has a cognitive (human) component and a functional (machine) component that can be exploited directly or indirectly.
- Exploiting the cognitive component aims at thinking processes: perception, decision making, and will; it requires understanding how the opponent's human-machine system receives, processes, and uses information.
- Exploiting the functional component aims at non-thinking processes: the system's ability to function, through information and systems confrontation and destruction leading to systems overmatch.
- The direct approach presents information or damages the system itself; the indirect approach attacks moral and mental factors or supporting capabilities the system depends on.

#### Information and Deception
- Deception is central to competitor thinking and to how Marines think about competition and war; it is an information activity that deceives the mind, the machine, or both, and is most effective when exploiting ambiguity.
- Tactical deception is conducted by any unit to gain surprise through camouflage, decoys, feints, demonstrations, ruses, and displays; the greater the surprise, the smaller the force required and the fewer the casualties; Marines adopt an "ambush mindset."
- Joint military deception supports operational-level campaigns, is conducted within approved authorities, and requires staff integration and tight security.
- Deception in support of operations security targets foreign intelligence entities with false, confusing, or misleading indicators and is a form of information denial.
- Chapter conclusion: information is power; the crux of the theory is creating and exploiting information advantages across the competition continuum by exploiting the cognitive and functional components of threat systems; advantages result from generating, preserving, denying, and projecting information more effectively than the opponent.

### Chapter 3 — Effective Use of Information
This chapter establishes principles of the information warfighting function and explains how the functions of information are applied to create the three information advantages across the competition continuum.

#### Principles of the Information Warfighting Function
- The information environment is global and enduring; there is no turning it off, and there is always an opportunity to gain or lose an advantage in it.
- All warfighting domains apply; information advantages can be created and exploited in every domain and combined across domains for combined-arms effects; most advantages are temporary.
- Information is the commander's business; commanders use it to induce ambiguity, uncertainty, and friction in opponents and must integrate it deliberately into plans and orders.
- All Marines have a role; every Marine's presence, posture, and actions communicate a message open to interpretation, requiring operations security and disciplined communication.
- Commanders choose between direct approaches (strength against strength) and indirect approaches (strength against critical vulnerabilities) to create and exploit information advantages.

#### Achieving Information Advantages
- The four functions of information align with the three advantages as a general guide for planning and operations.
- Battle for systems overmatch: gain and maintain access to opponent systems, preserve friendly information and information-dependent capabilities, actively deny information, and project information by overload or deception; the work is continuous on every point of the competition continuum.
- Competition for the prevailing narrative: narratives give meaning to facts; Marines nest a credible command narrative within existing cultural narratives, back it with visible action, measure its effectiveness, preserve it through consistent proactive messaging and archived unit history, and deny the opponent's narrative by exposing discrepancies and disrupting distribution.
- Building force resiliency: opponents impose cognitive disruption (disinformation, propaganda) and functional disruption (cyberspace and electromagnetic attack); resiliency requires understanding friendly vulnerabilities, rehearsing recovery, correcting narrative missteps transparently, media literacy and awareness of cognitive bias, and continuing the mission on commander's intent when degraded.

#### Information Advantages across the Competition Continuum
- Competition is continuous and may involve cooperation, irregular competition, and armed conflict simultaneously; Marines apply the function on all points of the continuum.
- Shaping the security environment: military engagement, security cooperation, and credible force presentation are inherently information-centric and communicate reassurance to allies and resolve to adversaries; garrison and community events support the Marine Corps narrative.
- Competition below the threshold of armed conflict is inherently information-centric; achieving advantage may require proactive use of capabilities normally reserved for wartime, under the right permissions.
- In armed conflict and general warfare, Marines seek systems overmatch, a favorable prevailing narrative, and force resiliency to attack the enemy's ability and will to fight, while preserving friendly data and networks with forceful offensive and defensive action.
- Chapter conclusion: the purpose of the function is to create and exploit the three information advantages by maneuvering in all domains and the information environment to generate, preserve, deny, and project information more effectively than opponents; all Marines, and particularly commanders, use information to shape the security environment and achieve objectives.

### Chapter 4 — Institutionalizing Information
This chapter explains how the Marine Corps makes information a primary instrument of maneuver warfare by distinguishing the function, integrating it with the other warfighting functions, and embedding it in planning, narrative, training, education, and force development.

#### Distinguishing the Information Warfighting Function
- All warfighting functions are distinct and mutually supporting; the information function's purpose is to create and exploit systems overmatch, prevailing narrative, and force resiliency.
- The function provides commanders and staffs a framework to understand and leverage the pervasive nature of information and to integrate generation, preservation, denial, and projection across all phases of operations.

#### Information and the Other Warfighting Functions
- Command and control requires assured access to trusted, timely, relevant information; resiliency to disruption combined with mission-type orders and commander's intent preserves tempo; trust in information and systems is essential.
- Maneuver has inherent informational aspects: signaling intent, demonstrating capability, driving tempo, and sometimes maneuvering to gain information by stimulating the enemy to act.
- Fires create physical (functional) or cognitive effects; doctrine calls for using fires more for cognitive effect on the enemy's will than for physical effect; nonlethal fires project or deny information.
- Intelligence informs understanding of enemy intent and of friendly, neutral, and threat information networks and decision making, and supports assessment of information effects.
- Logistics sets the outer limit of what is operationally possible, requires assured trusted information, and must manage its highly visible signatures.
- Force protection preserves the force through signature management, cybersecurity, and operations security, and protection against hostile information activities contributes to force resiliency.

#### Information in the Planning Process
- Planning must include functional and detailed information-planning considerations.
- Any unit or element can be used to generate, preserve, deny, or project information; information must be as ingrained in the Marine Corps Planning Process as any other warfighting function.

#### Prioritizing Information
- Commanders must prioritize activities that place information considerations at the forefront; communicating a strategy's existence, aligned with visible posture and readiness, reinforces the message.
- A unit tasked to create informational effects may be the main effort; deception operations exemplify information-focused effort.
- Prioritizing information includes monitoring the effects of messages and actions and adjusting command actions on that feedback.

#### Leveraging All Capabilities, and Ally and Partner Networks
- All capabilities, including those of allies and partners, must be leveraged; strengthening partnerships strikes directly at competitors' aim to divide the United States from its allies.
- Marines must combine new technology with legacy capabilities, especially in cyberspace, electromagnetic spectrum operations, and space, in keeping with combined arms and maneuver warfare.

#### Using Training Exercises for Real-World Effect
- Competitors readily observe exercises; commanders must understand how an adversary will observe and then design exercises to communicate messages and shape or deny observation, managing visible, administrative, electromagnetic, and digital signatures.
- Overseas exercises with allies and partners should be treated as tactical missions; they offer opportunities to amplify or suppress indicators but carry increased operations security and force protection risk.
- Adversaries exploit exercise imagery for false stories afterward; persistent presence and accurate unit histories build resiliency against this.

#### Practicing Discipline in the Information Environment
- Because the environment compresses the levels of warfare, individual behavior can significantly affect the mission and the Marine Corps.
- Every engagement in digital media must be evaluated for unintended consequences and operations security; Marines must consider second- and third-order effects of communications and actions wherever they are.

#### Command and Service Narrative
- A command narrative is broader than commander's intent; it anchors all public communication, exercises, and operations for internal and external audiences and, done well, inspires allies and deters competitors.
- Narrative development is a staff process beginning in problem framing, nested under US strategic and higher command narratives and guided by public affairs guidance, with supporting themes and messages.
- The Marine Corps cultivates a Service narrative to remain relevant to the public and connect Marines; command narratives must support it.

#### Doctrine, Training, and Education
- Doctrine provides the philosophy and framework; training develops proficiency; education cultivates understanding, creativity, and judgment; information doctrine builds on maneuver warfare.
- Training has two focus areas: basic information skills common to all Marines, and technical skills for Marines who apply information capabilities.
- Education must develop understanding of the information contest, of how competitors use information and technology, and of friendly vulnerabilities and how opponents exploit them.

#### Force Development and Design
- Enduring competition and a dynamic information environment demand institutional agility.
- Acquisition must keep pace with continual change, and the Marine Corps must aggressively test, experiment, and wargame new ideas and technologies in every domain.
- Chapter conclusion: Marines must embrace the information warfighting function as a core element of planning, training, and education; leverage exercises for maximum effect; integrate information with all warfighting functions; maintain command narratives; and keep pace with the environment, emerging technologies, and evolving threats.

## Mental Models

### Model 1 — Generate, Preserve, Deny, Project
- **Model:** Information advantage results from performing the four functions of information more effectively than the opponent, in combination.
- **Use:** Frame any unit's information activity by asking which function it performs and which of the three advantages it serves.

### Model 2 — Three Information Advantages
- **Model:** Systems overmatch, prevailing narrative, and force resiliency are the exploitable conditions the information warfighting function exists to create.
- **Use:** Test plans by whether they create or exploit at least one of the three.

### Model 3 — Cognitive and Functional Components
- **Model:** Every objective has a human (thinking) component and a machine (non-thinking) component, each attackable by direct or indirect approaches.
- **Use:** Generate options against any target by working the four cells: cognitive-direct, cognitive-indirect, functional-direct, functional-indirect.

### Model 4 — Compressed Levels of Warfare
- **Model:** Instant global visibility collapses the distance between tactical action and strategic effect.
- **Use:** Assume every action will be observed, transmitted, and distorted; evaluate second- and third-order effects before acting.

### Model 5 — Fight for Advantage
- **Model:** No information advantage is inherent; each must be competed for and most are temporary.
- **Use:** Treat the information environment as always-on contested terrain, at home station as much as forward.

### Model 6 — Narrative Nesting
- **Model:** Narratives already exist; credible command narratives are nested within cultural, joint, and strategic narratives and backed by visible action.
- **Use:** Build command narratives from higher narratives down and align actions to them rather than inventing new stories.

### Model 7 — Assault Through the Ambush
- **Model:** Force resiliency is the offensive-minded ability to continue the mission through cognitive and functional information disruption.
- **Use:** Develop unit and individual action drills for information attacks and rehearse recovery as routinely as any other battle drill.

- **Claim 1:** Information is central to every activity Marines undertake and to the functioning of societies, and the information environment is global, instant, and persistent.
  - **Therefore:** Local actions can have strategic effect, and every Marine's words and deeds matter to the mission.

- **Claim 4:** Information advantage results from generating, preserving, denying, and projecting information more effectively than the opponent.

- **Conclusion:** The Marine Corps institutionalizes information as a warfighting function and instrument of maneuver warfare so that commanders and all Marines create and exploit information advantages to penetrate the enemy system and impose their will across the competition continuum.

## Key Concepts & Definitions
- **Information Environment**: The global competitive space spanning the warfighting domains where all operations depend on information, including relevant social, cultural, psychological, technical, and physical factors.
- **Information Advantage**: An exploitable condition resulting from one actor's ability to generate, preserve, deny, and project information more effectively than another.
- **Information Warfighting Function**: The grouping of activities Marines apply to create and exploit information advantages in pursuit of mission objectives.
- **Informational Power**: The use of information, narratives, and technical means to advance interests and achieve the Nation's objectives.
- **Systems Overmatch**: The technical advantage of one side over another yielding fires, intelligence, maneuver, logistics, force protection, or command and control advantages.
- **Prevailing Narrative**: The narrative that is credible and resonates most with the intended audience, yielding a public opinion or perception advantage.
- **Force Resiliency**: The ability to resist, counter, and prevail against enemy and adversary reconnaissance, technical disruption, and malign activity such as misinformation, disinformation, and propaganda.
- **Information Generation**: Gaining and maintaining access to the information environment, building awareness of threats and opportunities, holding systems at risk, and creating the information needed to plan and operate.
- **Information Preservation**: Protecting and defending friendly information, systems, networks, narrative, and people, including maintaining the historical record and dispelling false information.
- **Information Denial**: Disrupting, concealing, or destroying the information an opponent needs to understand, decide, or act.
- **Information Projection**: Communicating, transmitting, or delivering information of any type to inform, influence, or deceive an observer or targeted system.
- **Competition Continuum**: The constant interaction between competitors spanning every action below and above the threshold of armed conflict, war being a form of violent competition.
- **Opponent**: Any actual or potential competitor, adversary, or enemy against which action is taken on any point of the competition continuum.
- **Misinformation**: False information that is not deliberately intended to cause harm.
- **Disinformation**: False information deliberately intended to cause harm or create an effect.
- **Thinking and Non-thinking Processes**: Human perception, cognition, and decision making versus pre-programmed or algorithmic machine processes, both taking information as input.
- **Cognitive Component**: The human, thinking-process element of an objective: perception, decision making, and will.
- **Functional Component**: The machine, non-thinking element of an objective: a system's ability to function.
- **Direct Approach**: Applying friendly strengths against an opponent's strengths of a similar nature.
- **Indirect Approach**: Applying friendly strengths against an opponent's critical vulnerabilities or weaknesses.
- **Tactical Deception**: Deceptive activities any unit conducts to gain advantages such as surprise.
- **Joint Military Deception**: Deception activities conducted within approved authorities to support operational-level campaigns and objectives.
- **Deception in Support of Operations Security**: Manipulating information available to foreign intelligence entities to make friendly intentions harder to interpret; a form of information denial.
- **Reflexive Control**: An information-centric theory of manipulating perceptions and actions to create confusion, paralysis, or desired behaviors in an opponent.
- **Command Narrative**: A credible narrative, broader than commander's intent, that anchors and guides a unit's public communication, exercises, and operations for internal and external audiences.
