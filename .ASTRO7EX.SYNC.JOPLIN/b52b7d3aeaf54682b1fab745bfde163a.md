[Extended Glossary](#extended-glossary)\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n## Terminology Primer (Read First)\\\n\\\n- **Value** → How light or dark something is (independent of color).  \\\n- **Value Contrast** → The *difference in value* between adjacent shapes. The biggest value jump grabs the eye first.  \\\n- **Edge** → The transition between two shapes/values/colors.  \\\n- **Edge Sharpness** → How quickly that transition happens. Razor-sharp edges command attention; soft edges recede.  \\\n- **Micro-contrast** → Small, local value differences (pores, fabric texture). Adds “crunch.”  \\\n- **Macro-contrast** → Big, global value differences (subject vs background). Defines the overall read.  \\\n- **Saturation** → Color intensity. Highly saturated areas pull attention—use sparingly near the focal.  \\\n- **Color Temperature** → Warm (reds/oranges) vs cool (blues/cyans). Warm advances, cool recedes (generally).  \\\n- **Visual Weight** → How strongly something attracts attention (size, value contrast, saturation, detail, warm hue, faces/text, sharpness).  \\\n- **Negative Space** → The “empty” area around subjects. Used to isolate and clarify.  \\\n- **Figure–Ground** → Clear separation of subject (figure) from background (ground).  \\\n- **Notan** → Simplified design reduced to 2–3 flat values (black/gray/white) to check readability.  \\\n- **Kissing Tangent** → Edges barely touch or align awkwardly; flattens depth and creates confusion.  \\\n- **Occlusion** → Intentional overlap; one object blocks another to show depth.  \\\n- **Leading Lines** → Lines (real or implied) that steer the eye toward the subject.  \\\n- **Rim Light** → Thin highlight on an edge that separates a form from its background.  \\\n- **Silhouette** → The readable outer shape of a subject; must be clear for instant recognition.\\\n\\\n> If a term appears below that isn’t in this primer, it will be defined in the **Extended Glossary**.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n## How to Use This Guide\\\n- **Pick one** weak area to improve per piece (e.g., “this week = Values”).  \\\n- **Work top-down**: Macro (Hierarchy, Contrast) → Meso (Balance, Flow) → Micro (Edges, Texture).  \\\n- **Test constantly**: Flip, Squint, Blur, and 3-Value Notan while composing—not after.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n## The 8 Core Principles\\\n\\\n### 3.1 Hierarchy\\\n**Definition**  \\\nDeliberate ordering of attention: **Primary → Secondary → Tertiary**. One clear winner first.\\\n\\\n**Controls**  \\\nClarity, speed of read, narrative priority.\\\n\\\n**Symptoms of failure**  \\\n- “Everything is loud.”  \\\n- Viewers ask “What am I supposed to look at?”\\\n\\\n**Design methods**  \\\n- **Crown the hero**: Give the focal area the largest **value contrast** *and* the **sharpest edges**.  \\\n- **Demote competitors**: Reduce saturation/detail/edge sharpness away from the focal.  \\\n- **Scale steps**: Use distinct size ratios (e.g., 1.0 → 0.6 → 0.35).  \\\n- **Isolation**: Carve **negative space** around the hero.\\\n\\\n**Quick fixes**  \\\n- Add a light-on-dark or dark-on-light junction at the hero.  \\\n- Soften/blur competing areas by 20–50%.  \\\n- Pull a subtle vignette to mute the periphery.\\\n\\\n**Context**\\\n- *Photo/Film*: Key + rim on subject; background one stop flatter.  \\\n- *Illustration*: Brightest accent and sharpest edge live at the eyes/hands.  \\\n- *UI*: Single primary CTA; all others clearly secondary.\\\n\\\n**Checklist**  \\\n- Can a stranger name the subject in 1 second? If not, reassign value/edge priority.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n### 3.2 Balance\\\n**Definition**  \\\nDistribution of **visual weight**. Two modes: **Symmetry** (calm) vs **Asymmetry** (dynamic).\\\n\\\n**Controls**  \\\nMood (stable vs energetic), comfort vs tension.\\\n\\\n**Symptoms of failure**  \\\n- Frame feels lopsided; eye slides out on the heavy side.  \\\n- Unintended tilt.\\\n\\\n**Design methods**  \\\n- **Symmetry**: Center, level, clean verticals/horizons; embrace monumental calm.  \\\n- **Asymmetry**: Counter a **big dull mass** with a **small high-contrast accent**.  \\\n- Keep heavy stuff off the extreme edges unless you *want* tension.\\\n\\\n**Quick fixes**  \\\n- Crop or nudge masses toward equilibrium; add a bright counterweight.  \\\n- Straighten horizons/verticals.\\\n\\\n**Context**\\\n- *Architecture*: Dead-center façade, axes aligned.  \\\n- *Street*: Large shadow block balanced by small lit figure.  \\\n- *Poster*: Massive character balanced by small bright title block.\\\n\\\n**Checklist**  \\\n- Squint: do left/right (or top/bottom) halves feel intentional?\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n### 3.3 Contrast\\\n**Definition**  \\\nDifferences that hook attention: **Value (king)** → **Edge** → **Color** → **Detail**.\\\n\\\n**Controls**  \\\nFirst read, separation, drama.\\\n\\\n**Symptoms of failure**  \\\n- Muddy midtones everywhere; subject merges with background.  \\\n- “Flat” image even with good color.\\\n\\\n**Design methods**  \\\n- Place the hero at the **highest value contrast** in the frame.  \\\n- **Edge strategy**: Sharp where you want attention, soft elsewhere.  \\\n- Reserve **saturation** and **micro-contrast** for the focal; keep background simple.\\\n\\\n**Quick fixes**  \\\n- Toggle grayscale; fix value design before color.  \\\n- Add rim light or dark halo to clarify **silhouette** (avoid glowing outlines).  \\\n- Drop background contrast locally around the hero.\\\n\\\n**Context**\\\n- *Product*: Specular edges define form; backdrop matte and neutral.  \\\n- *Concept art*: Fog the distance (low contrast), punch the subject (high contrast).\\\n\\\n**Checklist**  \\\n- Blur to 10–20 px: does the focal still pop? If no, rework values.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n### 3.4 Unity vs Variety\\\n**Definition**  \\\nCohesion (**unity**) with controlled differences (**variety**) so the image isn’t boring or chaotic.\\\n\\\n**Controls**  \\\nCoherence, style signature, ease of parsing.\\\n\\\n**Symptoms of failure**  \\\n- All parts different → noise.  \\\n- All parts same → monotony.\\\n\\\n**Design methods**  \\\n- Choose a **dominant family** (value range, hue range, shape language).  \\\n- Add **2–3 deliberate variations** near the focal (accent hue, unique texture, shape break).  \\\n- Use repetition-with-variation: echo motifs at smaller scales.\\\n\\\n**Quick fixes**  \\\n- Reduce hue spread; stick to one temperature family.  \\\n- Add a single accent (color/shape) at the focal; remove extras elsewhere.\\\n\\\n**Context**\\\n- *Landscape*: Cool unity with a warm sunlit accent.  \\\n- *UI*: One typeface family; hierarchy via size/weight, not random fonts.\\\n\\\n**Checklist**  \\\n- Summarize the piece’s “family” in one sentence; if you can’t, unify.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n### 3.5 Movement / Flow\\\n**Definition**  \\\nThe **path the eye travels** via lines, curves, light, and beats.\\\n\\\n**Controls**  \\\nPacing, narrative order, emotional feel (grace vs attack).\\\n\\\n**Symptoms of failure**  \\\n- Eye exits the frame fast; key beats get skipped; dead ends.\\\n\\\n**Design methods**  \\\n- **Leading lines** toward the hero (roads, gazes, arms, props).  \\\n- **S-curves/diagonals** for grace/energy; avoid parallel monotony.  \\\n- Place **stepping stones** (secondary beats) that march toward the focal.\\\n\\\n**Quick fixes**  \\\n- Add dark/light blockers at exit edges; bend lines back inward.  \\\n- Rotate/repose figures and props to point to the hero.\\\n\\\n**Context**\\\n- *Comics*: Z-pattern guides panel order; balloons reinforce flow.  \\\n- *Photography*: Shoreline arcs to lighthouse; clouds echo direction.\\\n\\\n**Checklist**  \\\n- Trace the eye with your finger: does it land on the hero and loop back?\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n### 3.6 Emphasis\\\n**Definition**  \\\nStacking multiple cues at one spot to create **punch** (value, edge, color, detail, isolation).\\\n\\\n**Controls**  \\\nImpact, memorability, message priority.\\\n\\\n**Symptoms of failure**  \\\n- “Nice, but nothing stands out.”  \\\n- Background steals the show.\\\n\\\n**Design methods**  \\\n- **Stack signals** at the focal: sharpest edge + biggest value jump + richest micro-contrast.  \\\n- **Isolate** the subject with negative space; simplify the surround.  \\\n- Use **unique color** (only saturated warm in a sea of cool neutrals).\\\n\\\n**Quick fixes**  \\\n- Remove micro-contrast from non-essentials.  \\\n- Add a small specular/edge accent on the subject; mute nearby areas.\\\n\\\n**Context**\\\n- *Poster*: One saturated accent on a desaturated scene.  \\\n- *Portrait*: Catchlights and crisp lashes; hair/background softened.\\\n\\\n**Checklist**  \\\n- Reduce to a 3-value Notan: does emphasis still read? If not, re-stage.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n### 3.7 Rhythm / Repetition\\\n**Definition**  \\\nPattern and cadence from repeated shapes/intervals with **variation**.\\\n\\\n**Controls**  \\\nCohesion, visual “beat,” forward motion.\\\n\\\n**Symptoms of failure**  \\\n- Even, mechanical spacing; or chaotic clustering with no cadence.\\\n\\\n**Design methods**  \\\n- Establish a **motif** (arches, triangles, circles). Repeat at smaller scales.  \\\n- Use **progression** (big → medium → small) and **irregular spacing** to feel natural.  \\\n- Align beats with flow so rhythm pushes toward the focal.\\\n\\\n**Quick fixes**  \\\n- Break perfect symmetry in the pattern; drop or shift one element.  \\\n- Remove one beat where spacing is too even.\\\n\\\n**Context**\\\n- *Architecture*: Colonnade rhythm funnels into doorway focal.  \\\n- *Landscape*: Fence posts diminishing into distance as tempo makers.\\\n\\\n**Checklist**  \\\n- Can you “tap” the cadence that accelerates toward the subject? If not, adjust spacing.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n### 3.8 Depth\\\n**Definition**  \\\nIllusion of space via planes, overlap, scale, perspective, atmosphere, and light.\\\n\\\n**Controls**  \\\nImmersion, realism, subject separation.\\\n\\\n**Symptoms of failure**  \\\n- Flat scene; **kissing tangents**; subject blends with background.\\\n\\\n**Design methods**  \\\n- Always aim for **Foreground / Midground / Background**.  \\\n- **Occlusion**: overlap shapes cleanly; avoid edge-kisses.  \\\n- **Atmospheric perspective**: distance = lighter, lower contrast, lower saturation.  \\\n- **Lighting**: Rim light separates; shadow mass unifies; keep a readable **silhouette**.\\\n\\\n**Quick fixes**  \\\n- Add a foreground silhouette or subtle vignette to create a near plane.  \\\n- Lift background blacks and desaturate by 10–30% to push it back.\\\n\\\n**Context**\\\n- *Film*: Haze + backlights for layers; extras as mid-plane texture.  \\\n- *Concept art*: Big foreground frame → mid action → distant landmark.\\\n\\\n**Checklist**  \\\n- Can you label 3 planes instantly? If not, add overlap or value stepping.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n## Cross-Principle Triage (Fix Any Image in 60–90s)\\\n\\\n1. **Hierarchy** → Boost value/edge at hero; demote competitors.  \\\n2. **Contrast** → Check in grayscale; step sizes toward focal.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n## Diagnostic Tests & Tools\\\n\\\n- **Squint Test** → Reduces detail; checks **value** hierarchy quickly.  \\\n- **Grayscale Toggle** → If the read dies in gray, color was doing the heavy lifting (bad sign).  \\\n- **Blur Test (10–20 px)** → Ensures macro-read and focal survive without edges.  \\\n- **Flip Test (horizontal)** → Reveals balance issues, tangents, awkward angles.  \\\n- **3-Value Notan** → Force design into black/gray/white; fix readability there first.  \\\n- **Edge Audit** → Mark the 5 sharpest edges; they should cluster at/near the focal.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n## Exercises That Build Skill Fast\\\n\\\n1. **Daily Thumbnails** (10–20, 2 min each) → Explore Hierarchy/Flow before detail.  \\\n2. **Notan Studies** (3 values) → From refs and masters; value design first.  \\\n3. **Tangent Hunt** → Print; circle every kissing tangent; redraw to overlap or separate.  \\\n4. **One-Focal Drill** → Make 3 comps where the focal is undeniable in 1 sec.  \\\n5. **Edge Control** → Paint with only soft edges; then add 10 hard edges exactly where needed.  \\\n6. **Atmosphere Ramps** → Practice pushing backgrounds back with value/saturation steps.  \\\n7. **Rhythm Lines** → Lay beats that accelerate toward focal; adjust spacing until it “taps.”\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n## Common Pitfalls (and the Cure)\\\n\\\n- **Golden-ratio worship** → It’s a tool, not a religion. Cure: prioritize value design.  \\\n- **Centering by habit** → Boredom. Cure: thirds/triangles/diagonals with intent.  \\\n- **Midtone soup** → No value hierarchy. Cure: push lights/darks; define a Notan.  \\\n- **Kissing tangents** → Flatness. Cure: occlude or separate decisively.  \\\n- **Busy backgrounds** → Focal lost. Cure: desaturate/soften/simplify background.  \\\n- **Edge noise** → Random crispness. Cure: hard edges only at focal, soft elsewhere.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n## Mini-Process: Compose on Purpose\\\n\\\n1. **Intent** → Write the sentence: *“The viewer must see ____ first.”*  \\\n2. **Choose an armature** → Thirds, triangle, diagonal, or spiral—just one.  \\\n3. **Notan** → Lock the 3-value design.  \\\n4. **Place the focal** → Highest value contrast + sharpest edges live here.  \\\n5. **Flow** → Lay leading lines/stepping stones toward the focal.  \\\n6. **Balance** → Add counterweight; avoid edge heaviness.  \\\n7. **Depth** → Fore/Mid/Back with atmosphere and occlusion.  \\\n8. **Edge & Color pass** → Hardness and saturation concentrated at the focal.  \\\n9. **Diagnostics** → Squint, grayscale, blur, flip. Fix what fails.  \\\n10. **Polish** → Micro-contrast only where the eye should linger.\\\n\\\n---\\\n\\\n## Extended Glossary\\\n\\\n- **Accent (Color/Value)** → Localized spike in saturation or brightness to attract attention.  \\\n- **Ambient Occlusion (Art shorthand)** → Soft darkening where forms meet;
