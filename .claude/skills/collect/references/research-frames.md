# Research frames for collect

Two frames the MS writer and the CDR can lean on. They shape a register or a brief; they add no new step.

## Report frame (conclusion first)
1. Write the question in one sentence, with at most three sub-questions.
2. Tier sources before searching: primary (official docs, papers, filings, measured data), secondary (reputable press, reports), tertiary (forums, social, aggregators: leads only, never evidence).
3. A key conclusion needs two independent sources. Two outlets quoting one origin count as one. With one source, label it "single-source claim".
4. Order: three to five lines of core conclusions, then one section per sub-question as conclusion, evidence, source, then an uncertainty block (not found, speculation, what would change the conclusion).
5. Date every key fact ("as of <month year>"). Every number has a source or is deleted. Speculation uses "may", "likely", "unverified".

## Brief frame (one paragraph for a researcher)
Write one self-contained paragraph for a reader who knows nothing of the project:
- Open with what the project is, why it exists and the current situation.
- State the one question and the decision it informs.
- Number three to six sub-questions inline.
- Say what to include and avoid; prefer primary sources; treat forums as weak signal.
- Define done: each key claim corroborated by independent sources, or the scarcity stated. End with a gap pass listing single-source claims and contradictions, repeated until clean.
- Fix the output per finding: source link, the specific claim, one line on why it matters.
