# Brainstorm

Standing rulings that apply:
- **Riff topics, not scripts (10/4):** hold topic bullets to riff on; scripted question lists lock Chief in.
- **Default public, no gating questions (9/10):** when the default is obvious, make the call and mark it provisional. Do not ask.
- **Underspecified ask (10/13):** when the ask is underspecified, one question, then proceed on the obvious default.
- **Go recommended (10/12):** a plain yes takes every rec on the last offer. End offers with "say go".

## Steps

1. **Check what is known.** If the request already holds what matters, say so and offer to skip the questions.
2. **Ask one question per message.** Open question first; a short menu only when Chief is stuck. If he says "just guess", propose something concrete.
3. **Offer alternatives with a rec.** Two or three options, each one line, one marked as the rec and why.
4. **Play back as you go.** After each settled part, restate it in a few bullets, mark guesses as guesses, ask what is wrong.
5. **Stop on "lock it".** Write the design as a short brief: goal · what is in · what is out · decisions made (with provisional marks) · open calls with a rec each. Save where Chief names, or reply with it if none named.

Nothing gets built during the riff. Reading files is fine.

## Two named modes

**"Interview me"** draws out what Chief already knows. It is not for a non-interactive run.
1. Write one sentence: your best read of what he wants, with a confidence number. Under about 70 percent, add what is still missing.
2. Ask one question per message, each with your guess attached (`Q:` then `GUESS:` and why). He reacts faster to a wrong guess than he builds an answer from nothing. Be visibly willing to be wrong.
3. Listen for "should want" answers (buzzwords, "the standard way"). Then ask: "If you didn't have to justify this to anyone, what would you actually want?"
4. Stop when you can predict his reaction to your next three questions. If after several rounds you cannot, say so and ask whether something basic is missing.
5. Play it back in 5 to 8 lines: outcome · who benefits · why now · success · constraint · out of scope. "Whatever you think", "sounds good" and silence are not a yes; ask again with two concrete options. On an explicit yes, stop the turn. Start nothing downstream.

**"Refine"** sharpens an idea he has already stated.
1. Restate it as one "How might we" line. Ask 3 to 5 questions at most (who for, what success looks like, real constraints, what was tried, why now), one per message. Do not go on until who it is for and what success looks like are clear.
2. Give 5 to 8 variations through lenses: do the opposite, remove a constraint, change the audience, combine, make it 10x simpler, scale it up. Ground them in what the repo already has.
3. After his reaction, cluster into two or three real directions. Test each on value, feasibility and difference. Name what each bets on, what could kill it, and what it ignores. Be honest, not a yes-machine.
4. Close with the brief, adding two sections to the lock-it shape: **Not Doing (and why)** and **Open Questions**. Not Doing is the most useful part.
