# Pricing

Use this when the brief asks what to charge, how to package, or whether to raise a price.

## Three axes

1. Packaging: what each tier includes.
2. Charge metric: what the price counts (per item, per use, flat fee).
3. Price point: the actual number.

## Price on value

The buyer's perceived value is the ceiling. The next best alternative is the floor for standing out. Price between them. Cost to serve is only a baseline. A competitor's price is a data point, not a target.

## First price: pick one you can learn from

On day one you place a bet. Pick a number, ship it, let buyers answer.

- Start in the order of magnitude that matches the buyer: about 10, 100 or 1,000 per month for individual, team or business-critical use.
- Avoid ultra-low pricing. It brings false traction, attracts people who never pay a real price, and is hard to raise later.
- If people pay without flinching, raise it. If nobody bites, you learned that in a week.

## Metric test

Ask: as the buyer uses more of this metric, do they get more value? If yes, it is a good metric. It should be easy to understand and hard to game.

## Tiers

Good, better, best. Entry tier: core and limited. Middle tier: the recommended one, full and fairly limited. Top tier: everything, two to three times the middle. Differ by features, usage limits, support, or access. Only add tiers if there is more than one kind of buyer.

## Research

Van Westendorp (too expensive, too cheap, expensive but possible, a bargain) gives an acceptable range. It does not predict conversion or revenue; test the candidate prices.

## Raising a price

Signs: buyers do not flinch, conversion is very high, "it's so cheap" feedback, real value added. Rollout: test on new buyers first, do not grandfather forever, move a small share of existing buyers then widen, explain the reason well ahead with a generous option.

## Pricing page

Clear comparison, recommended tier marked, one action per tier, FAQ, guarantee. Put prices in plain text, not in an image or behind "contact us," so people and AI assistants can quote them. Paste test: ask an AI with web access "what are the plans and prices?" A miss means the page is hard to read.
