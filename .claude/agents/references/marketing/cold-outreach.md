# Cold outreach

Use this when the brief asks for first-contact messages to people who do not know us, and for the follow-ups. Chief sends; you write.

## Principles

- Write like a peer, not a vendor. Use contractions. Read it aloud; if it sounds like marketing, rewrite.
- Every sentence earns its place. Cut anything that does not move the reader toward replying.
- Personalization must lead into the problem. If you delete the personal opening and the email still makes sense, it was decoration.
- Lead with their world. "You" and "your" should outnumber "I" and "we."
- One ask, low friction. An interest check ("Worth a look?") beats a meeting request. One call to action per message.

## Inputs to ask for

Who it goes to and why them, the outcome wanted, the specific problem solved, one proof point, and any fresh signal (news, hiring, a post). Work with what exists; note what would make it stronger.

## Shapes that work

- Observation, problem, proof, ask.
- Question, value, ask.
- Trigger, insight, ask.
- Story of a similar case, bridge, ask.

## Subject lines and follow-ups

Two to four words, lowercase, internal-looking. No pitch, no urgency, no emoji, no first name.

Send three to five messages with growing gaps. Each adds something new (an angle, proof, a resource) and stands alone. The breakup message is the last touch; honor it. Never send "just checking in."

## Before sending (Chief's call)

- Send from a secondary domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set, mailboxes warmed three to four weeks.
- Verify the list just before upload; keep bounces under 2 percent.
- Turn off open and click tracking. Measure replies.
- Put a plain-text opt-out line and a postal address in every message.
- A reply on any channel stops every other channel.

## Replies

Sort each reply: positive, information request, objection, not now, referral, out of office, unsubscribe. Answer a positive reply within minutes, with two proposed times. A human decides anything beyond that.

## Avoid

"I hope this finds you well", "my name is X and I work at Y", jargon (synergy, leverage, best-in-class), feature dumps, HTML or several links, fake "Re:" subjects, identical templates, a 30-minute call as the first ask. Also the AI tells: "it's not X, it's Y", "no X, no Y" lists, self-answered questions, "here's the thing", em dashes.

## How the Command uses it

Serves BOLO 138: the outreach step that finds the first buyers of the productized offer. You draft; list-building and sending stay Chief's. Not for adult-venture cold contact; if a brief asks for that, keep it generic and flag it to the CDR.
