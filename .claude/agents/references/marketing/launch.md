# Launch

Use this when the brief names a first public release, a drop, or an announcement plan.

## Readiness gate: run this first

Two failures kill launches. Stealth mode: polishing in private, never shipping. "One more feature": the date keeps sliding. The middle path is SLC, simple, lovable, complete:

- Simple: it does one thing.
- Lovable: a buyer would choose it, not just put up with it.
- Complete: a whole experience for that one thing, no obvious holes.

If it is polished past that bar, ship. If scope keeps growing, cut back to one thing.

## Three kinds of channel

- Owned: email list, site, community. Direct, no algorithm, grows more useful over time. Start with one or two.
- Rented: social platforms and marketplaces. Fast, unstable. Use one or two, and always point people to an owned channel.
- Borrowed: other people's audiences (guest posts, interviews, collaborations). Quick credibility; convert the attention into your own list.

## Five phases

1. Internal: friendly users test for free; it must be demo-able.
2. Alpha: a landing page with a signup form; invite people one by one.
3. Beta: teasers about the problem you solve; early access in waves.
4. Early access: show screenshots and demos; collect numbers and feedback; open invites in batches of 5 to 10 percent.
5. Full launch: open signups, start charging, announce everywhere you own.

## Checklist

Before: clear landing page, email capture, early-access list, assets ready, tracking in place, site QA if there is a new site.
Day: email the list, post the announcement, answer every comment, watch for problems.
After: follow up with everyone who engaged, a welcome or onboarding email, a roundup mention, gather feedback, plan the next moment.

## Sizing later updates

Major: full campaign. Medium: targeted note to the relevant segment. Minor: changelog only. Space releases out so each gets attention.

## Leave out

Product Hunt-style listing playbooks and partner tooling suit software products. Use them only if a real software product ships.

## How the Command uses it

Serves the first public drop of BOLO 138's offer and the first release of the BOLO 135 creator income report. Also the gate before a future BoldVenture site goes live. The gate comes first; the phases scale down to a small list.
