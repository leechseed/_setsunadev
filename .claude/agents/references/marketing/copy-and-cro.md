# Copy and conversion review

Use this to write page copy, to edit a draft, or to review a page that is not converting. Order: write, edit in passes, then review the page.

## Writing

Gather: the page's one action, the audience and their words, the offer and proof, where traffic comes from.

- Clear beats clever. A reader who must decode the line is gone.
- Benefits over features. Specific over vague ("cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes").
- Use the customer's words. One idea per section.
- Write only claims the source supports. When a claim is unsupported, name it, say why, offer the closest supportable line, and say what evidence would unlock it. Mark gaps `[NEED: ...]`; never invent proof.
- Page shape: headline, short subhead, one primary action, proof, the problem, three to five benefits, how it works in three or four steps, objections, final action with risk reversal.
- Button text says what they get ("Get the Report"), not "Submit" or "Learn more."

## Edit in seven sweeps

Run one pass at a time, and after each, recheck the earlier ones:

1. Clarity: can they follow it?
2. Voice and tone: consistent?
3. So what: does each claim say why the reader should care?
4. Prove it: evidence for every claim?
5. Specificity: numbers, timeframes, examples instead of adjectives?
6. Emotion: does the pain or the win land?
7. Zero risk: friction, unanswered objections, missing trust signals near the action?

Quick cuts: very, really, just, basically, "in order to". Swap utilize and leverage for use. Replace seamless, robust and innovative with the fact behind them. Keep sentences near 25 words or less.

AI-tell check on every edit: contrast reveals, "no X, no Y" lists, trailing pile-on clauses, self-answered questions, stock openers, em dashes in short copy. Fix from the facts; synonyms make new tells. Swap test: if a line fits a competitor unchanged, rewrite it.

## Conversion review, in order of impact

1. Value clarity: do they get it in five seconds?
2. Headline: specific, matches the traffic source.
3. Action: one primary, visible, repeated at decision points.
4. Scannability.
5. Trust: specific proof near the action.
6. Objections: price, "will it work for me", "what if it fails."
7. Friction: extra fields, unclear next step, slow or poor mobile.

Report as quick wins, high-impact changes, test ideas, and two or three alternatives for key lines with reasons.
