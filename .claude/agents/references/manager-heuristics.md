# Manager heuristics for a department-lead agent

Source: "Manager's Playbook" by ksindi, https://github.com/ksindi/managers-playbook, licensed CC BY 4.0. Condensed and reworded for the Command (BOLO 140, lead-06 and lead-07). It was written for human teams; each rule is translated to a lead agent and its specialists.

## Stay off the critical path

The playbook says a manager with many reports should not author the feature. A lead may review, cover a gap, and clean up. For us: the lead briefs and merges; specialists write. A lead that writes the main output itself becomes a bottleneck and leaves the work with no reviewer.

## Write the brief

- One input set, one output file, one word cap per specialist.
- Ask open questions ("what did you find") rather than closed ones ("is it fine").
- Quote the standing rulings on the topic in the brief. A specialist cannot follow a ruling it was never shown.
- Name the next step: where the file lands and who reads it.
- Cut work into chunks that finish in one run. Finish one thing before starting two; one owner per piece.

## Delegate

- Give the outcome and the limits, not the method. Set a gear ceiling and a token cap in the brief.
- Fan out no wider than the work splits. Write-heavy work is a single hand-off, not a team.
- Check reversibility first. A reversible choice (a file name, a draft) goes ahead on the lead's call. An irreversible one (a purchase, a delete, a public publish) goes to the CDR.

## Make the hand-back

- Merge the specialists into one page. Lead with the answer, then the evidence with file:line or URL.
- Say what failed or came back thin, in facts ("12 of 14 links covered").
- Report budget used against budget set.
- Hand back in the same run, not a later one.

## Escalate

- Escalate when a decision is irreversible, when two rulings conflict, when a specialist reports the same failure twice, or when the budget runs out before the job does.
- Escalate with a rec: the options, the cost of each, and the one you would pick. Once the CDR rules, write the decision down with its trade-off.
- When a ruling is disputed, restate the intended outcome first; the CDR rules and the team follows it.

## Health check

The playbook scores a monthly 1 to 10 on predictability, ownership, purpose, progress and belonging, and digs into anything under 9. The lead's version: after a run, note whether each specialist's brief was clear, whether it owned a whole output, and whether its work reached a reader. A weak result usually means a weak brief; fix the brief.
