# 📐 SSOT: 07.1 PROSE — how the LEECHSEED stories read on the page

**What this is.** The universe-wide prose style guide: the rules a page of draft is checked against before it counts as house prose, whichever IP it belongs to. It sits beside [07.2 Documents] and [07.3 Pages] as a sibling style guide (07_STYLE_GUIDES), and it draws its authority from five places already ruled or built: the texture system's T1–T12 discourse layers, Modern Poetics' P1–P10 constitution, the medium grammars' M1 PROSE contract, the Touchstones register, and the genre system's cultcore instance. It does not invent a house voice. Every rule below cites the canon doc or shelf book it comes from; where the universe needs Chief's own taste and no canon doc has ruled it yet, the rule is written as a question, marked **OPEN**, with one recommendation.

**The worked instance.** [OXO's Telling Profile](../../../../_CANON_NODES/oxo-telling-profile.md) is the first IP to rule its own T1–T10 answers (RULED 2026-09-23, "take the recs for row 18"). It is cited throughout below as the proof that this guide's universal principles can be instanced, never as the universe's default by itself — a second IP is free to rule its own Telling Profile differently, inside the floor this guide sets.

---

## Purpose

> **The split (CDR, 2026-09-29):** this guide governs how the stories read; how the Command writes its own docs and replies is [📐 ssot_07_2_documents_style.md](📐%20ssot_07_2_documents_style.md).


07.1 answers one question: what must be true of a page of LEECHSEED prose, in any IP, before it is ready to be judged on its own story's merits. It is the floor under every Telling Profile, not a replacement for one. A Telling Profile (T1–T12, per [ssot_01_texture_system.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_texture_system.md)) answers the Ten Questions of the Telling at one IP's grain; this guide states the universal defaults and constraints those answers are ruled against.

## Scope

Universe-wide: OVEREXITOUT, EVIL CHECK, LAKAD, ASTRO7EX, and every future IP. Per-story telling profiles are **instances** of this guide, never exceptions to it — where a Telling Profile rules a question differently from this guide's default (as OXO's T2 rules Anna out of POV entirely), the Telling Profile wins for that IP, because M1 PROSE holds that "the Telling Profile applies verbatim" once ruled ([ssot_01_medium_grammars.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_medium_grammars.md), M1). This guide only fails a draft where no Telling Profile has ruled the question at all, or where a rule below is universe-wide and not story-specific (the paratext disclaimer, §Explicit content, is the clearest case).

## Voice & person

T1 WHO SPEAKS separates four decisions that craft talk collapses into one: person (homodiegetic/heterodiegetic), level (extra-/intra-/metadiegetic), reliability, and time of narration (subsequent/simultaneous/prior/interpolated) — [ssot_01_texture_layer.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_texture_layer.md) T1, [ssot_01_texture_system.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_texture_system.md) T1. **The universal rule: no default person or tense across the universe.** Every IP records its own T1 line on its own Telling Profile before its prose is judged; a draft with no ruled T1 has not cleared this guide, whatever its sentences read like.

Reliability is judged against the **implied author**, not the real author or the narrator's own claims — the narrator's account can diverge from what the work as a whole is shown to endorse (Booth, *The Rhetoric of Fiction* — SHELF tier 1, held, not read; cited for this well-known claim only). OXO's worked instance: heterodiegetic, subsequent narration, "reliable narrator with a reliability gap reserved for the Administration's documents" (oxo-telling-profile T1, RULED 2026-09-23).

**Chief's tense/person default.** No canon doc rules a house-wide default; each IP rules its own. *Rec:* past tense, single internal-fixed focalization as the starting menu item for a new IP (matches OXO), struck or kept per IP's own storyform.

## Focalization

T2 WHO SEES is a separate decision from T1: **who speaks is not who sees** — the texture system's one load-bearing law, converged on independently by Hühn/Schmid/Schönert's three-dimension DNS model and Bal's EF/CF split ([ssot_01_texture_system.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_texture_system.md), "THE LAW"). Genette's focalization concept and Rimmon-Kenan's internal/external/zero vocabulary are the field's reference terms for this split (Genette, *Narrative Discourse Revisited*; Rimmon-Kenan, *Narrative Fiction* — both SHELF tier 1, held, not read).

**Universal default: internal fixed, one focal character per scene.** Variable or multiple focalization is licensed only when an IP's Telling Profile rules it explicitly — OXO's instance rules Tori-fixed by default with one licensed second lens (the Feed's surveillance eye, external, never a character POV) and denies Anna a POV outright, "a demonstration, not an argument" (oxo-telling-profile T2, RULED 2026-09-23).

The **distance dial** — cinematic / light / deep — is the house vocabulary for how close prose sits to the focal mind, named identically by Card (*Characters and Viewpoint*, [[BVX.0061]]) and Hall (*Writing Deep Point of View*, [[BVX.0273]]) twenty-six years apart. A scene's distance setting is recorded on its telling line (T6), not invented fresh each time.

## Motive on the page

**Universal rule (the tyranny of motive):** narration never states a character's motive outright. The reader infers it from what the character does, says and refuses. A line that says why a character acts, in the narrator's own voice, fails this guide. Source: Corbett ([[BVX.0196]]), whose ghost anchors the failure that motive-explaining would explain away; moved here from the character system, [ssot_02_character_systems_vertical_slice.md](../02_CHARACTER_SYSTEMS/📐%20ssot_02_character_systems_vertical_slice.md), OPEN call 3 (MOVED 2026-10-04, RULED, Chief: "all rec", BOLO 100). The character's L5 WOUND and L6 DRIVE set the motive; the page shows only its effects.

## Register & diction

T6 IN WHAT WORDS covers diction, syntax, distance policy, and free indirect discourse ([ssot_01_texture_layer.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_texture_layer.md) T6). **Universal default is the plain register**, per the Command's own standing rule (CLAUDE.md, SOP §7): name first, define terms on first use, short sentences, active verbs. Clark's fifty strategies (*Writing Tools* — SHELF tier 1, held) and Pinker's claim that clear prose shows the reader something true rather than performing expertise (*The Sense of Style* — SHELF tier 1, held) back the same default from the craft side.

A story may license a heavier register on its own Telling Profile — never as a universe default. OXO's instance rules three dialogue registers keyed to canon (Axiom/Noise/Truth) plus Southern Gothic diction for the Delta Coast bloc (oxo-telling-profile T6, RULED 2026-09-23); that register is OXO's, not the house's.

**Chief's profanity level.** No canon doc sets a house-wide ceiling or floor. *Rec:* none for a YA-adjacent cultcore IP (OXO's own audience contract, per its G1/G10 genre entries), ruled per-IP on that IP's genre contract otherwise.

## Sentence craft

No canon doc yet rules a house sentence style; this section runs on the shelf alone, by well-known claim only (none of these are distilled). Johnston's practical manual for sentence and paragraph power (*Word Up!* — SHELF tier 1, held) and Klinkenborg's claim that a sentence should carry one thought, revised by ear (*Several Short Sentences About Writing*, [[BVX.0166]], SHELF tier 2, held) both argue for economy over ornament. Clark's *Writing Tools* (above) backs concrete nouns and active verbs as the default instrument.

**Universal rule:** short-to-medium sentences as the base gait; length varies with T4 DURATION (below), not with the writer's mood.

**Chief's own sentence rhythm.** The shelf gives craft claims, not Chief's ear. *Rec:* the base gait above, with stretch beats (Sync-dive, trance, dread) licensed to run long and dilating, exactly as OXO's T4 already reserves stretch for Sync (oxo-telling-profile T4).

## Description & the sensorium

Ties [ssot_03_setting_system.md]'s S3 layer (the sensorium) directly: description is T4's pause/stretch machinery meeting setting's sensory layer ([ssot_01_texture_layer.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_texture_layer.md) T4). **Universal rule: description is motivated, never ornamental.** A detail earns its place by pointing at theme or distinctiveness — the rest is plumbing, not appeal (Kennedy, [[BVX.0349]], texture_layer T8's "gutter of implication" caution). Bal's threefold motivation test — delimitation, motivation, rhetorical binding — is the working check wherever a scene pauses to describe (texture_system OPEN call 10).

Show, don't tell is a **distance dial, not a commandment** (McKee, [[BVX.0175]], via texture_layer T6 — cited here as an existing canon-doc linkage, not a fresh shelf pick, per this guide's instruction to skip plot-structure books as sources). Rozelle's *Description and Setting* ([[BVX.0081]]) and Buckham's *Writing Active Setting* series ([[BVX.0056]], [[BVX.0268]], [[BVX.0270]]) — all SHELF tier 2, held — back active, sensory setting-writing by well-known reputation; Hardy's *Understanding Show, Don't Tell* ([[BVX.0253]], SHELF tier 2, held) backs the same distance-dial framing from the "told prose" diagnostic side.

**Universal rule: sensory detail routes through the story's own motifs when it touches theme.** OXO's worked instance: the uniform and the mirror, a door she could walk out of, the Feed — three motifs that carry the controlling idea without anyone saying it ([ssot_06_theme_system.md](../06_THEME_SYSTEMS/📐%20ssot_06_theme_system.md), THE MOTIFS). A description that could belong to any scene has failed this rule; one that only this story's theme could produce has passed it.

## Dialogue

Every line of dialogue should discharge at least one of Kempton's seven functions for a line of dialogue, or it is cut (Kempton, [[BVX.0084]], texture_layer/texture_system T6). Distance and register apply to dialogue exactly as to narration: OXO's worked instance rules three registers keyed to canon factions — Axiom (declarative, no questions), Noise (overlapping, unfinished), Truth (few words, all true, costly to say) — each with its own diction rule (oxo-telling-profile T6, RULED 2026-09-23).

**Universal rule: an IP rules its own dialogue registers on its own Telling Profile T6 line**, the way OXO ruled three; this guide does not hand down a house-wide register list, only the seven-function cut test and the instruction to keep registers canon-keyed rather than generic.

## Pace on the page

Ties [ssot_08_tracking_system.md](../08_TRACKING_SYSTEMS/📐%20ssot_08_tracking_system.md) §7 Tempo directly. T4 AT WHAT SPEED names the five speeds — scene, summary, stretch, pause, ellipsis (Genette, via [ssot_01_texture_layer.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_texture_layer.md) T4) — and the tracking system splits pace into **two tempos**: story tempo (world time per beat) and **audience tempo**, measured for prose as beats per 1,000 words (ssot_08 §7). The tracking system's own readout is the gap between the planned tempo curve and the measured one — where a draft drags or rushes against plan (ssot_08 §7).

**Universal rule: a prose draft's duration pattern is checked against its own planned tempo curve before it is called done** — not against a universal beats-per-1,000-words number, since that number is set per IP, per movement, on the tracking system's own lanes. Ellipsis is reserved for the story's own antagonist or threat where an IP rules it so: OXO's worked instance makes the Ban a hard cut, "a person removed mid-scene and never returned to" (oxo-telling-profile T4, RULED 2026-09-23).

## Explicit content & paratext

**Touchstone TS 1:1, "the bounded container"** (soft strength, RULED 2026-09-29, BOLO 91): erotic or ritual power tends to hold inside a container — a circle, a rite, an end date, a boundary — and fade or turn dangerous without one ([ssot_01_touchstones_register.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_touchstones_register.md)). Chief's own refinement, on the same ruling, is the paratext rule this guide carries directly: **the container lives outside the story, as a viewer-discretion disclaimer at the front of every book, so the scenes inside stay free of it.**

**Universal rule (T8 THRESHOLDS): every book in the universe carries a front-matter disclaimer** — fantasy, not a how-to — as its bounded container. This is a paratext rule, not a content rule; the scenes themselves are judged by P3 SATURATION below, never by the disclaimer. A detail earns a place in the front matter the same way it earns a place in a description, per Kennedy's gutter caution (texture_layer T8, [[BVX.0349]]): the disclaimer is one line, not a policy document restated at every book's door.

**The disclaimer, verbatim** (RULED 2026-10-04, Chief: "B recommendation", BOLO 92; draft B over a plain draft and a minimal one). Every book prints exactly this line in its front matter, unchanged:

> Fantasy, not a how-to: what happens in these pages stays in them. Reader discretion advised.

**P3 SATURATION** (Modern Poetics, [ssot_01_modern_poetics.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_modern_poetics.md)): sexuality is aesthetic, not genre — woven into the narrative's bloodstream, mastered rather than indulged, earned always. Forbidden: working around it (failure of nerve), spectacle without depth, refusing it outright (refusal of truth).

**Chief's own explicitness register across stories.** P3 states the doctrine; it does not set a page-level dial for how far prose goes before fading to paratext. *Rec:* OXO's own instance as the YA-adjacent default — T9 metalepsis withheld, no direct address, nothing crossed into second person (oxo-telling-profile T9) — with a more explicit register opt-in per IP on that IP's own genre contract, never as a universe default.

## Genre register (cultcore)

**G1 LABEL — cultcore**, RULED 2026-09-29 (BOLO 91, "cultcore — the genre of the outsiders"), is OXO's own series genre ([ssot_01_genre_system.md](../01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_genre_system.md), THE INSTANCE). Its G3 conventions, proposed in the genre system and RULED 2026-09-29, are the worked instance for any future cultcore IP's prose: outsiders set against an identity-shaping institution, the cult-as-container (TS 1:1 again, now as a genre convention, not just a Touchstone), the newcomer-vs-group induction beat, visibility as currency, belonging and selfhood trending opposite.

**G6 DELIVERY — immersive**: natives, not visitors; the world's own terms are used, never explained (Mendlesohn, [[BVX.0599]], genre_system G6). **Universal rule for cultcore IPs: prose does not stop to explain its own world's vocabulary — the reader is a native, per G6, and the institution's voice reads as a register distinct from the outsider's own** (OXO's Axiom vs. Truth, above, is the worked case).

This section applies only where an IP's own G1 rules cultcore or a related institutional-dystopia label; it is not a universal register, only a worked pattern for the genre family OXO opened.

## Meta-genre: the style bible

**Ruled 2026-09-16, genre OPEN 9; landed 2026-10-04.** This guide is the Command's style bible. Bawarshi and Reiff call a style bible a meta-genre. A showrunner's tone memo is another. A meta-genre describes a genre's social life beyond its contract. This guide is that home for the universe. Each IP's telling profile is a meta-genre instance under it. The other two terms, kairotic coordination (timing) and typification (cognition), are noted here and used nowhere else yet.

**Sources:** [[BVX.0581]] (Bawarshi and Reiff, *Genre*: meta-genre after Giltrow, style guides as genre about genre; typification after Schutz).

## The check

A short pass-list, read against a draft before it counts as ready. One line, one rule, one citation.

1. **Voice & person** — is T1 (person, level, reliability, narration time) ruled on this IP's own Telling Profile? (§Voice & person)
2. **Focalization** — does every scene know only what its one focal character knows, unless the profile licenses otherwise? (§Focalization)
3. **Register** — plain by default: active verbs, short sentences, terms defined on first use — unless the profile rules a heavier register? (§Register & diction)
4. **Sentence gait** — short-to-medium base, varied only where T4 duration licenses stretch? (§Sentence craft)
5. **Description** — does every sensory detail point at theme, distinctiveness, or a named motif, per the gutter rule? (§Description & the sensorium)
6. **Dialogue** — does every line discharge one of Kempton's seven functions? (§Dialogue)
7. **Pace** — does the draft's measured tempo match its planned curve, scene by scene? (§Pace on the page)
8. **Paratext** — does the book carry the front-matter disclaimer, one line, plain? (§Explicit content & paratext)
9. **Genre (cultcore IPs only)** — does the institution's voice read as a register distinct from the outsider's own? (§Genre register)
10. **Motive** — does the narration leave every character's motive unstated, for the reader to infer? (§Motive on the page)

## OPEN

All five RULED 2026-09-29 (Chief: "all right" on all recs, BOLO 92): each *Rec* below is now the rule. Call 5 rules a HOLD: the disclaimer's wording waits for its own front-matter pass.

1. **✅ Tense/person default for a new IP.** No house-wide default is ruled. *Rec:* past tense, single internal-fixed focalization, matching OXO's own instance, struck or kept per IP.
2. **✅ Sentence rhythm.** The shelf gives craft claims, not Chief's ear. *Rec:* short-to-medium base gait, stretch licensed only where T4 duration calls for it (Sync-dive, trance, dread).
3. **✅ Profanity level.** No ceiling or floor is ruled house-wide. *Rec:* no universe ceiling; profanity follows the speaker (their register and L-layers) and the story's T6 register. (CDR correction 2026-09-29: an earlier draft rec called OXO "YA-adjacent"; nothing in canon says so, so that rec was struck.)
4. **✅ Explicitness register across stories.** P3 SATURATION states the doctrine, not a page-level dial. *Rec:* explicit is allowed by default, written as fantasy first, with the container living outside the story as the front-matter viewer-discretion disclaimer (TS 1:1, soft, Chief 2026-09-29: "it needs to be that fantasy"); a story may dial down per its own genre contract. (CDR correction 2026-09-29: an earlier draft rec made "fade to paratext" the default, which contradicts Chief's TS 1:1 refinement; struck.)
5. **✅ The disclaimer's exact wording.** TS 1:1's refinement rules that the container lives in front matter, not the wording itself. *Rec:* hold for a dedicated paratext pass (07.2 or 07.3's territory) rather than drafting it inside this prose guide. **Wording RULED 2026-10-04** (Chief: "B recommendation"): see §Explicit content & paratext for the verbatim line.
