# 📐 SSOT: L9 EROS Synthesis — the sexuality shelf read against the schema

## 1. What this is

L9 EROS has one AUTHORED instance: Victoria Midnight, built 2026-08-15 against three sources (PSY.01 Walker, MSX.17 Perel, PSY.04 Rehor & Schiffman). Everything else on L9 since then has been that one instance's logic, propagated forward.

On 9/29 (BOLO 87/88) the Command distilled 25 new sexuality sources in one wave — nineteen MSX (MSX.18 through MSX.36) and six PSY (PSY.08 through PSY.13) — sourced specifically to fill the gap victoria-midnight-L9-eros.md named as its own open item: *"orientation and partners remain undocumented… that remains genuinely unwritten, and it is now the only part of L9 still empty."* The wave includes gay and male sex-work distills (MSX.11 through MSX.19 territory continued), bareback and pig-culture sources (MSX.24, MSX.28, MSX.35), cruising and venue ethnography (MSX.20, MSX.29, PSY.12), chemsex and drug-linked desire (PSY.08, PSY.11), sex-tourism epidemiology (MSX.34, PSY.09), craft and aesthetics sources (MSX.26, MSX.33), practice manuals (MSX.21, MSX.22, MSX.23, MSX.25, MSX.30, MSX.32), and two foundational theory texts (MSX.36 Foucault, PSY.13 Jung).

This is the first time that shelf has been read as a whole against L9's seven fields. It is a synthesis, not an edit: every proposed change below is provisional until Chief rules on it, the way the eleven 9/16 character calls stood as recommendations until "character calls go."

Two things the shelf makes unavoidable. First, queer and male-male desire cannot be an afterthought bolted onto a hetero-authored schema — nine of the twenty-five distills are gay- or bi-male-specific sex-work, cruising, or chemsex sources, and several (MSX.11, MSX.12, MSX.13, already in the library) predate this wave. Second, several sources (MSX.30, MSX.32) explicitly test whether a mechanism built for a man-and-woman pair transfers to two men, and answer yes for the mechanism, no for the ritual script — that distinction is itself schema-relevant.

## 3. Recurring methods across the shelf

Patterns at least two distills converge on independently, not proposed by any single source alone.

**Consent and desire are separate axes.** A "yes" motivated by money, professionalism, or fatigue is still consent; wanting something is a different fact from agreeing to it. MSX.18, MSX.22 (consent, not activity, separates kink from abuse), MSX.27, PSY.11 all state this independently. L9 currently has no field that keeps these apart; `desire_vector` and `erotic_safety_precondition` both get asked to carry it.

**Paid or staged intimacy runs a scripted-to-spontaneous spectrum, not a binary.** "Bounded authenticity" (Bernstein, used by MSX.19, MSX.27) and MSX.18's "genuinely felt in part" both refuse the choice between faking it and falling for it. Three sources, one industry literature.

**Nonverbal signal literacy is its own fluency, independent of what is wanted.** MSX.21 (`signal_language`), MSX.29 (`cruising_signal_literacy`), and MSX.22 (in-scene feedback is often nonverbal and must be actively read) all name a communication skill that can be present or absent regardless of desire's content.

**Arousal is a trainable mechanism, not a fixed trait.** MSX.23's plateau-and-wave model, MSX.30 and MSX.32's breath/muscle/sound "current," and PSY.10's presence-over-genitals finding all treat arousal as something built and moved, with a describable shape, rather than a mood that simply happens.

**Masculinity-policing shame attaches to receptive role, not to desire itself.** MSX.23's "Act Like a Man Box," MSX.25's "bottoming makes you a woman" myth, and MSX.34's mayate logic (the penetrator carries no stigma, only the receiver is marked) are three independent cultures naming the same mechanism: shame keyed to role, not to wanting.

**Substances and context are tools acting on a pre-existing want, not causes that manufacture it.** PSY.08 (meth exposes an existing template) and PSY.11 (desire precedes substance choice) converge on the same reversal of the standard "drugs cause unsafe sex" story.

**The object of desire stands in for something else.** PSY.13's Jungian claim (a symbol, never the literal target) is the theory underneath what MSX.17 already supplies to L9 as canon (Perel's separateness) — a second, older source arriving at the same structure from psychoanalysis rather than couples therapy.

**Disclosure runs its own register, separable from shame or desire.** MSX.28, MSX.29, MSX.31, and MSX.35 all document disclosure (HIV/STI status, porn use) as scripted, situational, and capable of moving over a life — not a single confession event and not a direct readout of `shame_index`.

**Venue and context comfort is measurable and distinct from general desire.** MSX.20, MSX.29, and PSY.12 each independently measure comfort with, or practice at, a sexualized space as its own variable, uncorrelated with orientation, outness, or HIV status.

## 4. The trope side

The pull list keys 430 tropes to sexuality · L9 (`_tools/tropes/data/domains/LAYERS.md`). They cluster into eight groups:

1. **Arousal mechanics & physiology** — the body's own machinery treated as a trope: *Anatomically Impossible Sex*, *Fantastic Arousal*, *Anorgasmia*.
2. **Consent, coercion & power** — where the consent line sits and who controls it: *Bed Trick*, *Safe Word*, *Power Dynamics Kink*.
3. **Identity & orientation policing** — how a character's stated identity gets tested or gatekept: *But Not Too Gay*, *All Gays are Promiscuous*, *Pragmatic Pansexuality*.
4. **Shame, secrecy & discretion** — desire that must stay hidden and the mechanics of hiding it: *Walk of Shame*, *Covert Pervert*, *Slut-Shaming*.
5. **Desire as narrative engine** — sex used to move plot rather than depict a person: *Sex Signals Death*, *Redemption Equals Sex*, *Plot-Inciting Infidelity*.
6. **Kink/fetish taxonomy & scene furniture** — the named vocabulary of practice: *Fetish*, *Kinky Role-Playing*, *Chastity Belt*.
7. **Commercial & transactional sex** — desire routed through payment: *Casting Couch*, *Sex for Services*, *Sex Tourism*.
8. **Body, display & desirability** — what marks a character as an object of want: *Hot as Hell*, *Lust Object*, *Exotic Equipment*.

The smaller character · L9 list (60 tropes) largely restates clusters 3, 4, and 8 at the character-trait level rather than the scene level (*Manly Gay*, *Straight Gay*, *Lipstick Lesbian* sit inside cluster 3; *Hot Teacher*, *Femme Fatale* sit inside cluster 8) — the two lists are not independent inputs, they are the same territory viewed from character and from scene.

## 5. PROPOSED CHANGES to L9

Numbered, at most ten, each a concrete rule change with cites and a one-line rec. L9 stays desire "structured, armored, and made safe"; every change below treats queer and male-male desire as first-class source material, not a special case appended after a straight default (per MSX.30 vs. MSX.32's own contrast: a mechanism built once, gender-neutral, beats a mechanism tolerated by exception).

1. **Split consent from desire.** `desire_vector` currently has no way to record that a want and an agreement are different facts. *Cites: MSX.18, MSX.22, MSX.27, PSY.11.* Rec: add a `consent_posture` sub-field alongside `desire_vector`, distinct from `erotic_safety_precondition`.

2. **Give `intimacy_mode` a scripted↔spontaneous axis.** Paid, staged, or performed intimacy needs to move within one scene without the schema forcing a real/fake binary. *Cites: MSX.18, MSX.19, MSX.27.* Rec: add `client_script` as an L9 sub-field, values ranging scripted to spontaneous, revisable mid-scene.

3. **Add a signal-fluency field.** Three independent sources document nonverbal desire/consent literacy as its own trait. *Cites: MSX.21, MSX.22, MSX.29.* Rec: add `signal_fluency` (fluent / functional / illiterate), separate from `desire_vector`.

4. **Let `desire_vector` hold an additive, non-terminating mode.** The current scalar (Tori: 3) assumes desire resolves on acquisition; several sources describe desire that accumulates without a finish line. *Cites: MSX.24, PSY.13, MSX.32.* Rec: add a `resolution_type` sub-field (terminating / additive) under `desire_vector`.

5. **Separate role-shame from exposure-shame inside `shame_index`.** Tori's `shame_index: 2` describes immunity to being shamed for being seen; masculinity-role shame (bottoming, receiving) is a different mechanism entirely and the current single scalar can't hold both. *Cites: MSX.23, MSX.25, MSX.34.* Rec: split into `exposure_shame` and `role_shame`, both 0–10, same convention as the current field.

6. **Add an arousal-mechanism subfield.** Several sources give arousal a describable shape (plateau-and-wave vs. spike-and-drop; current-and-redirect) usable directly in prose. *Cites: MSX.23, MSX.30, MSX.32.* Rec: add `arousal_curve` (spike / plateau / current) under `erotic_blueprint_type`.

7. **Adopt `erotic_pacing` as a craft rule, not a new field.** The withhold/fragment/bound/interrupt toolkit is scene-craft, parallel to how the 9/16 wave resolved "the tyranny of motive" as a house writing rule rather than schema. *Cites: MSX.26, MSX.33.* Rec: state as a narration rule for any L9-driven scene, no new field.

8. **Widen `erotic_safety_precondition` to include external/structural safety, not only in-scene negotiation.** Tori's is `control`, internal to her; several gay-male sources document a chemical, external, invisible-to-the-scene precondition (PrEP, U=U) as a distinct category. *Cites: MSX.18, MSX.24, MSX.29.* Rec: add an `internal`/`external` tag to `erotic_safety_precondition`.

9. **Adopt `disclosure_posture` on L9, not only L3.** MSX.31 fed it to L3 SOCIAL as a general disclosure trait; the HIV/STI-specific sibling sources (MSX.28, MSX.29, MSX.35) argue the sexual-health register is distinct enough, and load-bearing enough for gay male characters specifically, to warrant its own L9 field or an explicit cross-link. *Cites: MSX.28, MSX.29, MSX.31, MSX.35.* Rec: cross-link L3's `disclosure_posture` to L9 rather than duplicate it; rule at the next schema bump.

10. **Add `template_origin` as a pointer from L9 to L8.** The "lovemap"/fixed-young-template claim (PSY.08) and Jung's symbol-formation mechanism (PSY.13) both argue a character's adult `desire_vector` is downstream of an L8 IMPRINT-era formation event, not authored free-floating. *Cites: PSY.08, PSY.13, MSX.23 (the "rewiring" mechanic as a case of an adult-onset template repair).* Rec: add `template_origin` as a relational pointer from L9 to an L8 imprint event, echoing the existing ghost_ref/revenant_ref pattern proposed for L5.

## 6. Open questions

- **`polarity_role` (MSX.32) has no home yet.** It reads as an L9 field (active/receptive as a tradeable role) but overlaps L6 `drive_texture`'s dominant/submissive default in MSX.21 and MSX.22. Needs a ruling on which layer owns role-as-behavior versus role-as-arousal-source (echoing MSX.22's own Top/Dominant/Sadist axis split, proposal #3 above under a different name).
- **`practice_repertoire` and `scene_vocabulary` (MSX.21, MSX.35) may be one field, not two.** Both name a concrete-acts-and-dialogue vocabulary; whether they merge or stay separate (technique versus register) is unresolved here.
- **Foucault's claim (MSX.36) is structurally different from the other 24 distills.** Every other source treats `shame_index` as a value to score; Foucault argues the naming event that produces a sexual identity is itself a power mechanism, prior to any scoring. This may mean `shame_index`/`shadow_content` need an origin-event field, not just a number — closer to proposal #10 than to proposal #5, and possibly the same open item.
- **No source in this wave addresses lesbian or WLW-specific desire architecture.** The whole shelf is gay/bi/queer male plus mixed-gender academic and clinical sources; L9's only AUTHORED instance (Victoria Midnight) is a woman, but the wave that was supposed to fill her "orientation and partners" gap skews male. Whether that gap is now closed, widened, or simply relocated is a call for Chief, not this synthesis.
- **`venue_comfort_and_practice` (three sources: MSX.20, MSX.29, PSY.12) sits ambiguously between L7 and L9.** All three distills' own `feeds:` blocks split it across both layers. This synthesis followed the majority keying (L9 primary) but the schema owner should confirm which layer actually owns "comfort in a sexualized space."
