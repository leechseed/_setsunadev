# The texture system: the telling at character grade

Source: [📐 ssot_01_texture_system.md](../../../_SSOT/01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_texture_system.md). Look things up in the [reference](2-reference.md). Do tasks with the [how-to](3-how-to.md). Learn by doing in the [tutorial](4-tutorial.md).

## Texture earned a system

The texture layer began as a taxonomy: the Ten Questions of the Telling. The texture system promotes it. It now has a slice, an instrument and an instance. It is the fourth top-layer model, beside character (02), setting (03) and plot (04).

The [texture layer](../ssot_01_texture_layer/1-concept.md) stays the taxonomy source. This system is a sibling, not a replacement.

## The system owns the telling and nothing else

The system owns the discourse side: who speaks, who sees, order, speed, frequency, words, feeling, thresholds, frames and narratee. It owns the TELLING PROFILE and the scene card's `telling:` line. It owns the way a medium re-instruments all of it.

It owns nothing story-side. Dramatica's storyform owns structure. Plot systems own events. The setting system owns place. The 12-Layer Character Database owns who a character is.

## The exclusion runs both ways

Mediation belongs to the telling, never to the person told about. Card shows that viewpoint mechanics map onto none of the twelve character layers. Hühn, Schmid and Schönert hold the same view as their working assumption.

The texture system claims no story spine, no event structure and no character psychology. Those systems claim no telling.

## Who speaks and who sees are two decisions

Eight independent sources reach one law without reading each other: who speaks and who sees are two decisions, never one. Hühn, Schmid and Schönert prove it over fourteen essays. Bal calls the collapse "nonsensical." Card names the same split inside limited third person.

The law gives the system its spine. Every other field hangs from it.

## Distance is the dial the craft books turn

Craft vocabulary is a set of compressed settings on the fixed answer set. The distance between reader and focal character is the dial those books turn under many names. Hall removes barrier words. Kempton keeps viewpoint silence. Iglesias maps reader emotion. All three aim at one knob from three trades.

## The telling holds a baseline and overrides it per scene

The profile holds as baseline until a scene card's `telling:` line fires an override. The cycle runs at every rung from beat to movement. The plot engine does the same one system over.

## The OXO profile waits

The system does not need an example to work. The Outliers has no prose. Its profile is parked as ⏸ OXO telling profile (phase 2). The table of proposed values is a decision menu for Chief to strike or keep.

## The long read

The system gives a writer a precise answer to a question craft books answer by feel: why does this scene sound the way it does? The answer is a set of fields. T1 says who speaks. T2 says whose view the reader gets and what the reader is denied. T6 says how close the words sit.

A writer who fills these fields can audit a scene. The `t1_t2_gap` flag catches a narrator who knows more than the character. The barrier-word count catches a deep-point-of-view passage that drifts. The mood-curve entry names the payoff the scene owes. None of these checks decides what the story should say. They decide how it is told.

The twelfth layer closes the loop. T12 FUNCTION ties the telling to the storyform. A telling with T12 empty is only style, an author's habitual voice. A telling with T12 filled is an instrument of the argument.
