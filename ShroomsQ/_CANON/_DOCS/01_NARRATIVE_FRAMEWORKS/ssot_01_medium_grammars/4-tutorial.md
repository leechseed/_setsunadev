# Tutorial: move one telling across three media

You will take one storyform and re-answer three texture questions in three media. Keep the [reference](2-reference.md) open. This lesson uses the doc's own contract and texture tables. It writes no story.

## Step 1: hold the storyform fixed

Write one line: the storyform stays the same in every medium. Only the Telling Profile changes.

## Step 2: answer in prose (M1)

Open the texture table at row M1. T3 order is free. T4 speed is fully elastic: a sentence holds a year. T10 narratee is an implied reader. Note that T2 internal focalization is native and free.

## Step 3: answer in film (M2)

Move to row M2. T3 order is made by the cut. T4 speed is the edit. The cut is ellipsis and the long take is stretch. T10 narratee is the viewer, addressed only by VO. Note the change in T2: focalization becomes shot selection.

## Step 4: answer in games (M4)

Move to row M4. T3 order becomes topology, a quest order the player varies. T4 speed is player-paced. T10 collapses: the narratee is the player.

## Step 5: compare the three rows

Look at the three T10 cells. The reader is implied in prose. The viewer is addressed only by VO in film. The player is the narratee in games. The same storyform has three different audiences.

## Step 6: check the agency column

Return to the contract table. Prose and film give the audience no agency. Games give co-authorship. A game therefore needs a Touchstone check on its rules, because a mechanic can breach a Touchstone.

## Check yourself

You did it right if the storyform line never changed and every texture answer did. A Telling Profile that survives unchanged across media means you copied it, not re-instrumented it.
