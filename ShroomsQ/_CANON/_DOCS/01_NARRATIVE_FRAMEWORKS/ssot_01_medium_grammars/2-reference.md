# The medium grammars: reference

Source: [📐 ssot_01_medium_grammars.md](../../../_SSOT/01_NARRATIVE_FRAMEWORKS/📐%20ssot_01_medium_grammars.md). Version 1.2.2 (1.2.0 added the texture table; 1.1.0 added mode). Trunk: BLACK; ORANGE rows carry craft grammar only. Why: [concept](1-concept.md).

## THE CONTRACT TABLE — ten mediums at a glance

| Medium | Signal | Time regime | Agency | Native narrator | Native unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prose | words | reader-paced | none | the sentence voice (FID native) | chapter/scene |
| Film | image + sound | fixed-flow | none | the camera (+optional VO) | shot |
| TV / serial | film + episodic apparatus | fixed-flow, installment-gapped | none | series voice + paratext | episode |
| Games | image + sound + systems | player-paced | co-authorship | the system + environment | encounter/quest |
| TTRPG | speech + rules + dice | table-paced | collective authorship | the GM, live | session/campaign |
| Music video | image cut to music | fixed-flow, track-locked | none | the track | the track |
| Social / short-form | phone-native video | fixed-flow, seconds-scale | swipe (retention economy) | the creator-persona | the post |
| PMV / HMV | sourced image cut to music | fixed-flow, track-locked | none | the edit | the track |
| Porn (live-action) | performed scene | fixed-flow (viewer-scrubbed) | scrub/selection | direct address, normalized | the scene |
| Theatre | live bodies in real space | fixed-flow, co-present | none, but a live feedback loop | embodied ensemble | the act/performance |

Law: story-side is medium-independent; discourse-side is where the medium bites. A storyform survives adaptation. A Telling Profile does not (McErlean 0126: one IP at R7, many instantiations).

## THE GRAMMARS — how each medium re-instruments texture

### M1 · PROSE

Full interiority access. T2 internal focalization is native and free. Free indirect discourse is the signature instrument. Duration (T4) is fully elastic. Holdings: the entire CRE craft shelf. The Telling Profile applies verbatim.

### M2 · FILM

- T2 focalization is shot selection: POV, over-shoulder, insert (Grammar of the Shot 1042; Master Shots 0138/1053/1054).
- T4 duration is editing: the cut is ellipsis, montage is summary, the long take is stretch.
- T1 voice is camera plus optional VO. Interiority must be performed or shown, never stated free.
- Holdings: VIS shelf (100); Aronson 0188; Frayne 0228 (screenplay as the prose-side notation of film).

### M3 · TV / SERIAL

Film grammar plus the R6/R7 machinery made native. Seasons are nested storyforms. "Previously on" is repetitive frequency (T5) as apparatus. Title cards and cold opens are paratext (T8) as rhythm. Governing holding: Mittell (0565), complex TV as the operational poetics of seriality.

### M4 · GAMES

The player co-authors. Four native mechanisms:
- **Ludonarrative:** mechanics are meaning. Dissonance occurs when systems argue against the story. A mechanic can breach a Touchstone.
- **Environmental storytelling:** the setting slice narrates. S5 SCAR and S10 UNDERSIDE read as level design. The player is the focalizer.
- **T3 order becomes topology:** quest structure is the ladder with player-variable sequence. The four R7 series patterns appear as campaign architectures.
- **T10 collapses:** narratee is the player. Direct address is the tutorial voice.

Holdings: GAM (217): Berger 0093/0404, Breault 0534, Despain 0498, Once Upon a Pixel 0144, Finley 0104. House consumers: Bongobabe (the clicker loop as a duration grammar) and the h-game board.

### M5 · TTRPG

- The GM is a live extradiegetic narrator. The table is collective authorship: an emergent storyform.
- Rules are Touchstones made mechanical. A rulebook is a DOCTRINE 0 artifact with dice.
- Sourcebooks and gazetteers are printed setting slices. The slice can author TTRPG books, and the books validate the slice.

Holdings: GURPS 0447; Kobold 0458/0541; Against Worldbuilding 0349.

### M6 · MUSIC VIDEO — v1 scaffold (BOLO 16 deepens)

- The track is the autocrat. The edit obeys the beat grid. Narrative compresses to song length.
- Three canonical modes (Vernallis): performance, narrative, conceptual. Most videos braid them.
- Lyric-image counterpoint is the native thematic instrument (T7 by juxtaposition).
- Zero holdings. Vernallis, *Experiencing Music Video*, is the anchor acquisition candidate.

### M7 · SOCIAL / SHORT-FORM — v1 scaffold (BOLO 16 deepens)

- The hook economy: retention is won per second. The first second is the inciting incident or death. R1/R2 scale is the whole container.
- The creator-persona is a homodiegetic narrator. Its persistence across posts is the R7 serial pattern (braided, parasocial).
- The algorithm is distributor-paratext (T8): captions, sounds and trends are threshold apparatus the platform co-writes.
- House consumer: the Social channels project (25 conversations, zero artifacts).

### M8 · PMV / HMV — v1 scaffold, ORANGE (BOLO 16 deepens)

- Music-video grammar crossed with erotic function. Structure is an arousal ladder cut to a beat grid. HMV sources animate material. PMV sources live-action.
- Compilation logic is curatorial. Clip selection is the authorship (T1 voice is the edit itself).
- House echoes: the PMV call-and-response series concept (forgotten-generation doc) and the taxonomy-engine act-track vocabulary. Surfaces are Ultrasin-side per the firewall.

### M9 · PORN (live-action) — ORANGE

- Direct address is normalized metalepsis (T9/T10). Performer-to-camera is the default contract.
- The scene is the native unit, with a conventionalized escalation arc. Register vocabulary is the house register (taxonomy engine ruling 10). It covers photo-type production, dress and expression axes shared with the shoot set.
- Craft grammar lives here. Content systems live Ultrasin-side (§B, shoot set, taxonomy engine).

### M10 · THEATRE

Live co-presence. Duration is mostly scene-speed, so T4 cannot cut. Setting is physically real: S1 BODY is built, not described. Direct address is an honored tradition (T10). Every performance is a variant text. Holdings: PRF (15), the thinnest shelf, low priority by volume.

## TEXTURE BY MEDIUM · T3, T4, T10 per channel

The texture system's T11 field points here.

| Medium | T3 order | T4 speed | T10 narratee |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Prose | Free; any anachrony by sentence | Fully elastic; a sentence holds a year | Implied reader; narratee optional |
| M2 Film | Made by the cut | The edit: cut is ellipsis, long take is stretch | The viewer; addressed only by VO |

| M5 TTRPG | Emergent: the table sets sequence live | Table-paced; the GM stretches or cuts | The table, addressed live by the GM |

| M7 Social / short-form | Hook first; the whole container is R1/R2 | Seconds-scale; retention decides each second | The follower of the creator-persona |
| M8 PMV / HMV | Escalation order cut to the beat grid | The edit escalates with the track | The viewer, addressed by the curated edit |
| M9 Porn (live-action) | Conventional escalation arc | Scene-speed; the viewer scrubs | Direct address, normalized |
| M10 Theatre | Act order fixed, played live | Scene-speed; cannot cut | Direct address, an honored tradition |

## MODE · a fence around genre, listed per medium

Mode (animation, documentary, mini-series and the like) is a fence around genre, never a G-layer. Mode changes how a genre is made and sold, not what it owes the reader. The [genre system](../ssot_01_genre_system/2-reference.md) does not hold mode. Selbo fences mode off from genre (BVX.0576).

| Medium | Modes listed here |
|---|---|
| M1 Prose | none listed yet |
| M2 Film | animation, documentary |
| M3 TV / serial | mini-series, animation, documentary series |
| M4 to M10 | none listed yet; add a mode when a work needs one |
