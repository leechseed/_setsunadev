# LAKAD station: post-apocalypse reading list

**The question:** which post-apocalyptic stories carry up onto the station? It is three or four stations welded into one, between Earth and the Moon. It holds the main characters prisoner after their rocket is boarded.

**How to use it:** the list is one ranked line, best first. Each entry ends with "Steal for the station," the part that travels. The name, owner and factions of the station stay open.

---

1. **Fallout 3** (game, 2008, Bethesda Game Studios). You leave a sealed vault and explore a ruined Washington, D.C.
   Steal for the station: Megaton is a town built around an unexploded bomb. Give the station one thing everyone fears and lives beside. Rivet City is a rusted ship turned into a town. Each welded-in station can have its own town rules.

2. **Snowpiercer** (graphic novel 1982, Jacques Lob and Jean-Marc Rochette; film 2013, Bong Joon-ho; TV 2020). The last humans live on one endless train, ranked by car.
   Steal for the station: the rank is physical. Where you sleep tells everyone your class. Prisoners could live in the oldest, worst-fed station, and the guards could control the doors between sections. *Twist flag:* the film's ending reveals who runs the engine, and that is a stealable idea.

3. **The Expanse** (novels 2011 to 2021, James S. A. Corey; TV 2015 to 2022). Humans settle the solar system. Earth, Mars and the Belt are in cold war.
   Steal for the station: air, water and heat are money. Belters trade in debts, and a "station" is a nation. The Belt also has its own slang, body shape and politics, so each welded station can too.

4. **Metro 2033** (novel 2005, Dmitry Glukhovsky; game 2010, 4A Games). Survivors live in Moscow's subway after nuclear war.
   Steal for the station: each stop is its own small country. One runs on trade, one on a creed, one on a gang's rule. Travel between them is the story. Ammo works as money.

5. **Wool** (novel 2011, Hugh Howey; TV as *Silo*, 2023). Thousands live in one deep underground silo with strict laws.
   Steal for the station: the worst punishment is "cleaning." The prisoner is sent outside to wipe a sensor before dying. A station could send prisoners out an airlock on an errand. Another idea is that a lottery or the silo's founders control who is born.

6. **Mad Max: Fury Road** (film, 2015, George Miller). Warlord Immortan Joe rules a fortress and controls the water.
   Steal for the station: whoever owns one resource rules everyone. Followers worship him, and his war boys believe they will be rewarded after death. A boarder crew could be exactly that, a cult that serves the station's owner.

7. **Escape from New York** (film, 1981, John Carpenter). Manhattan is walled off as one giant prison. The guards stay outside.
   Steal for the station: a prison with no cells. The wall is the cell, and prisoners govern themselves inside. The station's guards can leave prisoners to form factions and only step in at the edges.

8. **Babylon 5** (TV, 1993 to 1998, J. Michael Straczynski). A huge neutral space station is home to many species and political factions.
   Steal for the station: the people who live in the lower levels have no say. The upper decks have ambassadors and the "Down Below" has the poor. The station's docking bays and customs decide who gets in.

9. **Star Trek: Deep Space Nine** (TV, 1993 to 1999, Rick Berman and Michael Piller). A station once built as an ore-processing base by an occupying power now sits in neutral hands.
   Steal for the station: an occupier's old forced-labor system leaves marks. Rooms built for torture and stacked bunks can still be there. The station can be a patched-over camp that new owners repurpose.

10. **Blame!** (manga, 1997 to 2003, Tsutomu Nihei). A city has grown without limit. Humans hide in its cracks while machines hunt them.
    Steal for the station: a structure so huge no one knows all of it. Old stations bolted together make maps that lie. Prisoners could discover parts of the station the owners forgot.

11. **Station Eleven** (novel 2014, Emily St. John Mandel; TV 2021). A flu kills most people. A traveling troupe performs Shakespeare for scattered towns.
    Steal for the station: art keeps people human. A prisoner theater, or a troupe with a pass to travel between the stations, gives your characters a safe way to move. Another idea is a museum of ordinary objects from Earth.

12. **The Road** (novel 2006, Cormac McCarthy; film 2009). A father and son walk south across a burned country, hunted by starving people.
    Steal for the station: scarcity as a mood. Show a station running out of food slowly, not in one big failure. The "good guys" rule (carry the fire, do not eat people) gives the main characters a code to hold.

13. **Alien: Isolation** (game, 2014, Creative Assembly). Ripley's daughter searches a dying space station and a monster hunts her.
    Steal for the station: a station in decline. Broken lights, bad air and locked doors tell the story. The survivors are scavengers who barter over medicine. Quiet and hiding beat fighting.

14. **BioShock** (game, 2007, Irrational Games). The underwater city of Rapture has collapsed into war after its founder's dream failed.
    Steal for the station: a founder's idea of freedom becomes a trap. Posters, audio logs and old ads can tell the station's history. *Twist flag:* the game's big reveal about who controls the player is the idea to study.

15. **Orphans of the Sky** (novella 1941, Robert A. Heinlein). On a huge starship, the crew has forgotten that it is a ship.
    Steal for the station: myths grow around machines. If the station's older generation lost its purpose, the young read engines as gods. Stationers could have scripture about the "Great Weld." *Twist flag:* the ship's secret is the setup.

16. **Fallout: New Vegas** (game, 2010, Obsidian Entertainment). A courier on a desert frontier is caught between three powers.
    Steal for the station: three or four big factions compete, and each has a good reason and a dark side. One faction runs the money, one runs the army, one runs the old tech. A prisoner can bargain among them.

17. **Aurora** (novel 2015, Kim Stanley Robinson). A generation ship slowly breaks down, because a closed world cannot repair everything.
    Steal for the station: small closed worlds fall apart. Crop failures, worn-out parts and shrinking populations make the station's reasons feel real. Mending, not building, is the daily job.

18. **Battle Angel Alita / Gunnm** (manga, 1990 to 1995, Yukito Kishiro). A scrap city sits under a floating rich city, and trash falls down to it.
    Steal for the station: the rich ring above and the scrap ring below. The station's old hulls could drop waste and salvage downward. The poor then live on the wreck of the powerful.

19. **Prey** (game, 2017, Arkane Studios). You wake on a research station that has been overrun, and its builders left behind rules you must unravel.
    Steal for the station: a station with layers. Each wing has its own corporate history, tools and secrets. The prisoner's job is to learn which doors the owners do not want opened.

20. **Dead Space** (game, 2008, Visceral Games). An engineer boards a huge mining ship that has gone silent.
    Steal for the station: a working-class ship, built to dig, now full of dead crew. Mining gear becomes weapons. Salvage crews that board dead ships are a good model for the people who board the rocket.

## Patterns

- **One resource rules the rest.** Water in Mad Max, air in The Expanse, the engine in Snowpiercer. Pick the station's one scarce thing.
- **Seams become borders.** Metro stops, Fallout towns and Belt stations each act like small countries. Each welded station can keep its own laws.
- **Rank is built into the space.** Cars, silo floors and upper and lower decks tell people where they belong. The station's layout can be its caste system.
- **Prisons without bars work best.** Escape from New York and Wool hold people with the environment. The outside is the cell.
- **The founders' story is partly a lie.** BioShock, Wool and Orphans of the Sky hide the truth about why the place exists. Give the station a secret origin.
- **Salvage is the daily economy.** Alita, Dead Space and Fallout run on picking through ruins. The hull, parts and old cargo are the money.
- **Slow decay beats sudden collapse.** The Road, Aurora and Isolation show small failures piling up. A tired, patched station feels real.
- **Culture survives in odd places.** Station Eleven's theater and Orphans of the Sky's myths show how people hold on. Give prisoners a song, a game or a religion.
