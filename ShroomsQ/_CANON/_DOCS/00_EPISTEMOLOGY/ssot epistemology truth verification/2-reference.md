# Truth verification: reference

Source: `ShroomsQ/_CANON/_SSOT/00_EPISTEMOLOGY/📐 ssot epistemology truth verification.md`, version 1.0.2, status canonical, trunk BLACK, tier foundation, applies to OVEREXITOUT, ASTRO7EX and LAKAD, dependencies none, last updated 2026-10-04. Why it exists: see the [concept](1-concept.md).

## Core Concepts

The two ideas under the frameworks.

### Truth as Foundation

Truth is the cornerstone of knowledge generation. Diverse methods give holistic understanding, clarity, accuracy and informed decisions in narrative development and worldbuilding.

### Framework Integration

The ten frameworks work independently and in combination. One claim often needs several. Different frameworks give different validity perspectives.

## The 10 Truth Frameworks

Each framework has a Definition, Components, Application and Limitations, and a Sources line.

### Framework 1: Empirical Observation

- **Definition:** verifies truth by observation, measurement and experiment.
- **Components:** observation of phenomena; quantitative and qualitative measurement; controlled experiment; repeatable verification protocols.
- **Application:** the scientific method; verification by replication across multiple instances.
- **Limitations:** observable phenomena only; needs measurement tools; cannot verify non-physical claims.
- **Sources:** PSY.67 (Masters and Johnson, *Human Sexual Response*); PSY.52 (*Homosexuality in Perspective*).

### Framework 2: Logical Reasoning

- **Definition:** deductive, inductive and abductive processes derive truth from premises.
- **Components:** deductive (general to specific; validity depends on premise truth); inductive (specific to general; probability-based, falsifiable); abductive (most likely explanation; inference to best explanation).
- **Application:** mathematical proof; hypothesis generation; argument validity.
- **Limitations:** deduction needs true premises; induction cannot guarantee universal truth; abduction gives likelihood, not certainty.
- **Sources:** TEC.04 (Minto, *The Minto Pyramid Principle*, 2010: deduction versus induction).

### Framework 3: Intuition and A Priori Knowledge

- **Definition:** truth from self-evident principles, independent of sensory experience.
- **Components:** axioms; foundational logical principles; innate knowledge claims; reasoning without empirical evidence.
- **Application:** mathematical axioms (1 + 1 = 2); ethical foundations; logical laws such as non-contradiction.
- **Limitations:** intuition is hard to tell from bias; "self-evident" varies by culture; abstract domains only.
- **Sources:** method, no source (BOLO 100 D3, 10/4).

### Framework 4: Consensus

- **Definition:** truth through agreement among experts or communities.
- **Components:** expert agreement; community normative standards; scholarly consensus; peer review.
- **Application:** legal precedent; ethical standards; historical interpretation; scientific paradigm shifts.
- **Limitations:** consensus can be wrong; authority can override evidence; group dynamics can suppress dissent.
- **Sources:** method, no source (BOLO 100 D3, 10/4).

### Framework 5: Coherence Theory

- **Definition:** truth as consistency within a belief system.
- **Components:** internal consistency; systematic fit across propositions; rational integration; non-contradiction.
- **Application:** philosophical systems; worldbuilding consistency; doctrine coherence; narrative logic testing.
- **Limitations:** several coherent systems can contradict each other; coherence does not guarantee correspondence; may preserve systematic errors.
- **Sources:** BVX.0575 (Wolf and others: consistency, cohesion and coherence).

### Framework 6: Correspondence Theory

- **Definition:** truth as alignment with objective reality or facts.
- **Components:** reality alignment; objective fact evaluation; empirical correspondence checks; external world matching.
- **Application:** hypothesis testing; fact-checking; historical accuracy; everyday truth assessment.
- **Limitations:** needs access to objective reality; observer effect in quantum mechanics; facts vary by interpretation.
- **Sources:** BVX.0100 (Pavel, *Fictional Worlds*).

### Framework 7: Pragmatic Theory

- **Definition:** truth as what functions effectively in practice.
- **Components:** practical consequences; utility and effectiveness; contextual truth; functional outcomes.
- **Application:** engineering design validation; business strategy; treatment efficacy; policy impact.
- **Limitations:** "works" does not mean "true"; outcomes depend on context; short-term and long-term effects can conflict.
- **Sources:** TEC.03 (NASA Systems Engineering Handbook, 2016: validation).

### Framework 8: Skepticism and Critical Thinking

- **Definition:** rigorous evidence and systematic questioning of claims.
- **Components:** claim interrogation; high burden of proof; evidence quality assessment; assumption testing.
- **Application:** peer review; investigative journalism; philosophical inquiry; fraud detection.
- **Limitations:** excess skepticism blocks knowledge; infinite regress of justification; decision paralysis.
- **Sources:** TEC.06 (Vaughan, *Challenger Launch Decision*, 1996); MIL.05 (MCDP 2 Intelligence, 1997).

### Framework 9: Revelation or Faith

- **Definition:** truth through spiritual experience or divine communication.
- **Components:** spiritual insight; revelation claims; faith-based acceptance; religious authority structures.
- **Application:** doctrine formation; religious practice; spiritual narrative; moral frameworks.
- **Limitations:** non-falsifiable; revelations conflict across traditions; personal experience varies; little intersubjective verification.
- **Sources:** BVX.0836 (Campbell, *Creative Mythology*).

### Framework 10: Historical Method

- **Definition:** truth through documentary analysis and corroborated evidence.
- **Components:** primary source analysis; secondary corroboration; historiographical interpretation; temporal context reconstruction.
- **Application:** event verification; biographical accuracy; cultural evolution; precedent.
- **Limitations:** incomplete records; survivor bias; interpretation varies by historian; anachronism.
- **Sources:** MSX.55 (Katz, *Gay American History*).

## How the Frameworks Check the Systems

A table of system, claim and checking framework. Two rules apply to every row.

| System | Claim | Frameworks |
|---|---|---|
| Character (02) | A trait or change is true to the person. | Coherence; Logical Reasoning; Empirical Observation |
| Setting (03) | A canon fact: a place, rule or custom exists. | Coherence; Correspondence |
| Plot (04) | An event happened and caused the next one. | Logical Reasoning; Historical Method |
| Theme (07) | The story proves its premise. | Logical Reasoning; Pragmatic Theory |
| Tracking (08) | A record matches the story. | Correspondence; Coherence |
| Worldbuilding (09) | A world claim fits the world's own rules. | Coherence; Correspondence |

1. A claim one framework cannot confirm needs a second framework.
2. Skepticism (Framework 8) tests any claim in any row. A claim that survives is checked. A claim that fails stays marked open.

## Implementation

How to apply the frameworks.

### Research Design Integration

Methodology alignment has five steps: identify the nature of inquiry; select frameworks for the domain; apply several for cross-validation; assess each one's limits; document epistemological assumptions. Interdisciplinary pairs: empirical with logical for science; consensus with correspondence for history; coherence with pragmatic for worldbuilding.

### Truth Framework Selection Guide

| Domain | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Scientific Research | Empirical Observation, Logical Reasoning | Skepticism, Correspondence |
| Historical Analysis | Historical Method, Consensus | Coherence, Correspondence |
| Philosophical Inquiry | Logical Reasoning, A Priori Knowledge | Coherence, Skepticism |
| Narrative Development | Coherence, Pragmatic | Correspondence, Consensus |
| Ethical Reasoning | A Priori Knowledge, Consensus | Coherence, Pragmatic |

### Benefits of Framework Understanding

Five benefits: enhanced credibility, nuanced analysis, bias mitigation, interdisciplinary collaboration, critical thinking development.
