# The Goon Room keeps the mess and tags the signal

The Goon Room is the primary workspace for chaotic research, drafting, and creative flow. The source gives it that one purpose.

## A workspace that asks for no structure

The writer dumps thoughts, researches, drafts, and experiments. The source says no structure is required.

The source tells the writer not to worry about organization, cleanup, structure, or perfect formatting. The workspace removes the cost of tidiness so the writer keeps the flow.

## Tags do the sorting later

Freedom needs one safeguard, or the signal drowns. The writer tags as the work goes. The tag marks what the passage may become: canon, a method update, a story update, a test, or a keeper.

The weekly review reads those tags. The writer searches for `#canon-ready`, moves those passages to `_STAGED/`, and eventually promotes them to `_CANON/`. The mess stays in the Goon Room, and only tagged signal travels on.

## The rule for the writer's attention

The source names two things to focus on: capturing ideas, and tagging for later retrieval. Everything else waits. The dated file name gives each session one findable home.

The [reference](2-reference.md) lists every field and tag. The [how-to](3-how-to.md) covers the tasks. The [tutorial](4-tutorial.md) walks a first session.

## The long read

The design trades order now for order later. A tidy workspace tempts the writer to stop and organize mid-thought. The Goon Room refuses that temptation by naming organization as something not to worry about. The tags are the only promise the writer makes, and the weekly review is the only gate. A passage without a tag is not lost; it stays in its dated file and can be searched. A passage with `#canon-ready` has declared itself a candidate, and the review decides.
