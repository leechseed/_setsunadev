**Who it's for:** Chief or an agent deciding how binding a Command document is and where it files · **What it does:** explains why every Command document sits on one of four rungs and how the rung is chosen · **Type:** concept

# Every document sits on one rung

The **Command Ladder** names the four rungs every Command document sits on. The rung says how binding the document is and what it may contain. Chief ruled it on 2026-09-15: "let's go with the NASA four."

## Four rungs separate what binds from what only guides

NASA tiers its directives in four levels, and the Command borrows them in its own words: policy, procedure, standard, handbook. Policy says what the Command does and why. A procedure says how, and it is mandatory. A standard sets a measurable shall for a class of product or process. A handbook gives guidance with "should" and forbids nothing.

## A spec is a tag, not a fifth rung

DoD's spec idea fits one document saying what one specific product must be. The Command keeps it as a tag on nodes and charters. Examples are the BOLO 24 charter and a persona sheet. A fifth rung would mix a kind of document with a kind of binding.

## One rule decides the rung

The decision runs in order. A document that forbids nothing is a handbook, since a rule that forbids nothing is decoration (DOCTRINE 0, Invariant III). A document that measures a product is a standard. A document that governs how the Command works is a procedure. A document that says what the Command is and why is policy.

## The policy rung stays tiny

The ladder keeps policy small: DOCTRINE 0 and nothing else until something earns it (Invariant I).

## The tag makes the rung visible

Every document carries one frontmatter line, first after the opening fence. The filing table records which documents carry it. A flat index shows where documents govern, depend, and cite outside bodies. The index also exposes the gaps: empty rungs at several levels.
