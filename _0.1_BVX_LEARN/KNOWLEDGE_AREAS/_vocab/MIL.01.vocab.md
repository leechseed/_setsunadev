# MIL.01 — VOCAB — MCDP 1 Warfighting

## CORE THESIS

- **independent wills** — the two sides of a conflict, each choosing freely; war is their collision, never one side's plan
  - ↳ "War is a contest between independent wills fought in permanent friction, uncertainty, and disorder, so certainty …"
- **friction** — the force that resists every action and makes the simple difficult
  - ↳ "… is a contest between independent wills fought in permanent friction, uncertainty, and disorder, so certainty and positive control are …"
- **uncertainty** — the permanent absence of complete information; the fog of war
  - ↳ "… a contest between independent wills fought in permanent friction, uncertainty, and disorder, so certainty and positive control are impossible; …"
- **disorder** — the breakdown of plans and order that grows as action proceeds
  - ↳ "… between independent wills fought in permanent friction, uncertainty, and disorder, so certainty and positive control are impossible; the side …"
- **certainty** — full knowledge before acting; unattainable, never a precondition for action
  - ↳ "… a contest between independent wills fought in permanent friction, uncertainty, and disorder, so certainty and positive control are impossible; …"
- **positive control** — directing every action from the top; impossible in war
  - ↳ "… in permanent friction, uncertainty, and disorder, so certainty and positive control are impossible; the side that wins is the side …"
- **superior tempo** — operating faster than the enemy, consistently, over time
  - ↳ "… that stops trying to control events and instead generates superior tempo — decentralizing decisions to subordinates who act on the …"
- **decentralizing decisions** — pushing decisions down to those who hold the local information
  - ↳ "… to control events and instead generates superior tempo — decentralizing decisions to subordinates who act on the commander's intent, focusing …"
- **subordinates** — the people who execute; in this doctrine, the decision-makers on the spot
  - ↳ "… and instead generates superior tempo — decentralizing decisions to subordinates who act on the commander's intent, focusing strength against …"
- **act** — to move on intent without waiting for orders or certainty
  - ↳ "… generates superior tempo — decentralizing decisions to subordinates who act on the commander's intent, focusing strength against critical vulnerabilities, …"
- **commander's intent** — the purpose behind a task; the why that outlasts the what
  - ↳ "… — decentralizing decisions to subordinates who act on the commander's intent, focusing strength against critical vulnerabilities, and shattering the enemy's …"
- **strength** — combat power, concentrated where it decides
  - ↳ "… to subordinates who act on the commander's intent, focusing strength against critical vulnerabilities, and shattering the enemy's cohesion rather …"
- **critical vulnerabilities** — the weak points that, struck, damage the enemy's ability to resist the most
  - ↳ "… who act on the commander's intent, focusing strength against critical vulnerabilities, and shattering the enemy's cohesion rather than grinding down …"
## INVARIANTS

- **Zweikampf** — Clausewitz's two-struggle; war as two wrestlers, force against counterforce
  - ↳ "The enemy is an independent will, not an object. War is a Zweikampf — two wrestlers, each exerting force and counterforce."
- **force** — one will's exertion on the other
  - ↳ "The enemy is an independent will, not an object. War is a Zweikampf — two wrestlers, each exerting force and counterforce."
- **counterforce** — the opposing will's answer to force
  - ↳ "The enemy is an independent will, not an object. War is a Zweikampf — two wrestlers, each exerting force and counterforce."
- **plan** — a scheme of action; in war, always met by a counter-plan
  - ↳ "Every plan meets a counter-plan; the situation is a product of continuous mutual adaptation."
- **counter-plan** — the enemy's answering scheme; the reason no plan survives contact
  - ↳ "Every plan meets a counter-plan; the situation is a product of continuous mutual adaptation."
- **situation** — the state of affairs at a moment; produced jointly, owned by neither side
  - ↳ "Every plan meets a counter-plan; the situation is a product of continuous mutual adaptation."
- **continuous mutual adaptation** — each side changing in response to the other, without end
  - ↳ "Every plan meets a counter-plan; the situation is a product of continuous mutual adaptation."
- **energy** — the capacity to act, which friction drains
  - ↳ "Friction is permanent. Friction resists all action and saps energy; it makes the simple difficult and the difficult seemingly impossible."
- **Self-induced friction** — friction a force creates for itself: unclear goals, complicated plans, complex organization
  - ↳ "Self-induced friction (unclear goals, complicated plans, complex organization) can be reduced; external friction cannot be eliminated, only fought through by will — while raising the enemy's."
- **unclear goals** — aims not understood by those executing them; a source of self-induced friction
  - ↳ "Self-induced friction (unclear goals, complicated plans, complex organization) can be reduced; external friction cannot be eliminated, only fought through by will — while raising the enemy's."
- **complicated plans** — plans with more parts than friction allows; a source of self-induced friction
  - ↳ "Self-induced friction (unclear goals, complicated plans, complex organization) can be reduced; external friction cannot be eliminated, only fought through by will — while raising the enemy's."
- **complex organization** — structure with too many layers and links; a source of self-induced friction
  - ↳ "Self-induced friction (unclear goals, complicated plans, complex organization) can be reduced; external friction cannot be eliminated, only fought through by will — while raising the enemy's."
- **external friction** — friction from the enemy, terrain, weather, and chance; cannot be eliminated
  - ↳ "Self-induced friction (unclear goals, complicated plans, complex organization) can be reduced; external friction cannot be eliminated, only fought through by will — while raising the enemy's."
- **will** — the moral force that pushes through friction
  - ↳ "Self-induced friction (unclear goals, complicated plans, complex organization) can be reduced; external friction cannot be eliminated, only fought through by will — while raising the enemy's."
- **fog of war** — the atmosphere of uncertainty in which all action takes place
  - ↳ "Certainty is unattainable; every action rests on incomplete information. The fog of war is structural."
- **structural** — built into the nature of war; not a defect to be fixed
  - ↳ "Certainty is unattainable; every action rests on incomplete information. The fog of war is structural."
- **Nonlinearity** — causes and effects out of proportion; small incidents decide outcomes
  - ↳ "Nonlinearity means minor incidents produce decisive effects."
- **minor incidents** — small events with outsized effects under nonlinearity
  - ↳ "Nonlinearity means minor incidents produce decisive effects."
- **decisive effects** — outcomes that settle the matter
  - ↳ "Nonlinearity means minor incidents produce decisive effects."
- **Judgment** — estimating possibilities and probabilities under uncertainty
  - ↳ "Judgment estimates possibilities and probabilities; it never removes them."
- **possibilities** — what could happen; judgment's raw material
  - ↳ "Judgment estimates possibilities and probabilities; it never removes them."
- **probabilities** — how likely each possibility is; judgment's estimate
  - ↳ "Judgment estimates possibilities and probabilities; it never removes them."
- **communications** — the links between units; they fail as disorder grows
  - ↳ "Disorder increases over time; only a general framework of order can be imposed. Plans go awry, communications fail, and repeated improvisation carries actions far from the original scheme."
- **improvisation** — acting off-plan when the plan has failed; a permanent necessity
  - ↳ "Disorder increases over time; only a general framework of order can be imposed. Plans go awry, communications fail, and repeated improvisation carries actions far from the original scheme."
- **original scheme** — the plan as first conceived, from which action drifts
  - ↳ "Disorder increases over time; only a general framework of order can be imposed. Plans go awry, communications fail, and repeated improvisation carries actions far from the original scheme."
- **commander** — the one who shapes the general flow and does not control each event
  - ↳ "The commander influences the general flow; the commander does not control each event."
- **influences** — shapes the flow without controlling it; the commander's real verb
  - ↳ "The commander influences the general flow; the commander does not control each event."
- **general flow** — the overall direction of events; the most a commander can steer
  - ↳ "The commander influences the general flow; the commander does not control each event."
- **control** — command of each event; the thing war does not permit
  - ↳ "The commander influences the general flow; the commander does not control each event."
- **event** — a single occurrence in the flow; not controllable one by one
  - ↳ "The commander influences the general flow; the commander does not control each event."
- **Outcomes** — results, which emerge from many local decisions
  - ↳ "War is a complex, distributed system; centralization contradicts its nature. Outcomes emerge from countless local decisions made on local information."
- **local decisions** — choices made on the spot by those present
  - ↳ "War is a complex, distributed system; centralization contradicts its nature. Outcomes emerge from countless local decisions made on local information."
- **local information** — what is known where the action is, and nowhere else
  - ↳ "War is a complex, distributed system; centralization contradicts its nature. Outcomes emerge from countless local decisions made on local information."
- **Full centralization** — one decision-maker for everything; contradicts the nature of war
  - ↳ "Full centralization under a single decisionmaker is inconsistent with what war is."
- **single decisionmaker** — the top of a fully centralized system; the wrong model
  - ↳ "Full centralization under a single decisionmaker is inconsistent with what war is."
- **Material factors** — forces, equipment, numbers; measurable, less decisive
  - ↳ "Moral and mental forces outweigh physical forces. Material factors are easier to measure and less decisive."
- **doctrine** — a shared way of thinking about war; the thing being read
  - ↳ "Any doctrine that reduces war to ratios of forces and equipment is inherently flawed."
- **effect of fires** — what firepower does; its greatest effect is moral, not material
  - ↳ "The greatest effect of fires is on the enemy's moral strength, not on their matériel."
- **moral strength** — the will and cohesion of a force; the true target of fires
  - ↳ "The greatest effect of fires is on the enemy's moral strength, not on their matériel."
- **matériel** — equipment and supplies; the physical, secondary target
  - ↳ "The greatest effect of fires is on the enemy's moral strength, not on their matériel."
- **Military action** — the use of force; a political instrument
  - ↳ "War must serve policy. Military action is a political instrument and its conduct must support political aims."
- **political instrument** — a tool of policy, which is what military action is
  - ↳ "War must serve policy. Military action is a political instrument and its conduct must support political aims."
- **political aims** — the ends of policy that military action must serve
  - ↳ "War must serve policy. Military action is a political instrument and its conduct must support political aims."
- **Speed** — rapidity in time; a weapon
  - ↳ "Relative speed and focus are the universal generators of combat power. Speed is a weapon; focus is the convergence of effects at the decisive time and place."
- **weapon** — anything that generates combat power, including speed
  - ↳ "Relative speed and focus are the universal generators of combat power. Speed is a weapon; focus is the convergence of effects at the decisive time and place."
- **focus** — convergence of effects at the decisive time and place
  - ↳ "Relative speed and focus are the universal generators of combat power. Speed is a weapon; focus is the convergence of effects at the decisive time and place."
- **convergence of effects** — many actions arriving together where it matters
  - ↳ "Relative speed and focus are the universal generators of combat power. Speed is a weapon; focus is the convergence of effects at the decisive time and place."
- **prudent risk** — risk accepted deliberately to achieve focus; economy everywhere else
  - ↳ "Focus demands strict economy elsewhere and therefore the acceptance of prudent risk."
- **Risk** — exposure to loss; inherent in action and inaction alike
  - ↳ "Risk is inherent in action and inaction alike."
