## CORE THESIS
Information is a warfighting function because advantage in it is never given: in a global, always-on environment where every action is a message and a local act is a global event in seconds, a force wins by generating, preserving, denying, and projecting information more effectively than its opponent — in combination, with a maneuver mindset — to create and exploit three advantages (systems overmatch, prevailing narrative, force resiliency) that let it influence the opponent's will and impose its own.

---

## INVARIANTS
*These are the unchanging principles — true regardless of context.*

1. **Advantage is fought for, never assumed.** The information age ended the presumption of persistent information superiority. Some advantages pre-exist, most are temporary, and all can be exploited by either side. Any advantage held must be exploited before it expires.

2. **The information environment is global, enduring, and never off.** Light-speed communication gives anyone with a device global reach; the levels of warfare compress — a tactical act becomes strategic in seconds, and the battlespace no longer has hierarchical or geographic boundaries. Assume every action will be exposed, communicated globally, and distorted by an opponent for disproportionate effect.

3. **Every action is a message.** All activity is physically detectable and observable and therefore communicates, intentionally or not. Presence, posture, and action are always open to interpretation — including by parties not yet in the fight who decide to enter based on what they see.

4. **Narrative gives meaning to facts, and credibility decides which narrative prevails.** The prevailing narrative is the one that is credible and resonates most with the intended audience. Credibility comes from visible action matching words. Narratives already exist in every society; new ones are rarely created — a command narrative is nested inside the existing ones.

5. **The four functions operate only in combination.** Generate, preserve, deny, and project are mutually supporting, mutually reinforcing, and overlapping; projection is always coordinated with denial. A plan that uses one in isolation leaves the others to the opponent.

6. **Every objective has a cognitive and a functional component.** Information is the input to both thinking (human) and non-thinking (machine) processes, which yields two avenues of approach to any objective — the operator and the system — each exploitable directly or indirectly.

7. **Trust is the basis of belief.** People no longer experience most events directly; the technology that mediates reality is the target. The human–machine interface is where perception is shaped, and volume, velocity, and value are the tangible levers on it.

8. **Information is the commander's business and every member's role.** It is not the realm of specialists. The commander integrates it into every plan; every individual protects it, leverages it, and understands what their own conduct communicates.

9. **The mindset is offensive and the model is maneuver.** Penetrate the opponent's system and tear it apart from within — do not wear it down. Assault through the ambush. The ultimate aim of an information advantage is to influence the opponent's will.

## HEURISTICS
*Rules of thumb for real-time decision-making.*

| Situation | Do This | Not This |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Planning any operation or project | Develop the narrative during problem framing, concurrent with planning; state audiences, narrative, what is denied, how effect is measured | Bolt communications on at the end |
| Entering a new audience or environment | Map the existing narratives and key stakeholders; nest your credible narrative inside them | Invent a new, unfamiliar narrative and expect it to take |
| Words and actions diverge | Fix the action — messages are made credible by visible action on the ground | Message harder |
| You acted against your own narrative | Be transparent and truthful, apologize if applicable, re-affirm the narrative, direct consistent action, then showcase the correction | Deny, delay, hope it passes |
| Opponent's narrative is resonating | Highlight the gap between what they say and do with evidence; disrupt their distribution | Argue inside their frame |
| About to post, speak, or act where it can be observed | Evaluate second- and third-order effects; assume global exposure and hostile distortion | Treat it as local, private, or off-duty |
| Building awareness of the environment | Generate: gain and maintain access; record and archive (screenshots, unit history, documents) for future use | Assume you will remember or find it later |
| Facing an opponent's strength | Indirect approach — your strength against their critical vulnerability | Strength against strength (attrition) |
| Under information disruption or setback | Continue action on intent with the information available; maintain tempo; execute rehearsed drills — isolate, disable, restore | Halt until the picture is clear |
| Deciding whether concealment or deception is worth it | It is, at any scale, in almost all situations; plan it and coordinate projection with denial | Skip it because it costs time |
| Judging whether the narrative works | Measure effectiveness with the intended audience even though it is subjective and slow | Count outputs and assume effect |
| Consuming information | Media literacy — distinguish primary source, news, commentary, manipulated media, parody, opinion; know your own cognitive biases | Trust the screen because it is on the screen |
| Judging a piece of information | Value it by its contribution to the decision at hand — timing, accuracy, relevance, context, trust | Value it by volume or recency |
| Running a training exercise or rehearsal | Treat it as observed; design which signatures to amplify and which to suppress | Treat it as a private rehearsal |

## CORE CONCEPTS (10)
*The essential theories, solutions, and mechanics.*

**1. Information as the Seventh Warfighting Function**
The newest Marine Corps warfighting function, alongside command and control, maneuver, fires, intelligence, logistics, and force protection. Like each of them it groups similar activities, and its purpose distinguishes it: to create and exploit information advantages in pursuit of mission objectives. All functions depend on assured access to trusted, timely, relevant information, and every one of them has inherent informational aspects — maneuver signals intent and stimulates the enemy to act so he can be observed; fires are used more for cognitive effect on will than for physical effect; logistics footprints expose intent; force protection communicates resolve. Institutionalizing information means making it a primary instrument of maneuver warfare.

**2. Information Advantage and Its Three Types**
Information advantage is an exploitable condition resulting from one actor's ability to generate, preserve, deny, and project information more effectively than another. Three primary types: **systems overmatch** — technical advantage yielding fires, intelligence, maneuver, logistics, force protection, or command and control advantages, won by disrupting the opponent's information-dependent systems while protecting your own (access is the precondition); **prevailing narrative** — public opinion or perception advantage yielding trust, credibility, believability; **force resiliency** — the ability to resist, counter, and prevail against reconnaissance, technical disruption, and malign activity. These generate further advantages: decision speed, surprise, tempo, mass, psychological.

**3. The Four Functions of Information**
**Generation** — gain and maintain access to the information environment; build awareness of threats, vulnerabilities, and opportunities; hold systems at risk; create the information needed to plan and operate (includes recording and archiving for future use). **Preservation** — protect and defend the information, systems, and networks used for planning and operations against internal and external threats; build and maintain the historical record; recognize and dispel misinformation and disinformation. **Denial** — disrupt or destroy the information the opponent needs to understand, decide, or act in coordination; includes concealment, OPSEC, communication discipline, signature management, counterintelligence. **Projection** — communicate, transmit, or deliver information of any type to inform, influence, or deceive an observer or targeted system, by words, media, or observable physical action. Always planned in combination; projection always coordinated with denial.

**4. The Information Environment and Its Characteristics**
The global competitive space spanning the warfighting domains where all operations depend on information — including information itself and every social, cultural, psychological, technical, and physical factor bearing on decisions. Five characteristics: instant and persistent global visibility; compressed levels of warfare and battlespace; truth, trust, and belief (trust in the source is the primary basis for judging true or false; misinformation is false without intent to harm, disinformation is false with intent); volume, velocity, and value (velocity is a function of volume against bandwidth and noise; value is judged by contribution to the decision); thinking and non-thinking processes. Competitors treat this environment as permanent war — blurring peace and war, winning without fighting, controlling narrative (Three Warfares: public opinion/media, psychological, legal), manipulating perception (reflexive control), and using technologies of decision advantage, disruption, and destruction.

**5. Informational Power and the Attributes of War**
Competition and war redistribute power through a contest of wills; informational power is the use of information, narratives, and technical means to advance interests. The attributes of war are information-centric: ambiguity and uncertainty are weapons that induce friction and hesitation, so Marines both defend against them and inject them into the opponent; complexity and fluidity degrade the opponent's ability to link cause and effect; the human dimension means the aim is the opponent's will; physical, moral, and mental factors interact — and the moral and mental (morale, leadership, esprit) are targets defended by tangible information means.

**6. Cognitive and Functional Components; Direct and Indirect Approach**
Every objective has a cognitive component (the human operator's perception, decision, will) and a functional component (the system's ability to process information). Each is exploited directly or indirectly. Cognitive-direct: alter what the operator perceives (decoys on the radar display). Cognitive-indirect: erode the will to stay in the fight (tailored propaganda). Functional-direct: render the system inoperable (electromagnetic attack). Functional-indirect: cut a supporting link the system depends on (denial of service against the C2 node). The direct approach is strength against strength (attrition); the indirect is strength against critical vulnerability (maneuver).

**7. Narrative Competition**
Narratives are essential to every operation because they give meaning to facts, and people understand the world through stories translatable to real experience. Method: generate knowledge of the audience's mental and moral factors and of the pre-existing friendly, neutral, and opponent narratives; nest a credible command narrative inside the existing cultural ones (some are too entrenched to replace — work within them); back every message with visible action; measure effectiveness with the intended audience; preserve by consistent, proactive messaging through multiple redundant channels and by archiving fact-based evidence (photo, video, audio, documents); deny the opponent's narrative by exposing the gap between their words and deeds and by disrupting their distribution. Expect credibility to be attacked the moment a message goes out. Projecting in a narrative contest is nearly everything you do.

**8. Force Resiliency**
Two kinds of disruption: cognitive (disinformation, propaganda aimed at how the force perceives itself and its situation) and functional (attacks on the systems it uses). Commanders instill the "assault through the ambush" mentality, build individual and unit action drills, and train against them routinely. The mindset is offensive; a running estimate of threats, vulnerabilities, and opportunities prevents complacency. Setback recovery: for a narrative setback — transparency, apology if applicable, re-affirm, act consistently, showcase the correction; for a systems setback — isolate, disable, restore. Media literacy and awareness of one's own cognitive biases make the force harder to influence. Continuing action on intent with a degraded capability preserves tempo and forces the opponent to react.

**9. Deception**
All warfare is based on deception; surprise is convincing the opponent you will do something other than what you plan, and the greater the surprise the smaller the force and the fewer the casualties. Three categories: tactical deception (any unit — feints, demonstrations, ruses, displays, decoys, camouflage), joint military deception (operational-level, within approved authorities, weighing effects on credibility), and deception in support of OPSEC (multiple false or confusing indicators aimed only at foreign intelligence collection). Adopt an ambush mindset in competition as well as combat: deception at any scale is worth its cost in almost every situation.

**10. Command Narrative, Nesting, and Information in Planning**
The command narrative is distinct from and broader than commander's intent: it anchors and guides all public communication, exercises, and operations a unit performs, serves internal and external audiences, provides enduring context for presence and mission, and when done well inspires allies and deters competitors. It is a staff process that begins during problem framing and runs concurrent with planning and design; it is nested under higher and strategic narratives and then developed into supporting themes and messages. In planning, any unit or element can be tasked to generate, preserve, deny, or project; an information effect can be the main effort; effects on audiences are monitored and actions adjusted. Doctrine gives the philosophy; training develops the proficiencies; education develops the understanding of the daily information contest.

## WHAT DOESN'T WORK
Assuming information superiority, treating information as a specialist's job, adding communications after the plan is made, inventing a new narrative instead of nesting in existing ones, messaging harder when actions contradict the message, non-credible narratives, treating any action as local or private, ignoring second- and third-order effects, using one function of information in isolation, projecting without coordinating denial, strength-against-strength when a critical vulnerability is available, halting under disruption until the picture clears, skipping deception because it costs time, counting outputs instead of measuring effect on the audience, trusting the screen without media literacy, ignoring one's own cognitive biases, failing to archive the record, treating exercises as unobserved rehearsals, denying or delaying after a narrative misstep, the peace-or-war binary when the opponent is in permanent competition, technology as a substitute for judgment of trust and value.

## APPLICATION — THE OPERATION

- **Information advantage → the persona account is an information operation (BOLO 24).** The four functions are the launch checklist. *Generate*: audience research, the reference set (5 core / 25 wide), screenshots of the register being copied, archived for future use. *Preserve*: the persona bible and canon, the post archive, the historical record — and recovery when a post contradicts the story. *Deny*: the operator–persona boundary — metadata, location, timing, device and account hygiene, and the `_PRIVATE/` gitignore line in a public repo. *Project*: the posting cadence, the grief season, the actions that make the narrative believable. Planned in combination or not at all.

- **Command narrative ≠ commander's intent → the persona bible is the command narrative.** Who she is, what she does, why she does it — the enduring context that anchors every post, season, and shoot, for both audiences (Papi/Claude internally, followers externally). Each post or season gets its own intent; the command narrative is what all of them nest under. Ultrasin and Bold Venture are the strategic narratives above it.

- **Nest, don't invent; credibility is action → the Baddie is an existing narrative.** Taxonomy already rules Baddie an attitude archetype; the reference account is the register. Doctrine says join the existing story rather than build an unfamiliar one, and back it with visible action. "Fictional arc mirrors the real arc, believability is the bar" is the doctrinal credibility rule. The canon-age rail is narrative preservation and legal protection in one line.

- **Narrative preservation → expect the say/do gap to be attacked.** The moment a message goes out, the opponent (audience, platform, rival) looks for the contradiction. Consistent proactive messaging across redundant channels; the archive as fact-based evidence; and the setback drill written before launch — transparent, re-affirm, act consistently, showcase the correction. The grief mechanic is a preservation asset only if it is never contradicted.

- **Force resiliency → the operator is the target.** Cognitive disruption (dogpiles, comment brigades, exposure attempts) and functional disruption (account loss, shadow-ban, platform rule change) are the ambushes. Write the action drills before day one: isolate, disable, restore for the account; re-affirm and act for the narrative. Continue posting on intent with degraded capability — tempo is resiliency.

- **Compressed levels and discipline in the information environment → nothing is local.** Every post, DM, and repo commit is evaluated for second- and third-order effects and assumed to be globally visible and hostilely distorted. The repo is public: what belongs in `_PRIVATE/` is a deny decision, made in advance, not after.

- **Volume, velocity, value → the posting doctrine.** Value is judged by contribution to the decision (the follower's conversion), not by volume. Rate, transmission medium, and style of presentation are levers on perception — the medium-grammar layer of the story lattice is where those levers live.

- **What MCDP 8 adds to the standard PM flow (BOLO 25).** An information line in every charter: (1) audiences — internal and external; (2) the narrative, developed at problem framing and nested under the trunk narrative; (3) the deny list — what is protected, from whom, how; (4) the measure of narrative effect with the intended audience; (5) which element of the project is tasked to generate, preserve, deny, or project, and whether an information effect is the main effort. A project's public face is a warfighting function of the project, not a communications afterthought.
