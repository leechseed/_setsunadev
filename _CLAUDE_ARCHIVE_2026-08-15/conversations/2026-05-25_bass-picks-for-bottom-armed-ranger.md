A cheap second phone or a small action camera mounted from the front, capturing your torso and posture while your primary camera stays on the rear, lets you cut between angles in editing and immediately doubles the production value of the same shoot. The next upgrades after that, in order of impact, are a continuous LED panel light (more important for video than for stills because shadows shift with motion), a lavalier or small shotgun microphone clipped just out of frame (audio quality is the single largest gap between amateur and professional video), and a basic editing app such as CapCut or DaVinci Resolve for cuts, color correction, and audio leveling. Total additional spend for that full video kit runs around $100 to $200 depending on the action cam.

The folding-chair-reversed setup you already have is genuinely strong and worth developing into a signature shot. Straddling a chair backwards puts the rear at camera height, frames the thighs through the chair back, and creates a visual rhythm that flat-on-the-bed shots cannot replicate. To elevate what you already have: shoot it against a solid-colored backdrop rather than a visible room (this also protects anonymity), side-light from one direction with the LED panel, and add the second-angle camera so every take produces both rear and torso footage from the same session. Three or four shoots in that exact configuration, with controlled lighting and two angles, give you a recurring signature scene type that builds visual recognition across your library and becomes part of your brand the same way a logo would.
