This is settled physics and conceptual, so no search or tools needed — I'll keep it concise and skip the padding.The term you're reaching for is **quantum entanglement**. Two (or more) particles get correlated such that measuring one instantly determines the state of the other, no matter how far apart they are. Einstein famously sneered at it as "spooky action at a distance" (*spukhafte Fernwirkung*).

The jargon cluster around it:

- **Entanglement** — the core phenomenon. Particles share a joint quantum state; you can't describe one without the other.
- **Bell's theorem / Bell inequalities** — the math proving this correlation is *real* and can't be explained by "hidden variables" (i.e. the particles weren't just secretly pre-set). Testing this won the 2022 Nobel (Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger).
- **EPR paradox** — Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen, the 1935 thought experiment that first poked at this as a supposed flaw in quantum mechanics.
- **Wavefunction collapse / measurement** — the act of measuring "fixes" the state.
- **Quantum nonlocality** — the umbrella term for "this correlation ignores distance."

Now the pushback, because the pop-science framing trips everyone here: **you cannot instantaneously communicate through entanglement.** This is the single most common misconception about it.

The reason is the **no-communication theorem**. When you measure your particle, you get a *random* outcome. Your partner across the galaxy also gets a random outcome. The two are correlated — but neither of you can *control* what you get, so neither can encode a message in it. You only discover the correlation later by comparing notes through a normal (light-speed-limited) channel. So no FTL telephone. The correlation is instant; usable information transfer is not.

Where entanglement *does* pay off: **quantum computing** (entangled qubits), **quantum cryptography / QKD** (tamper-evident key distribution), and **quantum teleportation** — which, despite the name, still requires a classical channel and moves a *state*, not matter or faster-than-light info.
