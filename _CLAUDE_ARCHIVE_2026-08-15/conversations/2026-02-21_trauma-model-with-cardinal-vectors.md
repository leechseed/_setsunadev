Here's the full research report. Let me walk you through the key decisions:

**The four cardinals, validated:**

- **Neglect** — trauma by *omission* (nothing given). Directly supported by Draijer's 2D model which empirically separates it from acute trauma as a distinct predictive axis.
- **Abandonment** — the better word for "unwanted." It captures both active rejection and passive emotional unavailability, and maps directly to Bowlby's attachment theory and ICD-11 C-PTSD criteria. "Unwanted" is the *felt experience*; Abandonment is the *clinical construct* that explains it.
- **Violation** — the classic "big T" trauma, trauma by *commission*. This is what most people mean when they say "trauma." It's mechanistically distinct because it's the only cardinal that doesn't require a relational failure to operate — it can happen with fully functional attachment.
- **Betrayal** — the fourth cardinal that your sketch was missing. Freyd's Betrayal Trauma Theory establishes this as neurologically and psychologically distinct from Violation because it requires *suppression of awareness* to preserve the attachment. Caregiver-perpetrated abuse is the prototype.

**The design pattern** maps directly to radar/spider charts used in psychology assessment. The polygon's *area* = total burden, and its *shape* = the dominant wound type — which directly guides intervention selection. The quadrant interaction effects (especially Neglect + Abandonment and Violation + Betrayal) are where the model gets clinically interesting, as those combinations produce qualitatively different presentations than any single cardinal alone.
