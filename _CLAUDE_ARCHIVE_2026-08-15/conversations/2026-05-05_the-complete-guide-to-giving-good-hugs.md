**The Philosophy**

A good hug is about *presence and pressure*. Most bad hugs fail because the person is mentally already pulling away — they're thinking about the hug ending while it's still happening. A good hug commits for the moment it lasts. Firm enough that the other person feels held, loose enough that they can breathe. Lead with your chest, not your hips. The "3-second rule" is real-ish — hugs lasting 3+ seconds trigger oxytocin release, which is why a quick pat-pat hug feels hollow and a held hug feels like something.

**The Steps (default friendly hug)**

1. Open arms early — telegraph it so they can match your energy
2. One arm goes over their shoulder, one under their arm (you both naturally figure out which side)
3. Hands land flat between shoulder blades, not flapping or floating
4. Squeeze once, firmly but not crushing — like a confident handshake's worth of pressure
5. Hold ~2-3 seconds
6. Release at the same time they do (read their body, don't be the first to break or the last to let go)

The big rookie mistakes: the limp noodle (no pressure), the pat-pat-pat (signals "okay we're done now" and kills it), the hip-pull-back A-frame (looks like you're avoiding them), and the over-hold (where you're still hugging and they've gone stiff).

**Types of Hugs**

**The Bro Hug** — Right hand goes into a handshake/grip first, then you pull in and clap the other guy on the back with your left hand 2-3 times. Hips can stay back. Hand lands on upper back/shoulder blade, firm slap-pats. Used for: greeting male friends, post-game, "good to see you man." Philosophy: affection without intimacy, the slaps are punctuation that say "we're close but masculine about it."

**The A-Frame / Side Hug** — Lean in shoulders only, hips back, often one-armed around the shoulders. Hand on far shoulder. Used for: coworkers, acquaintances, church, photo ops, people you're not sure want a real hug. Philosophy: warmth with a clear boundary. It's the safest hug. Also the most forgettable.

**The Friendly Full Hug** — Both arms, full chest contact, hand between shoulder blades, 2-3 seconds. Used for: close friends, family, hellos and goodbyes you actually mean. Philosophy: this is the default "real" hug. If you only master one, master this.

**The Bear Hug** — Both arms wrapped tight, often with a slight lift or rock side-to-side, big squeeze. Hands can be anywhere on the back, pressure is the point. Used for: reunions, big celebrations, when someone just got engaged/promoted/back from deployment. Philosophy: the hug as physical exclamation point. Reserved for big moments — a bear hug for a casual hello is unhinged behavior.

**The Comfort Hug** — Slower entry, one hand between shoulder blades and one higher up cradling the back of the head or upper back, longer hold (10+ seconds is fine), no pat-pat. You stay still and let them be in it. Used for: grief, breakups, bad news, someone crying. Philosophy: you are a wall they can lean on. Don't rush them out of it. Don't talk during it unless they talk first.

**The Romantic Hug** — Full body contact, hand can drift to lower back, head tucks into shoulder/neck, longer hold, sometimes a slow sway. Pressure is even and sustained. Used for: partners, hellos after time apart, "I missed you." Philosophy: this is intimacy, not just affection. The lower-back hand placement is the tell that distinguishes this from a friendly hug.

**The Intimate/Lovers Hug** — Chest to chest, hand on lower back or waist, other hand on neck or in hair, faces close, sometimes forehead-to-forehead. Long hold. Used for: between partners, often as a pause inside a longer moment. Philosophy: the hug as a destination, not a transition. You're not hugging hello or goodbye, you're just *being* there together.

**The Greeting/Goodbye Hug** — Quick, 1-2 seconds, both arms but not full pressure, hand high on the back. Used for: airport pickups, parties, "see you next week." Philosophy: it's a punctuation mark, not a paragraph. Keep it light, mean it, move on.

The single biggest upgrade most people can make: **commit to the hug you're giving.** Whatever type it is, be in it while it's happening. People remember the hugger who was actually there.
