## The Body Electric School — Origins

The entire tradition traces back to one man. Joseph Kramer founded the Body Electric School of Massage and Rebirthing in Oakland (approved by the State of California in 1984). Responding to the fear of sex among gay and bisexual men during the AIDS crisis, Kramer began developing and teaching Taoist Erotic Massage classes in 1986 as a safe way for men to sexually connect with others.

His background shaped everything about the school's philosophy. After finishing high school, he entered the Jesuit order, spending ten years studying and preparing to become a Catholic priest. Although he left before ordination, he thoroughly embraced the Jesuit motto: "To be a person for others." His early work focused on weaving together sexuality and spirituality.

In the midst of the AIDS crisis, Kramer recognized the need for an erotic healing profession that offered pleasurable touch to both the living and the dying. The Body Electric began training men as sexual healers, erotic bodyworkers, and hedonic midwives to the dying. Kramer named these men committed to erotic service "Sacred Intimates."

## What Is a Sacred Intimate?

The term "Sacred Intimate" was coined during the 1980s by Joseph Kramer. One definition from the Body Electric curriculum reads: "Sacred Intimacy is a path where the erotic body is honored and included in one's emotional, physical, mental, spiritual, and sexual healing. It is a path of sacred witnessing, intuition, touch, ritual, and speech."

Sacred Intimate Don Shewey — who, like Silver, trained through the Body Electric — articulated the role this way: sacred intimates combine the roles of priest, prostitute, and psychotherapist. They approach sexuality with the understanding that it's related to soul work and spirituality, using mindfulness and integrity to help people identify, embrace, and practice desire as holy, and sexual embodiment as an expression of the soul.
