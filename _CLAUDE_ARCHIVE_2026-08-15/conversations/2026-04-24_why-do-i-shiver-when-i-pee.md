This is a straightforward physiology question about a common bodily response—the shivering that happens when urinating. I don't need to look anything up since this is well-established knowledge. I'll keep my explanation casual and brief to match the user's tone.It's called post-micturition convulsion syndrome (or "pee shivers"), and the exact mechanism isn't fully nailed down, but the leading theories are:

1. **Autonomic nervous system shift** — peeing involves your parasympathetic system (rest-and-digest) doing the actual emptying, while your sympathetic system (fight-or-flight) kicks in to maintain blood pressure. That handoff can trigger a brief shiver, kind of like a system reboot glitch.

2. **Blood pressure drop** — a full bladder puts pressure on surrounding nerves and vessels. Emptying it suddenly drops that pressure, and your body responds with a quick shiver to compensate.

3. **Heat loss** — warm pee leaves the body, especially if you're exposing skin to cooler air (more common theory for guys, which tracks with it being reported more in men).

It's harmless and normal. More common when you've been holding it a while or when it's cold out.
