The user wants to know about gourmand perfumes and preferences in that category.## Gourmand Perfumes

**Gourmand** is a fragrance family built around edible, dessert-like notes — scents that smell like food but worn on skin. The term comes from the French word for "one who loves to eat."

### Core Notes
Gourmands are built from sweet, indulgent ingredients like vanilla, caramel, chocolate, coffee, praline, tonka bean, marshmallow, coconut, brown sugar, and warm spices like cinnamon or cardamom. The base is almost always rich and warm — often anchored by **benzyl benzoate, ethyl maltol** (a synthetic that smells like cotton candy), or **heliotrope**.

### The Spectrum

Gourmands range widely in character:

**Soft & Cozy** — Light vanilla, skin musks, a little sweetness. Wearable, close to the body. Think *Mugler Alien Goddess* or *YSL Libre*.

**Full Dessert** — Unapologetically edible. Smells like you bathed in crème brûlée. Think *Mugler Angel* (the OG gourmand, 1992 — chocolate, caramel, patchouli) or *Kayali Vanilla 28*.

**Dark Gourmand** — Sweet paired with smoky, leather, incense, or boozy notes. Less "dessert," more "velvet lounge." Think *Kilian Black Phantom* (rum, caramel, dark wood) or *Initio Oud for Greatness*.

**Fruity Gourmand** — Berry, peach, or cherry layered over a sweet base. Often younger, more playful. Common in celebrity and mainstream drugstore releases.

**Coffee/Tobacco Gourmand** — Roasted, bitter edges cut the sweetness. More sophisticated and gender-neutral.

### What Shapes Preference

- **Sweetness tolerance** — Some people find heavy gourmands cloying or headache-inducing; others live for that sugar wall.
- **Projection vs. skin scent** — Do you want a sillage that enters the room, or something intimate only detected up close?
- **Season/context** — Gourmands tend to read as fall/winter. Heavy ones in summer heat can become overwhelming.
- **Skin chemistry** — Sweet notes amplify or flatten depending on your skin's pH and natural oils. The same vanilla-heavy scent can smell clean on one person, cloying on another.
- **Patchouli tolerance** — Classic gourmands (especially Angel-lineage) use dark patchouli to ground the sweetness. People either love it or hate it.
