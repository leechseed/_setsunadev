---

## 🤖 Claude  <sub>2026-03-21 18:24:39</sub>

The Jewish equivalent of halal is **kosher** (or *kashrut* — the broader set of dietary laws it comes from).

Both systems govern what foods are permissible and how they must be prepared, but they differ in specifics. A few key kosher rules:

- No pork or shellfish
- Animals must be slaughtered in a specific humane method (*shechita*)
- Meat and dairy cannot be mixed or eaten together
- Certain animal species are outright forbidden

Interestingly, Muslims can generally eat kosher meat in the absence of halal, since the standards overlap significantly — though it doesn't work as cleanly in reverse for Jews eating halal.
