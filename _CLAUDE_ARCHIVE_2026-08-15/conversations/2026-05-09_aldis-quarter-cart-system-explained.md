# Aldi's quarter cart system explained

> **Conversation Overview**

The person asked about the Aldi shopping cart deposit system, specifically why a quarter is required to use a cart. Claude explained that the quarter-deposit mechanism locks carts together in the corral, and customers retrieve their quarter by returning and re-chaining the cart. Claude also explained the business rationale: the system incentivizes customers to return carts themselves, eliminating the need for dedicated cart-retrieval staff, which contributes to Aldi's overall cost savings and lower prices. Claude offered practical tips, including keeping a quarter accessible, the availability of a reusable keychain cart token sold by Aldi, and the common practice of customers handing off carts in the parking lot to bypass the deposit entirely.

## 🤖 Claude  <sub>2026-05-09 17:55:12</sub>

You insert a quarter to unlock a cart from the corral, and you get the quarter back when you return the cart and re-chain it to another one.

The point: it eliminates the need to pay employees to round up stray carts. Customers self-police because nobody wants to abandon 25 cents. That labor savings is part of how Aldi keeps prices low.

Tips:
- Keep a quarter in your car or wallet
- Aldi sells a keychain "cart token" (same size as a quarter) if you don't want to mess with change
- Some people will hand you their cart on the way out so you skip the deposit entirely
