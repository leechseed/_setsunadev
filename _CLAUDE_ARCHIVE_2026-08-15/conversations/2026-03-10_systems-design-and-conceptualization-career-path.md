## 🤖 Claude  <sub>2026-03-10 05:12:42</sub>

The field you're describing sits at the intersection of a few disciplines, but the closest overarching label is **Systems Design** or more specifically **Systems Architecture** — and the practitioner is often called a **Systems Architect** or **Solutions Architect**.

But let me sharpen the edges a bit, because what you're describing has a specific flavor.

---

**The core of what you're describing:**

You're not just designing a system — you're designing a *system plus its own documentation and validation framework*, across three abstraction layers (conceptual overview, guided implementation, empirical case study). That's actually closer to what's called **Knowledge Engineering** or **Methodology Design** — the craft of taking a domain of knowledge and packaging it into a *reproducible, teachable, testable system*.

The deliverables you're describing map cleanly onto something called a **Framework** (capital F, in the professional/academic sense):
- The 101 layer = **Conceptual Model / Reference Architecture**
- The For Dummies layer = **Implementation Playbook or Methodology Guide**
- The Case Study layer = **Validation / Proof of Concept**

---

**Adjacent titles/roles worth exploring:**

- **Business Analyst / Systems Analyst** — maps processes and requirements into documented systems
- **Enterprise Architect** — designs how systems, processes, and information fit together at an organizational level
- **Instructional Designer** — specifically focuses on structuring knowledge into layered, learnable formats (your three-layer model is very close to instructional design scaffolding)
- **Methodology Consultant** — people who literally sell packaged frameworks (think McKinsey's frameworks, or the Scaled Agile Framework)
- **Technical Writer / Documentation Engineer** — when the *output* is the deliverable

---

**The honest answer for what you're building:**

What you're describing — taking source material (literature, IP, a concept), abstracting it into a system, then producing a reference doc + implementation guide + case study — is essentially **Framework Development**. The closest professional home for that skill is somewhere between **Systems Thinking**, **Knowledge Architecture**, and **Curriculum/Instructional Design**.

Given your context with Bold Venture X, you might find the term **IP Systematization** or **World System Design** resonates — it's the process of turning creative or conceptual IP into a rigorous, operable, documentable system.

The edge curriculum is ready. A few things worth calling out directly:

**The single highest-leverage item on this entire list** is *Gödel, Escher, Bach*. It's long and it's strange and it will take real commitment — but it is the only book that operates simultaneously at the level of formal systems, creative IP, self-referential AI, and emergent complexity. **The one most people skip and most regret** is the statistics and research methodology stack — Booth's *Craft of Research*, Freedman's *Statistics*, Pearl's *Book of Why*. Without these, your case study layer will always be narrative rather than evidence. That distinction matters enormously when you're trying to prove a system works to anyone with real stakes in the question.
