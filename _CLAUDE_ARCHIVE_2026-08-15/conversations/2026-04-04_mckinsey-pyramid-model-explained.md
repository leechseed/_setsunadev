The McKinsey Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto's pyramid) - quick answer.The **Minto Pyramid Principle** (developed by Barbara Minto at McKinsey) is a communication framework for structuring arguments top-down:

**Core idea:** Lead with the answer, then support it.

**The structure:**
1. **Governing Thought** — Your single main conclusion/recommendation up front
2. **Key Arguments** — 3 supporting points (ideally) that prove the governing thought
3. **Supporting Data** — Evidence, facts, analysis underneath each argument

**Two rules that hold it together:**
- **MECE** — Each level must be Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive (no overlap, no gaps)
- **Vertical logic** — Each point must directly prove the one above it
- **Horizontal logic** — Points at the same level must be the same "type" of argument

**Why it matters:**
Executives and busy readers don't want to sit through your reasoning before getting your point. The pyramid forces you to synthesize first, justify second — which is the opposite of how most people naturally write (build-up → conclusion).

It's basically the structural backbone behind most McKinsey decks, memos, and slide logic. If you've ever seen a consulting deck where every slide title is a complete sentence stating a conclusion (not just a topic), that's the pyramid in action.
