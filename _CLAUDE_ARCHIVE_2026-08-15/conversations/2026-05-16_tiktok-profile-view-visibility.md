## 🤖 Claude  <sub>2026-05-16 09:33:46</sub>

No — TikTok's profile view history doesn't work that way. Here's the actual situation as of when I last had solid info, but let me give you the nuance because it's a feature people misunderstand a lot.

TikTok has a "Profile views" feature where someone can see who visited their profile in the **last 30 days**. But it only works if **both people have the feature turned on**. If you have it switched off, your visits don't get logged for anyone — and you also can't see who viewed yours. So it's reciprocal/opt-in.

A few things worth knowing:

It doesn't track *every* check individually. If you visit someone's profile five times in a day, you generally show up once in their list, not five times. It's "who visited" not a tally of every tap.

It only covers the rolling 30-day window. Anything older drops off.

It's separate from things like video views and story views, which have their own visibility rules.

If you want to browse profiles without showing up at all, you turn off Profile views in your settings (Settings and privacy → Privacy → Profile views). The trade-off is you then lose the ability to see your own visitors too.

## 🤖 Claude  <sub>2026-05-16 09:34:38</sub>

It's actually a real tension, and you're probably right that a lot of people keep it off — but the reasons to keep it *on* are mostly about wanting the information flowing the other direction.

The main draw is curiosity about who's looking at *you*. If you're someone building a following, dating, or just nosy about whether a particular person keeps checking your profile, the feature is the only way to get that. And since it's reciprocal, the "price" of seeing your visitors is letting others see you. Some people happily pay that price because the intel matters more to them than the exposure.

There's also a creator/networking angle. If you're trying to grow, knowing who's repeatedly visiting can tell you who to engage with — a potential collaborator, a superfan, someone worth following back. For people treating TikTok as a business tool rather than passive scrolling, that's genuinely useful.

And honestly, a chunk of people just never touch the setting at all. It's on by default in a lot of cases, and most users don't go digging through privacy menus. So "kept it on" often really means "never thought about it."

Your instinct is sound though — the people who *deliberately* think about it tend to land on "off," because the asymmetry feels bad: you mostly notice the feature when you get caught visiting someone, not when it benefits you. The benefit is abstract; the embarrassment is concrete. That's a classic setup for people to opt out once they're aware it exists.
