Here's a breakdown of any potential faux pas to consider:

**Mild considerations, not dealbreakers:**

- **Name tape in the back** — In military use, name tapes go on the front, but you're wearing it backwards (bill to the back), which is why the name tape ends up visible. This is a non-issue in a civilian kitchen context. Nobody's going to dock you points for it.

- **Wearing it backwards** — Some older or more traditional chefs/kitchen managers might side-eye a backwards cap in a professional kitchen, but in most modern kitchens, especially casual or back-of-house environments, it's completely normal and practical (keeps the bill out of your way while you work).

- **Military surplus gear in civilian contexts** — Completely acceptable. Lots of people repurpose military gear for work because it's built tough. No faux pas there at all.

**Actual things to watch for in a kitchen specifically:**

- **Hair containment** — Depending on your jurisdiction and the kitchen's health code standards, a patrol cap may or may not count as adequate hair coverage. If you have longer hair, you may still need a hair net underneath. A patrol cap covers the top but not always the sides or back.

- **Hygiene perception** — If it's visibly worn/stained from non-kitchen use, that could raise eyebrows from a health inspector or manager. Keeping it clean is the main thing.

**Bottom line:** No real faux pas. It's functional, it fits your head, and in a kitchen nobody cares about the aesthetics of your cap as long as your hair is contained and it stays clean. Wear it comfortably.
