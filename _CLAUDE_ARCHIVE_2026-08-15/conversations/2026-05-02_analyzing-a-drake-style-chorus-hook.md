**What's hitting:**
The "she ain't into you" anchor is strong. It's mean, conversational, repeatable — exactly the Drake-petty energy you're going for (think the smug observational mode, not the in-his-feelings mode). The repetition is doing the work a hook needs to do.

"when she with her boo, not you" is the best line in the whole thing. The "boo / not you" near-rhyme with "not" wedged between is sneaky clever, and you're naming a third party which makes it cut deeper. That's your money line.

**What's not hitting:**
"what she got to do, nothing" is the weak link. The setup "what she got to do" is promising something punchy and "nothing" doesn't pay it off — it deflates instead of escalating. It's also too abstract sitting next to line 4, which has a specific scene (her with someone else). 

Structurally your A/B pattern is:
- A: she ain't into you
- B: [observation]
- A: she ain't into you  
- B: [escalation/twist]

You want B1 to set up B2's reveal. Right now B1 is just shrugging.

**Some directions to try for line 2:**
- Mirror the structure of line 4: "where she wanna be? not you" / "who she thinkin' bout? not you"
- Drop a specific image — a location, a time, a phone going off
- Make it a question that the next A-line answers

Also consider: those last two repeats of the hook line at the end are safe. Could vary one — drop the vocal, let the funk pocket breathe, then come back in. Or layer a "yeah" or a whispered ad-lib on the second one. Drake choruses always have that one little texture moment.

Last thing — Drake hooks usually have one concrete sensory detail (a city, a text message, a time of night). This is all abstract. Even one tiny image would ground it and make it feel like a scene instead of a statement.

🏆 **My pick: #2 — "phone lit up at 2, not you"**

Reasons:
- It's the most Hotline Bling. That song lives on the late-night phone imagery. You'd be tipping the hat without copying.
- "2" and "you" rhyme, which strengthens the whole hook's sonic pattern (you, do, boo, you, two, you — the whole thing rides one vowel sound).
- It tells a tiny story: it's late, her phone is going off, it ain't him. That's a scene.
- Summer single needs that one detail people quote back. "Phone lit up at 2" is quotable.

Runner-up is #3 for the "mm" — if you want maximum nonchalant and you're confident you can sell that vocal moment, that's the move. The Drake hum is criminally underused in imitations.

One more thought: whichever you pick, try recording the second "she ain't into you" at the end half-spoken, almost throwaway. Hotline Bling does this — the repetition isn't always sung at full energy, sometimes it's just muttered. That's the nonchalance.
