## CORE THESIS (1 sentence)
Study techniques are useless if your body is broken, your beliefs are wrong, and your self-regulation is absent — fix the foundation before optimizing the methods.

---

## INVARIANTS
*These are the unchanging principles — true regardless of subject, age, or context.*

1. **Sleep consolidates memory.** Cutting sleep to study more destroys the process that locks in what you learned. 7-9 hours, consistent schedule. Non-negotiable.

2. **Recognition ≠ Recall.** If you can recognize it when you see it but can't produce it from memory, you don't know it. Most students confuse familiarity with mastery.

3. **Effort = encoding strength.** The harder your brain works to retrieve or process information, the stronger the memory trace. Easy study = weak learning.

4. **Spacing beats massing.** Distributed practice across days/weeks always outperforms cramming the same total hours into one block. This is one of the most replicated findings in cognitive science.

5. **Testing IS learning.** Retrieval practice isn't assessment — it's the learning event itself. Pulling information from memory strengthens it more than any amount of re-exposure.

6. **Beliefs constrain performance.** If you believe intelligence is fixed, you avoid challenge, interpret struggle as failure, and plateau. Growth mindset isn't motivational fluff — it changes behavior under difficulty.

---

## HEURISTICS
*Rules of thumb for decision-making while studying.*

| Situation | Do This | Not This |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Finished reading a section | Close book, write what you remember | Reread it |
| Planning a study session | Mix 2-3 subjects in blocks | Grind one subject for hours |
| Material feels easy/familiar | Test yourself — that "ease" is probably an illusion | Move on assuming you know it |
| You're exhausted | Sleep, study tomorrow | Push through with caffeine |
| Struggling with material | Reframe: "I'm building new pathways" | Conclude you're not smart enough |
| Reviewing for an exam | Space reviews: 1 day → 3 days → 1 week → pre-exam | Cram the night before |
| Taking notes | Write questions and key concepts, not transcriptions | Highlight or copy text |
| After every study session | Ask: "What can I recall? Where are my gaps?" | Just close the book and leave |

## CORE CONCEPTS (6)

**1. Holistic Learning Framework**
Learning is a stack: Health → Beliefs → Motivation → Metacognition → Techniques. Each layer depends on the one below it. Don't optimize Layer 5 while Layer 1 is broken.

**2. Learning Myths**
False beliefs create invisible ceilings. The big ones: learning styles are real (they're not), rereading works (it doesn't), more hours = more learning (quality > quantity), multitasking is efficient (it's task-switching with a cost).

**3. Metacognitive Cycle**
Plan → Monitor → Evaluate → Adjust. Before studying: what am I learning and how? During: am I actually retaining this? After: did my approach work? Next time: what changes?

**4. Spaced Repetition**
Review after class → next day → 3-5 days later → 2 weeks later → before exam. Each review at the point where you're about to forget maximizes retention per minute spent.

**5. Retrieval Practice**
Self-quiz, flashcards, write from memory, teach someone, do practice problems. The act of pulling from memory — not re-exposure to material — is what builds durable knowledge.

**6. Interleaving**
Mix problem types and subjects within a session instead of blocking one type. Feels slower and harder. Produces significantly better transfer and discrimination ability.

## WHAT DOESN'T WORK (kill list)
Rereading · Highlighting · Passive note-copying · Cramming · Studying while sleep-deprived · Marathon single-subject sessions · Multitasking · Relying on "learning styles"
