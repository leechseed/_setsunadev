## 1. The Field Map

The craft you're describing doesn't live in a single department. It draws from four parent disciplines:

| Discipline | What It Contributes |
|---|---|
| **Systems Thinking** | The cognitive grammar — how to see feedback loops, leverage points, emergent behavior |
| **Enterprise / Solution Architecture** | The professional practice of designing complex systems at scale |
| **Knowledge Engineering** | How to structure, formalize, and make knowledge reproducible |
| **Instructional Design** | How to layer knowledge into 101 → guided → validated formats |

Your three-layer deliverable model (Overview / For Dummies / Case Study) is essentially an **instructional systems design** output applied to a **systems architecture** artifact. The people who do this professionally are often called:

- **Methodology Designers**
- **Enterprise Architects**
- **Technical Program Architects**
- **Knowledge Architects**
- **Curriculum Architects** (in academic or training contexts)

## 2. Tier 1 — Foundational Literacy

*Start here. These are the books that give you the mental models everything else plugs into.*

---

### **Thinking in Systems** — Donella H. Meadows
> The single best entry point to systems thinking as a discipline. Meadows teaches you to see the world in stocks, flows, feedback loops, and delays. This is the grammar of the entire field.

- **Why it matters:** Before you can design a system, you must *think* in systems. This rewires how you read anything.
- **Deliverable it builds:** Conceptual Model / 101 Layer

---

### **The Fifth Discipline** — Peter Senge
> Senge operationalizes systems thinking inside organizations. Introduces the concept of the "learning organization" and makes abstract systems theory applied and human.

- **Why it matters:** Bridges pure theory into organizational practice — relevant if your systems will be *implemented by people*, not just machines.

---

### **An Introduction to General Systems Thinking** — Gerald Weinberg
> More rigorous and technical than Meadows. Weinberg brings a software/engineering lens to systems theory. Dense but foundational.

- **Why it matters:** Teaches you to think about *complexity* and *simplification tradeoffs* — essential when you're abstracting a concept into a system model.

---

### **The Design of Everyday Things** — Don Norman
> Not about systems in the technical sense — but about the design of models that humans can *understand and use*. Introduces affordances, mental models, and feedback.

- **Why it matters:** Your "For Dummies" layer lives here. Norman teaches you how systems fail when they don't match the user's mental model.

## 3. Tier 2 — Systems Thinking & Architecture

*Once you have the mental models, these build the professional vocabulary and toolset.*

---

### **Software Systems Architecture** — Rozanski & Woods
> The canonical professional text on architecture as a *practice* — not just diagrams, but viewpoints, perspectives, and stakeholder-driven documentation.

- **Why it matters:** Introduces the idea of **architectural viewpoints** — essentially what you're calling your three layers. Different stakeholders need different views of the same system.

---

### **Designing Data-Intensive Applications** — Martin Kleppmann
> Despite the title, this is fundamentally a book about designing complex systems for reliability, scalability, and correctness. The conceptual chapters are gold.

- **Why it matters:** Teaches you to reason about *tradeoffs* in system design — a core skill for anyone architecting anything at scale.

---

### **Enterprise Architecture as Strategy** — Ross, Weill & Robertson
> The MIT Sloan book on how organizations design their operating models. Introduces the concept of the **Foundation for Execution** — the core systems a business runs on.

- **Why it matters:** If your systems are meant to operate inside organizations or business contexts, this is the professional architecture language you need.

---

### **The TOGAF Standard** — The Open Group *(reference, not casual reading)*
> The industry-standard enterprise architecture framework used by large organizations globally.

- **Why it matters:** Not a page-turner, but a reference. Understanding TOGAF teaches you what a *mature, professional framework* looks like — its phases, deliverables, governance structure.
- **Read:** The overview and ADM (Architecture Development Method) sections first.

## 4. Tier 3 — Knowledge Architecture & Framework Design

*This tier is specifically about packaging knowledge into frameworks — the most direct ancestor of what you're building.*

---

### **The Checklist Manifesto** — Atul Gawande
> A surgeon examines how simple structured protocols dramatically reduce failure in complex, high-stakes domains.

- **Why it matters:** This is about operationalizing tacit expert knowledge into a reproducible system — exactly what your "For Dummies" layer does. The entire book is a case study in knowledge architecture.

---

### **Structured Analytic Techniques for Intelligence Analysis** — Heuer & Pherson
> The CIA's methodology framework for structured thinking. A masterclass in taking cognitive processes and formalizing them into repeatable, documentable techniques.

- **Why it matters:** Direct model for your work. Each technique in this book has: a description, a step-by-step procedure, and a validation method. That is your three-layer model exactly.

---

### **Conceptual Blockbusting** — James L. Adams
> A Stanford classic on structured creativity and conceptual design. Teaches you to move from vague concept to explicit, communicable model.

- **Why it matters:** The front end of your process — how you go from source material (a piece of literature, an IP) to a formalizable concept.

---

### **The McKinsey Way** — Ethan Rasiel
> How McKinsey designs and presents frameworks as consulting deliverables. The MECE principle (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive) is introduced here.

- **Why it matters:** MECE is the core test for a well-designed framework. Every layer of your deliverable should pass this test.

---

### **Systemantics (The Systems Bible)** — John Gall
> A sardonic but brilliant examination of how systems actually behave vs. how they're designed to behave.

- **Why it matters:** Essential humility for any systems designer. Teaches you where systems fail — which directly informs your Case Study / Validation layer.

## 5. Tier 4 — Methodology & Documentation Craft

*How to produce the actual deliverables — the 101, the guided playbook, the case study.*

---

### **Every Page Is Page One** — Mark Baker
> A radical rethink of technical documentation. Baker argues that in an information-dense world, every document must be self-contained, topic-based, and actionable.

- **Why it matters:** Directly applicable to your 101 layer. How do you write a reference document that works for someone who lands anywhere inside it?

---

### **Docs for Developers** — Bhatti, Corleissen, Lambourne, Nunez, Waterhouse
> A practical guide to producing technical documentation that is actually used. Covers reference docs, tutorials, how-to guides, and explanations — which map almost exactly to your three layers.

- **Why it matters:** The **Diátaxis framework** introduced in adjacent work (by Daniele Procida) gives you the exact four-quadrant model your three layers approximate: *Tutorial / How-To / Reference / Explanation.*

---

### **The Dummies Series Style Guide** *(meta-study)*
> Rather than one book, study the actual structure of 3-4 Dummies books in domains you know. Reverse-engineer the formula: chapter structure, sidebars, assumption of reader baseline, step cadence.

- **Why it matters:** You cited this format explicitly. The best way to learn it is to dissect it structurally, not just read it.

---

### **Case Study Research: Design and Methods** — Robert K. Yin
> The definitive academic text on designing rigorous case studies as a research and validation method.

- **Why it matters:** Your third layer is a case study. Yin teaches you how to design one that actually *proves something* rather than just illustrating it.

---

## 6. Tier 5 — Validation, Case Studies & Applied Research

*How to test whether your system actually does what you designed it to do.*

---

### **Thinking, Fast and Slow** — Daniel Kahneman
> How humans actually process information and make decisions — which is what any system you design must ultimately work *within*.

- **Why it matters:** Grounds your validation layer in cognitive reality. A system that ignores how people think will fail in implementation even if it's logically perfect.

---

### **The Lean Startup** — Eric Ries
> The validated learning loop: Build → Measure → Learn. A practical framework for iterative system validation.

- **Why it matters:** Your Case Study layer is essentially a lean experiment. Ries gives you the structure for designing it to produce *actionable signal*, not just narrative.

---

### **Measuring What Matters** — John Doerr *(OKRs)*
> How to define objectives and key results — essentially, how to know if your system is working.

- **Why it matters:** Before you can run a case study, you need a measurement framework. OKRs are one of the cleanest tools for this.

## 9. Key Vocabulary to Internalize

These are the terms that signal fluency in this craft:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Viewpoint** | A perspective on a system tailored to a specific stakeholder's concerns |
| **Abstraction Layer** | A level of detail at which a system is described |
| **MECE** | Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive — the test of a complete framework |
| **Reference Architecture** | A generic, reusable model that can be adapted to specific contexts |
| **Ontology** | A formal definition of concepts and their relationships within a domain |
| **Validated Learning** | Knowledge gained through structured experimentation, not assumption |
| **Affordance** | What a system signals it can do — how it communicates its own use |
| **Leverage Point** | Where a small intervention produces disproportionate system change |
| **ADM** | Architecture Development Method — the phase-based process in TOGAF |
| **Diátaxis** | A four-quadrant documentation framework: Tutorial / How-To / Reference / Explanation |
| **Feedback Loop** | A mechanism by which a system's output affects its own input |
