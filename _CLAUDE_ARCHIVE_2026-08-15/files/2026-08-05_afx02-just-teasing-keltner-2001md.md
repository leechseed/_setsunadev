## CORE THESIS
A tease is an intentional provocation wrapped in playful off-record markers that together comment on something relevant to the target. Whether it lands as affection or as assault is not determined by the content — it is determined by the ratio between how aggressive the provocation is and how many markers are wrapped around it, and that ratio is set by whose *face* is at stake. Power and familiarity both reduce face concern, which means both make teasing more frequent **and more brutal**.

---

## INVARIANTS
*These are the unchanging principles — true regardless of context.*

1. **Two components, both required.** Intentional provocation + playful off-record markers. Strip the markers and you have direct hostility. Strip the provocation and you have a joke, not a tease. Both must be present or it isn't teasing.

2. **The tease must comment on something relevant to the target.** Play directed at someone that doesn't touch anything true about them isn't a tease. Amusing anecdotes, pretend-role play, and general joke-telling all fall outside the category for exactly this reason.

3. **Face governs the whole system.** Goffman's *face* — the self-image a person claims through socially approved attributes — is what a tease threatens and what off-record markers protect. Everything downstream is a function of how much face concern is present in the room.

4. **The hostility ratio is the mechanism.** Aggressive provocation + few markers = hostile. Less aggressive provocation + many markers = affiliative. Demonstrated experimentally: holding provocation hostility constant, romantic-partner teases with fewer markers produced more anger and contempt and less amusement, desire and love.

5. **Low face concern produces more direct and more hostile behavior.** This is the counterintuitive engine. When you care less about someone's social standing — or your own in front of them — you stop hedging. Politeness is a *symptom* of face concern, not of kindness.

6. **High-status people tease more, and tease harder.** Replicated across settings: senior hospital staff joked at junior staff more than the reverse; doctors teased more than midwives, who teased more than nurses; high-status fraternity members used more aggressive provocations and fewer politeness tactics; high-status boys at a basketball camp were more physically aggressive in their taunting. Sociometrically popular children teased more.

7. **Familiarity increases hostility — and the pleasure stays.** The prediction runs against intuition and held up: basketball campers taunted the *same* partners more aggressively on day three than on day one, and reported just as much enjoyment. Closeness doesn't soften the tease; it removes the need to hedge while simultaneously improving the aim.

8. **Two things reliably trigger teasing: norm violations and interpersonal conflict.** Well replicated across observational, narrative, ethnographic and self-report methods. Where you find teasing, look upstream for a broken rule or an unresolved dispute.

9. **Teasing is a developmental achievement, not a childhood behavior.** Generating and reading a tease requires holding multiple contradictory intentions at once. Comprehension of irony and sarcasm doesn't consolidate until roughly ages 11–13. Before that, children default to literal interpretation.

10. **Teasing is not bullying.** Bullies do tease — but with maximal provocation and minimal markers. Kicking, name-calling and object-taking *without* off-record markers are direct hostility, categorically.

11. **Markers work in any modality.** Verbal (exaggeration, metaphor, formula, idiom) and nonverbal (prosody, facial exaggeration, mimicry, the wink) are equally valid. This is why the definition survives across cultures, ages and species-adjacent contexts.

12. **The target's face position shapes how the tease is read.** Rejected and neglected children attributed hostile intent to a teaser more readily than popular and average children did. The same tease is a different event depending on where the target stands.

13. **Off-record communication collapses under urgency.** Teasing is highly unlikely during emergencies, physical trauma, accidents or funerals. Urgent norm violations get direct commentary instead.

## HEURISTICS
*Situational rules — true most of the time, in most contexts.*

| If... | Then... |
|---|---|
| You want a line to read as affectionate | Add markers. Don't soften the content. Cruel content with heavy marking reads warmer than mild content delivered flat. |
| You want the same line to sting | Strip the markers. Keep every word identical. This is the cleanest tonal switch available in dialogue. |
| You need to establish a hierarchy without exposition | Show who teases whom and who cannot tease back. Direction of teasing *is* the org chart. |
| You're writing flirtation | Write it as teasing. Direct declaration of attraction is face-threatening for both parties; indirect provocation with plausible deniability is the actual mechanism. If unreciprocated, the attraction can be denied and both faces survive. |
| A relationship is deepening | Let the teasing get *rougher* while the pleasure holds. That's the empirical signature of increasing familiarity. |
| A relationship is breaking | Let the teasing get rougher while the pleasure drops. Same escalation, opposite meaning — and the audience will feel it before they can name it. |
| A scene has teasing but feels weightless | Add the upstream cause. Teasing follows a norm violation or a conflict. Without one it's just banter. |
| Your characters tease during a genuine crisis | You've made a character statement, whether you meant to or not. Off-record play doesn't survive urgency; anyone still doing it is signalling something specific about their relationship to the danger. |
| You need a character to be read as cruel | Give them low face concern for others. Low agreeableness — coldness, competitiveness, absent kindness — predicted more hostile teasing in both fraternity members and romantic partners. |
| A character is being teased and doesn't respond | That's realistic. Counter is the most common response; ignoring is second among children; nearly half of 8–12-year-olds in one lab study went silent under taunting. |
| You're writing a young character | Under about 11, they read the tease literally and feel it as hostile — 97% of 5–10-year-olds reported negative emotion at being teased, versus 78% of 11–13-year-olds. |
| You want to mark a faction or culture | Vary the marker vocabulary, not the content. Different groups tease about the same things using different off-record dialects. |

## CORE CONCEPTS

### 1. The Definition
> A tease is **an intentional provocation accompanied by playful off-record markers that together comment on something relevant to the target.**

This definition is the entire contribution of the paper. It was built to do four jobs simultaneously: cover every behavior anyone has called teasing, clarify what kind of aggression and play are involved, separate teasing from bullying and from polite reprimand, and explain how the same act can produce affiliation or injury. Prior definitions failed on all four because they were built from single research contexts.

### 2. Provocation — and its three possible referents
The provocation can be verbal (a nickname, a sarcastic comment) or nonverbal (a poke, a physical imitation). It refers to exactly one of three things:

- **(a) Something about the target** — appearance, personality, intellect, social ability, behavior. Usually a deviation from a norm. Pointing, exaggerated mimicry.
- **(b) The relationship between teaser and target** — pinning and taunting signal *power over*; eye covers, arm restraints and provocative touch signal *closeness with*. The pre-index ("I know something you don't know") provokes by claiming asymmetric knowledge.
- **(c) An object of interest to the target** — offer-withdrawal games. A mother presents a desired object and pulls it back when the infant reaches. The tease is about control of the thing.

Note that provocation can also *start positive*: a positive overture becomes provocative at the moment it's retracted or repudiated.

### 3. Off-Record Markers — the full catalog
Off-record communication deliberately violates the norms governing on-record speech (direct, relevant, honest, appropriate), thereby signalling that a non-literal reading is available. The paper's catalog:

**Verbal**
- **Formulaic expressions** — oft-heard, ritualized phrases; "playing the dozens" rhymes; set phrases like "yes, dear"; repetitive rhythmic phrases in family routines.
- **Exaggeration** — exaggerated detail, excessive profanity, implausible claims, absurdly excessive characterizations. *Paradox worth noting: the more exaggerated the insult, the easier it is for the target to receive it as light-hearted.*
- **Idiom and metaphor** — relationship-specific idioms; pejorative and metaphorical nicknames; ambiguous word classes (the Kaluli *bale to* words).
- **Third-person reference** — calling someone present "he" or "she" instead of addressing them, when delivered with exaggerated pitch or emphatic stress.
- **Rhetorical questions.**

**Nonverbal / prosodic**
- Exaggerated facial expressions; mimicry of the target's mannerisms; iconic displays (the wink, one finger rubbed over another, the paradoxically aggressive raised fist and chin).
- Elongated vowels; sing-song voice; emphatic stress; short switching pauses; loud rapid delivery; dramatized sighs; utterances markedly louder or quieter than the ones before.
- Mock-scolding: angry delivery that isn't angry. The Kaluli formally distinguish *Keab* (sounding angry, not angry) from *Enteab* (actual anger) by intonation alone. Young children grasp this early and mock-scold back.

**Redressive actions** (adjacent, not identical): affiliative laughter, friendly physical contact, knowing eye contact, implicit praise embedded in the tease; and negative-politeness moves like apologies, hedges and deferential displays — including the teaser's *own* embarrassment.

### 4. The Face-Threat Model
The paper's central diagram reduces to a causal chain:

**Context face concern → strategy → content → outcome**

| Face concern | Strategy chosen | Provocation | Markers | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Low** (informal setting, high power, high familiarity) | Go on-record — or tease bluntly | More aggressive | Few | **More hostile** |
| **High** (formal occasion, high-status other, distance) | Go off-record — or say nothing at all | Less aggressive | Many | **Less hostile** |

Two variables drive face concern: **power** and **social distance**. Both work the same direction. Less power or more distance → more face concern → more hedging.

### 5. The Three Functions (implicit across the review)
- **Socialization / norm enforcement** — teasing follows norm violations across every culture studied, and teaches the norm without a direct reprimand.
- **Conflict negotiation** — an indirect, playful way to handle a dispute. Siblings escalate teasing as they escalate conflict. High-school girls teased to negotiate conflicts over boys and over intimacies. Department store workers teased hardest when allocating scarce office space.
- **Affiliation, flirtation and identity** — courtship signalling, romantic idiom, group bonding, the display of relationship itself.

### 6. Development Curve
- **~5 years** — recognizes highly salient teasing but biases toward literal interpretation.
- **~6 years** — can identify salient off-record markers; begins to notice sarcasm differs from sincerity.
- **~8–9 years** — discerns intended meaning much better; can distinguish playful from aggressive fighting; picks up on ironic criticism as funny, though not to adult degree.
- **~11–13 years** — the consolidation point. Full grasp of multiple contradictory intentions. Teasing turns symbolic and playful. In one interview study, 45% of first graders reported symbolic teasing versus 80% of eleventh graders. Bullying frequency declines sharply from around age 12.

Children and adults produce irony with the same intonation patterns — heavy stress, slow speech, nasalization — but children pair it with *negative* facial expressions where adults pair it with playful positive ones.

### 7. Individual Differences and the Fool
Low agreeableness predicts more hostile teasing. Frequently-teased children score lower on peer-rated acceptance and higher on rejection — others feel less face threat toward them, so they get teased more *and* harder.

The **Rukka** is the sharpest single finding for character work: in the Faroese community studied, the village fool is teased by everyone for the community's delight — and when a Rukka moves from one fishing boat to another, *he immediately becomes that boat's Rukka*. The role is bound to the position, not to the person.

Cross-culturally, individuals occupy the clown role and tease even at the most solemn occasions.

### 8. Culture
Cultures with heightened face concern tease less, and tease less harshly, when they tease at all. Asian American romantic partners used more off-record markers and were less hostile than European American partners in the same paradigm. Cultures known for directness of communication tease children frequently and from both parents; in many White middle-class American families, mothers rarely tease infants while fathers do — particularly to young sons.

## NOTABLE QUOTES

> face is "an image of self delineated in terms of approved social attributes"
> — Goffman (1967), p. 5, as quoted in the paper

*The foundation the entire model rests on. Face is not pride or ego — it's a claim made in public, in a currency the group has already approved.*

> "the playful nip denotes the bite, but it does not denote what would be denoted by the bite"
> — Bateson (1972), p. 180, quoted by Straehle (1993) in the paper's definitions table

*The cleanest statement of the mechanism in the whole literature: the tease carries the shape of the attack and withholds the attack.*

## WHAT DOESN'T WORK
Defining teasing by specific behaviors (insults, verbalizations, taunting) — the definition then fails to travel across contexts, ages and modalities; defining it as verbal only, which excludes all of infant and nonverbal teasing; equating teasing with bullying, which imports conclusions from bullying research that don't hold; treating aggression alone as the defining feature, since unintended aggression and purely hostile aggression aren't teasing; treating play alone as the defining feature, since role-play, games and anecdotes aren't teasing; assuming familiarity softens teasing; assuming gender differences are large (they exist but are smaller than commonly asserted, and often confound with status); reading the teaser's intent off the target's response; assuming a teaser and a target experience the same event.

## BVX EXTRACTION LAYER
*Proposed template extension — v2.1. Flagged for approval; not yet locked into the spec.*

**Model 1 — The Tease Dial (two knobs).** Every teasing beat gets two settings: *provocation heat* (1–5) and *marker count* (1–5). Ratio determines reception. This makes tonal calibration a knob-turn instead of a vibe. It also means the same written line can be re-tuned across drafts without rewriting content.

**Model 2 — Teasing as free hierarchy exposition.** In an ensemble the size of The Academy, direction-of-teasing renders the entire status map with no dialogue spent on explaining it. Who teases up? Who can't tease back? Who gets teased by everyone? Three teasing beats can establish what three pages of exposition would.

**Model 3 — Marker vocabulary as faction dialect.** Factions don't need different topics; they need different *markers*. One faction teases through metaphor and nicknames. Another through prosody and mimicry. Another through formulaic ritual phrases. Same provocations, unmistakably different rooms. This is a dialogue-differentiation tool that costs nothing in plot.

**Model 4 — The intimacy meter.** Rising hostility + steady pleasure = bond deepening. Rising hostility + falling pleasure = bond failing. A relationship can be tracked across a long arc on a single two-value reading — directly useful for the cohesion problem, because it gives a continuous measurable line where "how are these two doing right now" is usually vibes.

**Model 5 — The marker-strip.** The single cheapest cruelty move in dialogue: a character delivers the same joke they've delivered all season, and this time drops the wink. Nothing else changes. The audience feels the floor move.

**Model 6 — The Rukka slot.** A scapegoat role bound to a *position* rather than a person — whoever occupies it inherits it. Structurally perfect for a class-year or dormitory system: the newest arrival becomes the target automatically, and the previous target is released the moment someone else arrives. It gives an institution a self-perpetuating cruelty with no villain required.

**Model 7 — Teasing as initiation intensity gauge.** Hazing rites are teasing with the marker count deliberately reduced. A rite's brutality can be specified precisely — provocation heat 5, markers 1 — and its "official" framing as play is exactly the alibi the off-record component provides. This is directly load-bearing for a school with an Administration.

**Model 8 — The urgency cutoff.** Teasing dies in emergencies. Which means the *first scene where nobody teases* is a structural marker. If Year 1 is a clean sync-cult and Year 2 is neon rot, the moment teasing stops functioning is a candidate marker for the Hardware Gate.

**Cross-entry bridge to AFX.01.** Panksepp's PLAY circuit is the biological substrate; Keltner's teasing is what PLAY looks like once it has to operate inside a status hierarchy with face at stake. Panksepp says PLAY shuts off under FEAR. Keltner says teasing gets crueler as face concern drops. Stack them and you get a diagnostic: a world where teasing is escalating *and* play is disappearing is a world where the hierarchy has hardened and the safety is gone. Two independent instruments, one reading. Flag for the Quartermaster log — this is the first cross-domain convergence in the AFX series and is the kind of pattern the tripwire is watching for, though one convergence is not two.

## COMPREHENSIVE GLOSSARY
*Alphabetical. Section references in brackets.*

**Bale to words** — A recognizable class of deliberately ambiguous words used by the Kaluli of Papua New Guinea for teasing. Example of idiom functioning as an off-record marker. *[Concept 3]*

**Demeanor** — Goffman's term for behaving according to norms of politeness, modesty and self-control in order to maintain one's own face and others'. *[Invariant 3]*

**Deference** — Goffman's term for expressed appreciation of others; a face-maintaining move. *[Invariant 3]*

**Enteab** — Kaluli speech form conveying genuine anger, distinguished from *Keab* by intonation alone. *[Concept 3]* — Usage: proof that the marker can live entirely in prosody.

**Face** — Goffman: an image of self delineated in terms of socially approved attributes. The thing a tease threatens and markers protect. *[Invariant 3, Notable Quotes]*

**Face work** — Active maintenance of face: avoiding sensitive topics, disregarding face-threatening actions. *[Invariant 3]*

**Formulaic expression** — Ritualized, oft-heard phrasing used as an off-record marker; violates the norm of appropriate communication. *[Concept 3]*

**Hostility ratio** — *(reader's shorthand for the paper's Figure 1 mechanism)* The relationship between provocation aggressiveness and marker count that determines whether a tease reads hostile or affiliative. *[Invariant 4, Concept 4]*

**Keab** — Kaluli speech form in which the speaker sounds angry but is not. *[Concept 3]*

**Negative face** — The desire to act freely, without imposition by others. *[Invariant 3]*

**Negative politeness** — Redressive tactics expressing a desire not to impinge: apologies, hedges, deferential displays, minimization, impersonalization. *[Concept 3]*

**Off-record marker** — A signal that deviates from the norms of direct, relevant, honest, appropriate communication, indicating a non-literal reading is available. The load-bearing half of the definition. *[Concept 3]* — Usage: the wink, the sing-song voice, the absurd exaggeration.

**Offer-withdrawal game** — Teasing form in which a desired object is presented and pulled back on the target's display of interest. The earliest form observed between caregivers and infants. *[Concept 2]*

**On-record communication** — Direct, relevant, honest, literal speech. The baseline that off-record markers violate. *[Concept 3]*

**Playing the dozens** — African American ritualized insult exchange; the paper's paradigm case of formulaic and exaggerated marking. Notably observed only between friends. *[Concept 3, Invariant 7]*

**Positive face** — The positive claims a person makes about the self. *[Invariant 3]*

**Positive politeness** — Redressive tactics expressing approval: praise, expression of common ground, affiliative laughter, friendly contact, knowing eye contact. *[Concept 3]*

**Pre-index** — A linguistic form that provokes the listener into requesting information the speaker is withholding. "I know something you don't know." *[Concept 2]*

**Provocation** — The intentional act, verbal or nonverbal, directed at some attribute, relationship, or object of interest belonging to the target. The other half of the definition. *[Concept 2]*

**Rukka** — The community fool in the Faroese setting studied by Gaffin (1995). Teased by all, to the community's delight; the role transfers to whoever occupies the position. *[Concept 7]* — Usage: the model for a role-bound rather than person-bound scapegoat.

**Social distance** — Degree of unfamiliarity between parties. Greater distance raises face concern, which softens teasing. *[Concept 4]*

**Social power** — Reduced dependence on others; lowers face concern; raises both teasing frequency and hostility. *[Invariant 6, Concept 4]*

**Sounding** — Ritualized insult exchange, often attributing non-normative characteristics to the target as a way of enforcing norms. *[Concept 3]*

**Strategic indirectness** — Goffman/Brown & Levinson: the general practice of achieving a face-threatening aim through indirect means. Politeness, flirtation and teasing are all instances. *[Invariant 3, Heuristics]*

**Symbolic teasing** — Warm's (1997) term for less blatant criticism using language and gesture to convey play; increases sharply with age (45% of first graders → 80% of eleventh graders). *[Concept 6]*
