The whole system rests on one rule: **you shoot ahead of where you publish.** Producing 4 per batch while releasing 3 means the buffer grows ~1/week, giving you cushion for the weeks the day job eats you alive.

## 2. The Operating Model

**Batch + backlog, not film-and-release.** You cook several dishes in one session, edit them as a batch, then drip them out on a fixed schedule. This is mise en place for content — you already think this way on the line.

**Buffer, not vault.** Don't pre-film a frozen year. Hold a rolling 2–4 week buffer so you can still react to trends and to your own comment section. Shoot ahead, keep replenishing.

**Two batch models:**

- **Model A — Weekly batch (recommended to start).** One shoot day/week, 4–5 dishes. Releases 3, banks 1–2. Keeps you reactive to trends, sessions stay manageable around the day job.
- **Model B — Biweekly mega-batch.** One big shoot day every 2 weeks, 7–8 dishes. Fewer shoot days, more kitchen-efficient, but longer grueling sessions and slower trend reaction. Shift to this only once the workflow is dialed (Phase 3+).

## 3. The Repeatable Week

The Model A week, assuming three fixed release slots (e.g. Tue / Thu / Sat, evenings, when food content peaks):

| Block | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan** | Start of week | Pick 4–5 dishes from the pillar rotation + 1 trend-jack off current search data. 30–60 min. |
| **Shop + prep** | Day before / morning of shoot | Source ingredients, prep mise for all dishes. ~1.5 hrs. |
| **Batch shoot** | One dedicated day | Cook + film 4–5 dishes back-to-back. Multi-cam + ASMR audio pass per dish. 4–6 hrs. |
| **Batch edit** | Split across 1–2 evenings | One short ≈ 1 hr to cut. Hook in first 2 sec, tight pacing, clean audio. |
| **Publish** | 3 release days | Upload, keyword caption + on-screen text, post at peak slot, reply to comments. ~30–45 min each. |

Lock your release days and times and never move them — appointment rhythm trains both the algorithm and the audience.

## 4. Content Pillars & Rotation

Four pillars so you're never staring at a blank shoot list, and so the channel reads as a focused lane (which the algorithm rewards — focused beats scattered):

1. **Japanese classics / regional** — authentic, specific, technique on display. Your core lane and your moat.
2. **Trend-jacks** — whatever's spiking in TikTok/Pinterest/Google search, cooked *properly*. Buys you search traffic.
3. **Fermentation / pickles** — your business-synergy pillar (ferments, quick pickles, half-sours). Phases in heavier from Month 7.
4. **Affordable-aspirational** — "you can make this on a tabletop" comfort builds. Matches the Zojirushi/accessible format.

**Default weekly rotation:**

| Slot | Pillar |
|---|---|
| Release 1 | Japanese classic / regional |
| Release 2 | Trend-jack (rides current search) |
| Release 3 | Rotating: fermentation ↔ affordable-aspirational ↔ experimental |

Style note across all pillars: **texture-forward audio.** Crispy/flaky/gooey/crunchy is trending hard — that's your ASMR edge turned into a content thesis. Mic it accordingly.

## 5. The Year in Five Phases

### Phase 0 — Pre-Launch (≈4 weeks before going live)
**Goal: lock the format and bank the buffer. Publish nothing yet.**
- Finalize the rig (Zojirushi + cameras + ASMR audio), run 2–3 test shoots, dial lighting and audio.
- Build your file/naming system + an editing template you reuse every time.
- Batch-shoot until you've banked **9–12 Shorts** before day one.
- Set up channels, handles, and your keyword/caption strategy.
- **Output: 0 published, 9–12 banked.**

### Phase 1 — Launch & Establish (Months 1–3)
**Goal: consistency and format discovery. Resist pivoting fast.**
- Release 3/week on fixed days/times.
- Weekly batch shoot (4–5 dishes) to hold and grow the buffer.
- Run all four pillars; watch what lands. Don't kill anything before Month 3.
- **Output: ~39 Shorts published.**

### Phase 2 — Optimize & Lean In (Months 4–6)
**Goal: double down on winners, cut the dead weight.**
- Skew the rotation toward whatever's performing.
- Launch **one recurring named series**.
- Add a YouTube long-form "process" cut — one every 1–2 weeks, pulled from shoots you're already doing (one shoot, multiple cuts).
- Begin scaling production value (higher-end dishes / burner phase if you're ready).
- **Output: ~39 more (~78 cumulative).**

### Phase 3 — Scale & Synergy (Months 7–9)
**Goal: turn on the business engine.**
- Launch the **fermentation/pickle series** tied to your commercial backend — content *and* product R&D in one shoot.
- Cross-post to Reels if bandwidth allows.
- Start brand-deal / affiliate outreach if your metrics support it.
- Consider shifting to Model B (biweekly mega-batch) now that the workflow's dialed.
- **Output: ~39 more (~117 cumulative).**

### Phase 4 — Consolidate & Monetize (Months 10–12)
**Goal: systematize and switch on revenue.**
- Lock your 2–3 highest-performing formats and fully systematize them.
- Push brand deals, affiliate, and the funnel to your food products (pickles / cart).
- Build a deeper buffer (4+ weeks) to free up time for the monetization work.
- Year-end review + Year 2 plan.
- **Output: ~39 more (~156 total for the year).**

## 7. Buffer Health Rules

- **Never publish below a 2-week bank.**
- 🟢 Green: ≥3 weeks banked — you're healthy.
- 🟡 Yellow: 2 weeks — add a shoot this week.
- 🔴 Red: <1 week — emergency batch, drop everything.
- Keep **3–5 evergreen Shorts** (no trend dependency) as a reserve you only burn in a Red week.

- Is the buffer healthy or are you running hot?
- Audio/visual quality drifting? Re-baseline.
- Monetization readiness: brand interest, affiliate fit, product funnel performance.

## 10. Reality Note on Year 1

This schedule builds an *asset*, not an instant paycheck. New food channels usually take 6–18 months to reach meaningful revenue, and ad money on Shorts is pennies regardless. So in Year 1, judge this less as income replacement and more as two things: (1) an audience you own, and (2) a marketing funnel feeding your real-world food products. That funnel — pickles, the cart, whatever's next — is where the near-term dollars actually live, and it's the reason the fermentation pillar earns its weight. Treat the channel as the engine; treat the food businesses as what the engine drives.
