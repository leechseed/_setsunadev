## 01 — Identity & Positioning
**Status:** `[IN PROGRESS]` — Stage 1 (taste-by-negation) live

**Frame.** Before the business has a shape, the operator has to have a lens. Positioning is not a tagline — it's the angle of attack on reality that everything else hangs off of. This is the most upstream section in the whole doc and the work that most founders skip, which is why most service businesses look interchangeable.

**Questions.**
- What bores you in the existing space, in fine-grained detail? (Stage 1 work)
- What aesthetics, performances, narrative structures, and cultural postures genuinely move you?
- Three reference points (creators, works, eras) that share a through-line — what is the through-line?
- What's the gap in the space you can feel but haven't articulated yet?
- What kind of practitioner are you actually becoming — art director, cultural strategist, curator, auteur, hybrid?
- What is your unfair advantage *in this specific niche* (creator-self, taste, network, theory chops, work ethic)?
- What would you be embarrassed to be associated with, even if it paid?
- One-sentence working positioning statement (revisited every 30 days for Year 1)

## 03 — ICP / Customer
**Status:** `[OPEN]`

**Frame.** "Adult content creators" is too broad to build on. The sharper the ICP, the higher the price, the shorter the sales cycle, the better the work. Define one primary, one secondary, and a clear no-fly list.

**Questions.**
- Primary ICP: revenue band ($1K/mo, $10K/mo, $50K/mo, $200K+/mo creator)?
- Vertical: alt, cosplay, amateur, pro-aesthetic, couples, kink-specific, femboy, fetish-specific, mainstream-thirst, lewd-but-not-explicit?
- Career stage: launching, scaling, plateaued, rebranding, transitioning off platform?
- Psychographics: what do they care about beyond money? (Aesthetic? Status? Career arc? Anonymity? Art?)
- Who do you *refuse* to work with and why? (Reputational firewall.)
- Where do they currently spend their service budget, and what are they unhappy about?
- Adjacent buyers: managers, agencies, studios, brands? Or strictly direct-to-creator?
- Geographic concentration: US, US+EU, English-speaking global, specific cities (LA, Miami, Atlanta, London, Berlin, Tokyo)?

## 06 — Market & Competition
**Status:** `[OPEN]`

**Frame.** You don't need to win the whole market. You need to know where the white space is, who's already there, and what they're vulnerable to. This is recon, not strategy — strategy comes after.

**Questions.**
- Top 10 service providers in adult creator services right now — names, positioning, pricing?
- Top 5 in adjacent niches you might steal from (mainstream creator agencies, alt fashion brand consultancies, music industry creative directors)?
- Where is each one weak — pricing? taste? speed? communication? cultural fluency?
- What's the white space — the position no one is currently occupying?
- What are the substitutes? (DIY + AI tools, in-house hires, friend-of-friend networks, content agencies.)
- Threats: AI commoditization, platform policy shifts, payment processor crackdowns, market saturation?
- What does the next 18 months of the market look like — who's rising, who's getting stale?
