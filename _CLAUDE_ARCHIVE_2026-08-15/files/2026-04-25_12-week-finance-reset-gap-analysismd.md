## Critical Gaps in the Plan

### 1. Whole Topics Missing

Yang skips or barely touches **eight** core areas:

- **Tax-advantaged accounts.** Week 6 says "brokerage" and namechecks ETFs but *skips Roth IRA, Traditional IRA, 401(k) match, and HSA entirely*. This is the single biggest miss. A taxable brokerage before a Roth IRA is wrong for almost everyone in your situation.
- **Insurance audit.** Health, renters, auto, disability, life. The video has an auto insurance affiliate link in the description and that's it. Insurance is where most people quietly bleed money.
- **Tax planning.** No W-4 review, no withholding check, no mention of how to track deductions if you have any 1099 income. As someone with both W-2 (kitchen) and entrepreneurial (BVX) potential, this is not optional.
- **Credit score / credit report.** Week 9 talks about *using* credit cards but never tells you to pull your credit report or monitor your score.
- **Identity theft protection / credit freezes.** Free at all three bureaus. Yang doesn't mention it.
- **Sinking funds.** The single most useful budgeting concept ever invented and he doesn't say the word once. (See [Sheet 4](#sheet-4--sinking-funds).)
- **Beneficiary designations.** Five-minute task on every bank/brokerage/retirement account. Skipped.
- **Variable income smoothing.** Critical for kitchen work where shifts and tips fluctuate.

### 2. Sequencing Problems

Yang's order has a few real issues:

- **W5 before W4.** Building a $1K emergency fund *before* understanding consumer debt is fine. But he doesn't tell you to *pause* aggressive debt payoff while building the fund. You should: minimum payments on debt → $1K saved → then attack debt.
- **W6 before retirement match.** If your job offers a 401(k) match, capturing it comes *before* a taxable brokerage. Free money beats S&P 500 returns every time.
- **W9 (credit cards) at week 9.** This belongs at week 2 or 3. If you're carrying balances, your "expense cutting" is moot until APR is addressed.
- **W12 stretch goals at the end.** Stretch goals belong in week 1 — they're the *reason* for the other 11 weeks. Move them to week 1 as direction-setting, then refine in week 12.

### 3. Definitions Yang Hand-Waves

- **"Categorize expenses."** Three buckets (fixed / discretionary / debt) is too coarse. You need 8–12 categories to spot real leaks.
- **"Pay yourself first 10%."** 10% of gross? Net? Take-home? He doesn't say. **Use 10–20% of net pay.**
- **"Cut 10–30%."** Of what baseline? Of total? Of each line? **Per line — and only on lines that yield ≥$10/mo.** Below that, it's friction for nothing.
- **"Open a brokerage."** Which type? Taxable? Roth IRA? He just says "brokerage." (See [Brokerage / Retirement](#brokerage--retirement) below.)

## Tools & Programs the Video Doesn't Name

### Budgeting / Transaction Aggregation

You need *something* that pulls all your transactions in one place. Options ranked by what fits your style:

- **Monarch Money** (~$15/mo or $100/yr) — best Mint replacement; clean UI, manual rules, net worth dashboard, goal tracking. Closest to "I want to set this up once and barely touch it."
- **Copilot** ($13/mo, ~$95/yr; iOS/Mac only currently) — slick, very good auto-categorization.
- **YNAB** ($15/mo, $109/yr) — best for **active budgeters** who want envelope-style zero-based. High learning curve. Probably overkill if you just want visibility.
- **Rocket Money** (free + $4–12/mo premium) — bill negotiation built in. Cheaper but less robust as a primary tool.
- **PocketGuard** (free + premium tier) — simpler "what can I spend" view.
- **Free fallback:** Google Sheets + manual CSV imports from each bank monthly. Tedious but $0 and full control.

**My pick for your style:** Monarch as the primary aggregator + a Sheets workbook for net worth and goals (you'll want it in markdown-friendly format anyway).

### High-Yield Savings (HYSA)

Yang says "competitive HYSA" without naming any. Top options:

- **Wealthfront Cash** — currently among the highest APYs, FDIC-insured up to $8M via partner banks, instant transfers, no fees.
- **Ally Bank** — long-time favorite; slightly lower APY but excellent UX and "buckets" feature for sinking funds (huge for week 5+).
- **SoFi** — high APY *if* you set up direct deposit; otherwise the rate drops.
- **Marcus by Goldman Sachs** — solid, no-frills.
- **CIT Bank Platinum Savings** — competitive rates, requires $5K+ balance for top tier.

Always check current APYs — they shift. Don't pick one based on a YouTube video from last year.

### Brokerage / Retirement

This is the section Yang completely fumbles. **Order of operations** (the actual order, not Yang's):

1. **401(k) up to employer match** — free money. Even 3% match doubles your return on day one.
2. **Pay off any APR ≥7%** (credit cards, often personal loans).
3. **Roth IRA** — $7,000/yr limit (2025–2026). Tax-free growth. Use [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com), [Schwab](https://www.schwab.com), or [Vanguard](https://investor.vanguard.com).
4. **HSA** *if* you're on a high-deductible health plan. Triple tax-advantaged. Often skipped because nobody explains it.
5. **Back to 401(k)** to max out (annual limit ~$23,500 in 2025).
6. **Taxable brokerage** — what Yang actually meant in week 6.

For ETF picks: **VTI** (total US market) or **VOO** (S&P 500) are the standard picks. **VT** if you want global. Don't overthink it.

### Debt Tools

- **Undebt.it** — free debt-payoff planner; supports avalanche, snowball, custom strategies.
- **Unbury.us** — dead simple debt payoff visualizer.
- **Debt Destroyer Calculator** — Yang mentions it; it's the FINRED government tool.
- **For credit card APR negotiation:** call your issuer. Script: *"I've been a customer for X years and I'm considering moving my balance to a competitor offering a lower APR. Is there anything you can do on my rate?"* Hits ~50% of the time.

**Avalanche vs. Snowball:**
- *Avalanche* = pay highest APR first. Mathematically optimal.
- *Snowball* = pay smallest balance first. Psychologically optimal — quick wins keep you going.
- Pick the one you'll actually stick to. Avalanche is "right." Snowball is "real."

### Credit Monitoring

- **Credit Karma** (free) — VantageScore from TransUnion + Equifax. Good enough.
- **Experian app** (free tier) — gives you actual FICO 8.
- **Your credit card issuer dashboard** — most show your FICO free now (Discover, Chase, Capital One, etc.).
- **AnnualCreditReport.com** — your three official reports, free weekly. Check for errors and fraud.
- **Credit freezes** — free at Equifax, Experian, TransUnion. Set them up once, lift temporarily when applying for credit.

### Bill Negotiation & Subscription Audit

- **Rocket Money** — auto-finds and cancels subs; negotiates bills (takes a cut of savings).
- **Trim** — similar; less aggressive.
- **DIY method:** export 3 months of card/bank statements → ctrl-F for "monthly," "subscription," any service names → cancel ruthlessly.

### Tax & Document Management

- **FreeTaxUSA** ($0 federal, $15 state) — best price-to-quality for simple-to-moderate returns.
- **Cash App Taxes** — totally free including state.
- **TurboTax / H&R Block** — fine but you're paying for marketing.
- **For 1099 / self-employment (BVX income when it starts):** consider **QuickBooks Self-Employed** or **Wave** (free) for tracking. Or just a dedicated checking account + Google Sheet — that works great until you cross ~$10K/yr in business activity.
- **Password manager:** Bitwarden (free) or 1Password ($3/mo). Non-negotiable for financial accounts.

## Spreadsheet / Excel Setup

Yang gives you a free "Net Worth Tracker" link in the description and that's it. You need a real workbook. Here's the architecture.

### The Five Sheets You Actually Need

1. **Cashflow Tracker** — monthly income, expenses by category, "profit"
2. **Net Worth Tracker** — assets vs. liabilities, snapshot per month
3. **Debt Payoff Schedule** — every debt, APR, min payment, payoff order
4. **Sinking Funds** — known irregular expenses pre-funded monthly
5. **Goals & Stretch Plan** — 1/5/10-year targets with monthly contribution

You can build all five in one Google Sheets workbook with separate tabs. Or use Monarch for #1–2 and a sheet for #3–5.

### Sheet 1 — Cashflow Tracker

Columns:
```
Date | Description | Category | Subcategory | Amount | Account | Fixed/Variable | Notes
```

Categories (use these, not Yang's three):
1. **Housing** (rent, utilities, internet, renters insurance)
2. **Transportation** (gas, insurance, maintenance, registration)
3. **Food — Groceries**
4. **Food — Dining/Delivery** (separate so you can see the leak)
5. **Health** (insurance premium, copays, meds, gym)
6. **Debt Payments** (minimum + extra)
7. **Subscriptions/Software**
8. **Entertainment/Hobbies**
9. **Personal Care**
10. **Gifts/Misc**
11. **Business — BVX** (kept separate from personal)
12. **Income** (W-2 net, tips, side income)

Then a summary section: total income, total expenses, profit, savings rate (%).

### Sheet 2 — Net Worth Tracker

```
Date | Asset/Liability | Account | Balance | Category | Notes
```

Categories:
- **Assets:** Cash, HYSA, Brokerage, Roth IRA, 401(k), HSA, Vehicle (KBB), Other
- **Liabilities:** Credit Cards, Auto Loan, Student Loans, Personal Loans, Other

Take a snapshot on the **last day of every month**. Plot the trendline. This is the single most motivating chart you'll ever look at.

### Sheet 3 — Debt Payoff Schedule

```
Debt Name | Balance | APR | Min Payment | Strategy Order | Payoff Month | Total Interest
```

Add a small simulator section: "If I pay $X/mo extra, payoff date moves from A to B and I save $C in interest." This is what makes you actually pay extra.

### Sheet 4 — Sinking Funds

This is the concept Yang completely skips. **Sinking funds = monthly contributions for known irregular expenses.**

Examples:
```
Fund          | Annual Cost | Monthly | Current Balance | Target Date
Car Insurance | $1,800       | $150    | $X              | Renewal date
Car Maintenance| $600         | $50     | $X              | Rolling
Christmas/Gifts| $600         | $50     | $X              | Dec
Travel        | $1,200       | $100    | $X              | Rolling
Medical/Dental| $600         | $50     | $X              | Rolling
Tech replacement| $1,200      | $100    | $X              | Rolling
```

Without sinking funds, every irregular expense feels like a "surprise" and wrecks your budget. With them, your monthly is *boring and predictable*.

Tip: Ally Bank's "buckets" feature does this natively. Otherwise, one HYSA + a tracking sheet.

### Sheet 5 — Goals & Stretch Plan

```
Goal | Target Amount | Target Date | Monthly Contribution | Current Progress | % Complete
```

Yang puts this at week 12. It belongs at week 1.

## Your Situation: Cook + Entrepreneur in FL

Things the generic plan doesn't account for that apply directly to you:

- **Variable income (kitchen).** Build a "smoothing buffer" — one month of base expenses in checking, separate from the $1K emergency fund. This absorbs slow weeks without dipping into savings.
- **Tip income tax handling.** If you receive significant cash tips, declare them. Underreported tips → no provable income → no apartment, no car loan, no mortgage. Bad short-term win, terrible long-term.
- **No state income tax (Florida).** Good news. But Florida sales tax is 6% + local — it's already baked into spending, no action item.
- **Self-employment (BVX).** If/when BVX generates income:
  - Open a separate business checking (Mercury, Bluevine, or Novo — all free, online).
  - Track income and expenses from day one. Schedule C deductions matter.
  - At ~$1K+ in net annual SE income, you owe **15.3% self-employment tax** plus regular income tax. Set aside ~25–30% of net business income for taxes.
  - Quarterly estimated taxes (April 15 / June 15 / Sept 15 / Jan 15) once you cross the threshold.
- **No employer-sponsored 401(k) for SE income.** Once BVX is making real money, look at **Solo 401(k)** or **SEP IRA** — both let you contribute a lot more than a Roth IRA.
- **Florida-specific:** Florida has no homestead estate tax issue you need to worry about for years, but if you ever buy property here, the homestead exemption is significant.
- **Hurricane season insurance check.** If you rent, get **renters insurance with flood + hurricane coverage** — most standard policies exclude wind/flood in FL.

## Account Architecture (Bank Setup)

Yang says "automate transfers." He doesn't say *to where*. Here's the structure:

```
                    ┌────────────────────────┐
[Paycheck] ───────► │  Primary Checking      │
                    │  (1 month expenses)    │
                    └─────┬──────────────────┘
                          │
        ┌─────────────────┼─────────────────────┐
        ▼                 ▼                     ▼
 ┌─────────────┐   ┌──────────────┐    ┌──────────────────┐
 │ Bills       │   │ HYSA         │    │ Brokerage        │
 │ Checking    │   │ ─ Emergency  │    │ ─ Roth IRA       │
 │ (autopay)   │   │ ─ Sinking    │    │ ─ Taxable        │
 │             │   │   Funds      │    │                  │
 └─────────────┘   └──────────────┘    └──────────────────┘
```

Why two checking accounts: one for fixed bills on autopay (rent, utilities, subs) and one for spending. You never accidentally spend rent money on takeout.

## Pre-Flight Checklist

Before you even hit week 1, do these:

- [ ] Pick a transaction aggregator (Monarch / Copilot / YNAB / Sheets)
- [ ] Pick a HYSA (Wealthfront / Ally / SoFi / Marcus)
- [ ] Set up password manager (Bitwarden / 1Password)
- [ ] Confirm direct deposit and pay schedule (need this for automation)
- [ ] Locate logins for: every bank, every credit card, every loan, retirement accounts, brokerage
- [ ] Pull one free credit report from AnnualCreditReport.com as baseline
- [ ] Decide: are you going avalanche or snowball on debt?
- [ ] Block out 2 hours every Sunday for the next 12 weeks

Once these are in place, week 1 takes ~90 minutes. Without them, week 1 takes a month and you give up.
