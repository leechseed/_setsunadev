## 1. Concept Summary <a id="concept-summary"></a>

A locally-owned, fast-casual Chinese concept positioned as a **lunch destination** for [CITY] state employees and office workers, with a small intentional dine-in space and three layered revenue streams across the day:

- **Lunch (11am-2pm):** Office worker dine-in + walk-in takeout (peak revenue)
- **Afternoon (2pm-5pm):** Boba tea / Asian drinks bar + study cafe + Asian snack retail corner
- **Dinner (5pm-8pm):** Family takeout + delivery + light dine-in
- **Late-night (Thurs-Sat 9pm-1am):** Pickup window + delivery only, lobby closed

Tight 30-35 item core menu, wok-to-order with strategic hot-hold for high-volume items. Built for a 30/30/40 split between dine-in / walk-in takeout / app delivery.

The "let's go here instead because…" line we're trying to earn:
> *"It's the spot."*

That undefined-but-clear status that Pita Pit currently has with state workers. No Chinese restaurant in [CITY] owns that with the under-35 crowd. Vacant territory.

## 2. Strategic Positioning <a id="positioning"></a>

### What we are
- Fast-casual Chinese, modern brand, local feel
- Wok-to-order quality at fast-casual speed (5-8 min build)
- Premium-tier ticket: $14-17 average
- Multi-daypart with intentional layered revenue
- Tech-forward operations (POS data, demand forecasting, direct ordering)
- A genuine *destination* — atmosphere matters

### What we are NOT
- Cheap-and-fast (Panda Express owns that)
- Cheap-and-quality (family takeouts own that, can't beat their cost basis)
- Buffet (dying model, labor impossible)
- Generic-Asian-restaurant aesthetic (red/gold/dragon) — boring and fungible
- Authentic-regional-Chinese fine dining (different game entirely)

### The triangle pick
Speed-Quality-Price: pick two.
**We pick Speed + Quality.** Premium pricing absorbs the cost of both.

### Competitive frame
We are NOT competing with Panda Express, Chopstix, China Dragon, Ming Tree.
We ARE competing with **Pita Pit, Newk's, Voodoo Dog, Jersey Mike's** — the lunch-destination tier for [CITY] professionals.

That's a much weaker competitive set with no Chinese player in it.

## 4. The Edge Stack <a id="edges"></a>

Six compounding edges. None of them is huge alone. Together they're a moat.

### Edge 1: Tight menu + menu engineering as discipline
- 30-35 items max
- Quarterly review classifying every item: Star / Plowhorse / Puzzle / Dog
- Reprice or kill underperformers every 90 days
- POS data drives the decisions
- **No competitor in town does this**

### Edge 2: Demand forecasting from POS data
- Pull daily sales, layer in: day of week, weather, [SCHOOL]/FAMU games, state holidays, local events
- Build forecasting model in Python
- Output: prep sheets that adjust automatically
- Worth ~3-5% of revenue in food cost savings ($25-35K on $750K)
- **Leverages owner's technical skill set directly**

### Edge 3: Direct ordering + loyalty data
- Own ordering site (Toast/Square/custom)
- Loyalty points, every 10th order comped
- Build email/SMS list of engaged customers
- Drive slow days with targeted pushes
- Each % of order volume shifted from app to direct = +20-25% margin on those orders
- **Highest-leverage tech investment available**

### Edge 4: Atmosphere + identity
- Modern but warm aesthetic, not corporate, not generic-Asian
- Brand name avoids Dragon/Wok/Garden/Express
- Real photography, real social presence
- Trained, present staff
- **In [CITY], this alone is 50% of differentiation**

### Edge 5: Agile menu / cultural responsiveness
- Permanent rotating "limited time" slot — 1-2 items, swap every 6-8 weeks
- Test trends (Sichuan, birria-fusion, tanghulu, whatever's next)
- Generates social content
- Codifies winners into permanent menu
- **Family places will serve same items in 2030 they served in 2010**

### Edge 6: Operating system — SOP layer
- Documented recipes (weights, not handfuls)
- Prep, opening, closing checklists
- Standardized portioning (scales, not eyeballs)
- Daily/weekly inventory in spreadsheet
- Training videos for every station
- **Makes the business sellable and replicable**

### Bonus edge: Multi-daypart layering
See [Section 5](#daypart-model). Three businesses sharing one footprint.

## 5. Multi-Daypart Operating Model <a id="daypart-model"></a>

### The day at a glance

| Time | Service | Lobby state | Revenue mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11am-2pm | Lunch — dine-in + takeout | Full, peak | Highest density |
| 2pm-5pm | Boba bar + light snacks + study cafe | Active, low-density | Drinks margin (75-85%) |
| 5pm-8pm | Dinner takeout + delivery + light dine-in | Moderate | Family + delivery |
| 8pm-9pm | Wind down | Closing | Light |
| 9pm-1am (Thu-Sat only) | Late-night pickup window + delivery | CLOSED, kitchen-only | College takeout |

### Layer 1: Lunch (the anchor)
The whole concept exists to win this daypart. Office workers, $14-17 ticket, dine-in groups, walk-in takeout.

### Layer 2: Boba + Asian snack retail (the lobby utilizer)
- 8-12 boba/milk tea drinks on the menu
- Sealing machine ($800-1500), train one staffer on tea brewing
- Cross-trained cashier handles boba prep — not a full barista
- **Asian snack retail corner:** shelf of imported ramens, Pocky, Hi-Chew, weird Japanese Kit Kats, Asian sodas, Hello Panda, etc. High-margin impulse buys, signals identity, gives college kids a reason to come in.
- **Refrigerator with imported ice cream** (mochi, taiyaki, red bean, ube). Same logic.
- This is the "All Saints" play — capture the nerdy / weeb / outsider student crowd that wants a third place
- Drinks alone: $400-800/day potential

### Layer 3: Dinner (the family takeout layer)
Standard Chinese takeout business. Phone orders, app orders, walk-in pickups.

### Layer 4: Late-night window (Thurs/Fri/Sat 9pm-1am)
- Lobby is closed
- Pickup window only (or pickup shelf in vestibule)
- Tight late-night menu: lo mein, fried rice, sesame chicken combos — wok stuff that travels well
- Delivery via apps
- Targets college kids post-bar / studying late
- Reference: how the dessert/ice cream window concept works
- **Estimated $4-6K/week additional revenue with marginal labor cost**

### Why this works
- Lobby pays for itself even when "empty" — atmosphere drives premium pricing on lunch
- No daypart is wasted; afternoon and late-night were dead zones for incumbents
- Each layer has a different customer, different staffing intensity, different margin profile
- Diversified revenue = lower risk

## 7. Financing Stack <a id="financing"></a>

You don't write a $390K check. Standard structure:

### Target capital stack

| Source | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Owner equity injection | $60-80K | Must come from you / partners |
| SBA 7(a) loan | $250-300K | 20-25 yr amort, ~10.5-11.5%, ~$2,500-3,000/mo payment |
| Equipment financing | $40-60K | Often easier, equipment is collateral |
| Vendor terms / credit | $20-30K | Available after a few months operating |

### Sources of the $60-80K equity

1. **Personal savings (18-24 months aggressive saving):** target $25-40K
2. **Adult content venture income:** if hits $5,200/mo target by late 2027, contributes $30-50K
3. **Partner equity:** silent partner with $30-50K for 20-30% ownership
4. **Family loan:** lower interest, longer terms, no dilution
5. **ROBS rollover:** if 401(k) exists from past employment, tax-free if structured right
6. **Florida programs:** Florida First Capital, JEDC small business loans, FL Microfinance Loan Program ($5-50K)

### SBA 7(a) requirements
- Personal credit 680+ ideal
- 2 years tax returns
- Business plan + 3-year financial projections
- 10-20% equity injection from borrower
- Personal guarantee required
- Pre-qualification process: 60-90 days

### Risk exposure
- Personal $60-80K equity (could lose entirely)
- Personal guarantee on SBA loan (~$250K+ liability if it fails)
- 2-3 years of below-market labor
- Personal credit damage on default
- NOT at risk: house (FL homestead), other LLCs, IP

### Months 1-3 launch crew (7 people, ~250 weekly hours)

- Owner (60+ hrs)
- 1 wok cook FT (~45 hrs)
- 1 line/prep cook FT (~40 hrs)
- 2 counter/cashier (1 FT 35 hrs, 1 PT 20 hrs)
- 1 shift lead / asst manager FT (~45 hrs)
- 1 PT weekend/night (15-20 hrs)

**Monthly labor cost: ~$22-25K**

### Months 6-12 expansion (if revenue hits)

Add: 1 more line cook, 1 late-night counter, 1 more PT cashier
**Monthly labor cost: ~$26-30K**

### The critical hire: GM / right-hand person

Non-negotiable role. If you can't find this person, don't open.

**Profile:**
- Marine Corps NCO archetype: leader by competence, not babysitter
- 45-50 hrs/week, salaried $50-60K + 5-10% revenue-based bonus
- Authority to hire/fire counter staff (not cooks)
- Owns schedule, inventory, daily cash recon
- Closes 4 nights/week, opens 3 mornings/week (so owner can sleep)
- Trusted with keys, safe code, deposits

**Where to find:**
- [CITY] veteran community (Vet Center, [SCHOOL] SVA)
- Hospitality assistant managers stuck at corporate (Newk's, Pita Pit, Chipotle, Panera)
- TCC / [SCHOOL] culinary grads who want operations not BOH

**What to offer:**
- Salary + revenue bonus
- Real authority and ownership
- Path to 1-3% equity after year 2
- Documented systems (not flying blind)
- Respect and development

### Empowered-not-corralled culture mechanics

- Decision authority matrix posted on wall
- Daily 10-min pre-shift huddle
- Weekly 15-min 1-on-1 with each FT employee
- Profit share / bonus structure tied to targets
- Documented training for every station

## 9. 24-Month Launch Roadmap <a id="roadmap"></a>

Realistic launch: **mid-to-late 2028**. Not 2026, not 2027.

### Phase 1: Foundation (May 2026 - Nov 2026)
**Focus: stabilize income, build wok foundation, save**

- Stay at [EMPLOYER] full-time
- Launch + stabilize adult content venture (target $1,200/week)
- Save aggressively (30-40% rate)
- Read wok-specific texts: Grace Young *Breath of a Wok*, Kenji *The Wok*, Chinese Cooking Demystified YouTube
- Buy carbon steel wok + turkey fryer burner ($200), practice 3x/week at home
- Begin scouting [CITY] neighborhoods (drive Capital Circle SE, Bradfordville, Mahan, Thomasville Rd weekly)

### Phase 2: Wok apprenticeship (Nov 2026 - May 2027)
**Focus: real wok experience, system observation**

- Reduce [EMPLOYER] to 25-30 hrs OR transition out
- Get hired at a Chinese restaurant in [CITY]
- Position: "learning Chinese cuisine" — true and non-threatening
- Work wok, prep, line stations
- Mentally catalog: ordering, inventory, prep timing, customer flow
- DO NOT work at a place geographically near where you plan to open
- DO NOT poach staff later

### Phase 3: Concept development (May 2027 - Nov 2027)
**Focus: nail the concept, begin formal capital path**

- Capable on wok at production speed by now
- Active menu R&D, brand identity work
- Travel: Atlanta, Orlando, Charlotte to eat at fast-casual Asian concepts (Pei Wei, Stir Fry Cafe, etc.)
- Begin formal real estate scouting with broker
- Begin SBA pre-qualification (60-90 days)
- Build financial model, business plan
- Identify potential GM candidate(s)

### Phase 4: Pre-launch (Nov 2027 - May 2028)
**Focus: lease, build, hire, stock**

- Sign lease, begin DBPR plan review
- Buildout (8-16 weeks depending on space condition)
- Recruit and pre-hire GM
- Equipment purchase / install
- Health inspections, final permits
- Recruit core team
- Begin brand / social media presence (don't wait till launch)

### Phase 5: Soft launch (mid-2028)
**Focus: training, calibration, fix what breaks**

- Friends and family week
- Limited menu, limited hours
- Iterate hard based on what breaks

### Phase 6: Grand open (mid-to-late 2028)
**Focus: marketing push, run the playbook**

- Full menu, full hours
- Marketing campaign
- Track everything via POS
- First quarterly menu engineering review at month 3

### Wok experience without burning bridges — rules

1. **Geographic separation.** Work somewhere on Apalachee, open on Capital Circle SE. Different customer pools.
2. **No staff poaching.** Hire from culinary grads, vets, other restaurants.
3. **Honest framing at the right moment.** "Learning Chinese cuisine" when applying. "Different concept different area" when leaving.
4. **30-day notice + gratitude on exit.** Most owners will respect it.
5. **Concept differentiation = ethical cover.** Fast-casual w/ boba and late-night ≠ traditional family takeout.
