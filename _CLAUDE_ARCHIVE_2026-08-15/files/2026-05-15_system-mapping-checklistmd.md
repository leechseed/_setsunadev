# System Mapping Checklist

A step-by-step procedure for understanding any system (BVX included) without going in circles.

> **The one rule:** You are never building *one* model. You are building a small *set* of deliberately incomplete models, each answering one question. The vertigo comes from trying to hold all of them in your head at once. Don't. Put each on its own page.

## 1. Before You Start

- [ ] Pick **one system** to map. Not "everything." Write its name at the top of a page.
- [ ] Get four blank pages (or four canvas tabs). One per artifact. Keep them separate on purpose.
- [ ] Accept up front: **each page will be incomplete.** That is correct, not failure.
- [ ] Write this reminder somewhere visible: *"A system is a way of looking, not a thing out there."*

---

## Step 1 — Make the Boundary Decision

The boundary is **not discovered, it is chosen for a purpose.** Decide it, don't hunt for it.

- [ ] Finish this sentence in writing:
      *"For the purpose of ______, this system includes ______ and excludes ______."*
- [ ] If you can't fill the purpose blank, you're not ready to map yet — pick a purpose first.
- [ ] List 3–5 things that are **clearly inside** the boundary.
- [ ] List 3–5 things that are **clearly outside** but touch it (these become your *environment*).
- [ ] Note anything you're **unsure** about. Don't resolve it — just park it. It often resolves later.

> If "what is BVX" feels unanswerable, that's because it has no single answer. Different purpose = different boundary. This step is where the circling stops.

---

## Step 2 — Draw the Rich Picture

One messy page. The whole mess, all at once. No structure required yet.

- [ ] On a single page, sketch **everything** in the system: people, parts, money, content, tools, tensions.
- [ ] Use doodles, arrows, stick figures, speech bubbles. Ugly is fine. Words-only is *not* — make it visual.
- [ ] Draw the **conflicts and frustrations**, not just the tidy parts. Mark where things feel stuck.
- [ ] Do NOT try to organize it. Let it be chaotic. Structure emerges *from* this drawing later.
- [ ] When the page feels "full enough," stop. Don't perfect it.

> This is Checkland's rich picture. Its whole job is to get the mess *out of your head and onto one surface* so you can look at it instead of spinning inside it.

---

## Step 3 — Map the Structure

New page. One question only: **What are the parts and how do they connect?**

- [ ] List the major components (aim for 5–9 — if you have 30, you're mixing levels; group them).
- [ ] Draw each as a box.
- [ ] Connect boxes with lines showing **relationships** (depends on, owns, feeds, reports to).
- [ ] Do NOT add time, flow, or sequence here. Structure only. Resist it.
- [ ] Check: could someone understand *what the system is made of* from this page alone?

---

## Step 4 — Map One Process

New page. One question only: **How does work move through the system over time?**

- [ ] Pick **one** important flow (e.g. for BVX: idea → content → published → revenue).
- [ ] Draw it as a sequence: start → steps → end. Time moves left to right.
- [ ] Mark where the process **waits**, **branches**, or **hands off** to someone/something else.
- [ ] If you find yourself wanting to map a second process — good, but do it on *another* page.
- [ ] One process per page. Never combine them.

> "Not everything is a process" — correct. Process is just *one aspect*. That's why it gets its own page and not the whole map.

---

## Step 5 — Map the Feedback

New page. One question only: **What reinforces what? What balances what?**

- [ ] Identify loops: "more of X causes more of Y, which causes more of X" (reinforcing) or "...which causes *less* of X" (balancing).
- [ ] Draw each loop as a circle of arrows. Label each loop **R** (reinforcing) or **B** (balancing).
- [ ] Find at least one loop that **helps** the system and one that **fights** it.
- [ ] Mark the points where a small change would have a big effect (leverage points).

---

## Step 6 — Run the Holon Check

Every system is both a *whole* and a *part*. You cannot understand a level from inside it. Look up, look down.

- [ ] **Look UP:** Name the larger system this one sits inside. *That is your context and your constraints.*
      → "BVX sits inside ______, which limits/shapes it by ______."
- [ ] **Look DOWN:** Name the sub-systems inside your components. *Those are your mechanism — the how.*
      → "Component ______ is itself made of ______."
- [ ] Confirm you mapped Steps 1–5 at **one consistent level.** If a box is really a whole sub-system, that's a *separate* mapping job — note it and move on. Don't map it now.

> This is the fix for "where do I even stand." You stand at one level, borrow context from above, borrow mechanism from below.

---

## Step 7 — Stop, Review, Iterate

- [ ] Lay all four artifacts side by side: rich picture, structure, process, feedback.
- [ ] The "real system" is the **overlay** of these — not any single one. Read them together.
- [ ] Ask: *Did the purpose from Step 1 get answered?* If yes, you're done. Stop.
- [ ] If a new question appeared, that's a **new mapping job** with its **own purpose and boundary** — start a fresh Step 1. Do not bolt it onto these pages.
- [ ] Park leftover unknowns in a list. You don't need to resolve everything. Incomplete is the design.

## Quick Reference Card

| Step | Page | The one question it answers |
|------|------|------------------------------|
| 1 | — | Where is the boundary, and for what purpose? |
| 2 | Rich Picture | What does the whole mess look like? |
| 3 | Structure | What are the parts and how do they connect? |
| 4 | Process | How does work move through it over time? |
| 5 | Feedback | What reinforces and what balances what? |
| 6 | Holon Check | What's above (context) and below (mechanism)? |
| 7 | — | Did I answer my purpose? If yes, stop. |

**If you feel yourself circling:** you are trying to answer two questions on one page, or map two levels at once. Split them. One page, one question, one level.

*Sources behind this: Weinberg (boundary as a way of looking), Checkland (rich picture, purpose), Koestler & Salthe (holons, look up/look down), Ackoff (the "mess"), system dynamics (feedback loops), aerospace/architecture practice (separate views per question).*
