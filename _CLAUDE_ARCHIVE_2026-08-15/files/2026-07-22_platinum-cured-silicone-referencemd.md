## Material fundamentals

- **Chemistry class:** addition-cure silicone. A platinum compound catalyzes the cure, versus the tin-based catalysts used in cheaper condensation-cure silicones.
- **Why the cure method matters:** the platinum-catalyzed reaction cross-links polymer chains into a stable, non-porous matrix and releases **no byproducts**. Condensation-cure silicones off-gas during curing (the vinegar smell is acetic acid). A finished addition-cure piece is chemically inert.
- **Stability:** the cured matrix doesn't degrade under normal use; a properly made piece holds its physical properties for decades.

## Property specifications

| Property | Spec / note |
|---|---|
| Porosity | Non-porous — microorganisms cannot penetrate the surface |
| Chemical reactivity | Inert; no leaching, no reaction with body fluids or standard cleaners |
| Temperature tolerance | Approx. −65°F to 400°F — survives boiling-water sterilization and freezer use |
| Elasticity | Retains flexibility and tear resistance over years; no brittleness with age |
| Biocompatibility | Same material family used in permanent medical implants; true allergy is extremely rare |
| UV / ozone | Resistant; prolonged direct sun can still shift some pigments slightly |
| Colorfastness | Quality pigment systems don't bleed, transfer, or fade under normal use |

## "Medical-grade" — what the claim actually requires

The label is widely abused. A genuine medical-grade claim implies:

- **Biocompatibility testing** — cytotoxicity, sensitization, and irritation studies
- **USP Class VI certification** — biological-reactivity testing under defined conditions
- **Batch traceability** — every batch tested and documented
- **FDA-registered facility** (voluntary for this product class, but a real signal when present)

Working rule for the instrument: "medical-grade" on a listing is a *claim to verify*, not a spec. Absence of supporting documentation downgrades the claim.

## Authentication protocol

Field checks for whether a piece is genuinely platinum-cure. Useful both for acquisitions and as a published methodology segment.

### Sensory checks (on receipt)
- **Odor** — genuine material has little to none when new. Strong chemical, rubbery, or plasticky smell = red flag; persistent odor = ongoing off-gassing.
- **Surface residue** — should never leave an oily film on hands or develop a slick coating over time. Un-washable slickness suggests an uncured or degrading material.
- **Color behavior** — no color transfer during cleaning; no rapid dulling of vibrant pigments.

### Documentation checks (pre-purchase)
- Maker can produce **material sourcing info / MSDS** on request
- **Third-party lab testing** exists (not universal among good small makers, but a strong positive signal)
- Willingness to discuss process details openly — cure system, pigments, QC steps

### Small-maker evaluation criteria
- Transparency about silicone supplier and process
- Community-verified consistency across orders
- Responsive to detailed technical questions
- **Price floor logic:** genuine platinum-cure has a real material cost. Prices dramatically below market are themselves evidence of substitution.

### Hand-poured QC markers (per specimen)
- Consistent wall thickness; no structural thin spots
- Surface free of air bubbles and flow marks
- Detail fidelity matches the maker's promotional imagery
- Fully cured: uniform firmness, zero surface tack

## Care & sterilization protocol

### Routine (after each use)
Warm water + mild unscented soap. Antibacterial dish soap is sufficient. Soft-bristle toothbrush for textured surfaces and fine detail work.

### Full sterilization (any of the following)
| Method | Parameters |
|---|---|
| Boiling water | Full submersion, 3–5 minutes |
| UV cabinet | Baby-bottle sterilizer boxes work well for routine cycles |
| Steam autoclave | Material tolerates medical autoclave cycles |
| Hydrogen peroxide | 3% solution, chemical sterilization without heat |

### Avoid
- Harsh abrasives (surface scratching)
- Acetone and strong solvents
- Bleach solutions above ~10%
- **Prolonged contact with silicone-based lube** — can cause surface swelling over time (see [[#Lubricant compatibility]])

## Storage protocol

Directly feeds the [[Storage & Accession Protocol]].

- **Individual separation is mandatory.** Even quality silicones can chemically migrate against each other over long contact. One piece, one enclosure.
- **Enclosure:** breathable fabric (cotton/satin) pouch preferred; individual boxes or divided compartments acceptable.
- **Environment:** cool, dry, out of direct sunlight. Avoid temperature extremes for long-term storage.
- **Never seal while damp** — no plastic bags or airtight containers on a wet piece; even a non-porous surface can host growth in trapped moisture.
- **Structural support:** thin sections and delicate protrusions must not bear load in storage. Large pieces need support that keeps weight off narrow geometry and connection points.

## Firmness reference (Shore scale)

Standardized durometer language for the database schema and for communicating with makers.

| Shore rating | Class | Character | Typical application |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00-20 | Very soft | Near-gel, extreme flex | Specialty softness; can lack insertion structure |
| 00-30 to 00-50 | Soft | Compresses easily under finger pressure | Beginners, larger pieces, forgiving fit |
| A-05 to A-15 | Medium | Flex + structure balance | Default for most designs |
| A-20 to A-30 | Firm | Holds shape, targeted feel | Experienced use; thin sections; sharp texture definition |
| A-40+ | Very firm | Near-rigid, minimal flex | Specific functional requirements only |

**Variable-firmness constructions** (custom work): soft tip / firm base; firm core / soft exterior shell; progressive firmness along the length. Worth capturing as a schema field where applicable — a single Shore number undersells dual-density pieces.

## Lubricant compatibility

- **Water-based:** fully compatible; the default.
- **Hybrid:** compatible.
- **Silicone-based:** technically usable on high-quality silicone, but prolonged contact risks surface swelling — treat as incompatible for archive pieces as a conservation policy.

## Safety design features

Checklist items every specimen should pass; absence is itself a review finding.

- Flared base or equivalent retrieval-safe geometry
- Graduated size transitions — no abrupt diameter jumps
- Rounded edges throughout; no sharp mold lines
- Firmness appropriate to the geometry (thin sections need enough Shore to avoid structural failure; large pieces need enough softness for safe use)

## Hand-pour manufacturing notes

Context for maker conversations and for the specs-day narration voice.

- Two-part systems: polymer base + platinum catalyst, mixed at exact ratios (commonly **1:1 or 10:1** by formulation). Small ratio deviations → incomplete cure, altered properties, surface defects.
- **Vacuum degassing** after mixing pulls out entrained air; skipped or rushed degassing shows up as bubbles and weak points — one of the QC markers above.
- Molds are typically themselves platinum-cure silicone (best detail reproduction), prepped with cure-compatible release agents.
- Pour technique varies by design: fast pours minimize flow lines on smooth geometry; slow, controlled pours capture heavy texture.
- Small-batch economics allow per-piece inspection that mass production can't — the structural argument for the boutique-maker tier of the acquisition ladder.
