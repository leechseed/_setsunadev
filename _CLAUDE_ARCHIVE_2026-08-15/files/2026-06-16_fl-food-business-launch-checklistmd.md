## 1. The Big Picture

**Why commercial, not cottage.** Florida's cottage food law is generous ($250K/yr, no
license) but it bans exactly the things you keep gravitating toward: fermented foods,
pickled/acidified foods, hot sauce, jerky — and it's **direct-to-consumer only** (no
wholesale, no store shelves, no register placement). A licensed commercial kitchen removes
all of those limits.

**Two agencies — your product picks the lane:**

| Agency | Regulates | Your products? |
|---|---|---|
| **FDACS** (Dept. of Agriculture) | Packaged food you jar/bag and sell at markets, to stores, or online | ✅ This is your lane |
| **DBPR** (Hotels & Restaurants) | Food made to eat on the spot (restaurants, food trucks) | Only if you also serve fresh/hot food |

You're making **packaged shelf-stable goods → FDACS**.

## 2. Two-Tier Product Strategy

Launch on Tier 1 to get cash flowing and learn the permit/inspection rhythm. Add Tier 2
once you're ready for the ferments and hot sauce.

| | **Tier 1 — Launch Now** | **Tier 2 — Phase 2** |
|---|---|---|
| **Products** | Jams/preserves (muscadine, mayhaw, fig, guava, pepper jelly), honey, infused vinegars, dry rubs/seasoning blends, flavored salts, granola, candied/spiced nuts, baked goods | Hot sauce, pickles, shelf-stable kimchi/kraut, salsas, fermented/probiotic items |
| **Classified as** | Acid / high-sugar / dry, shelf-stable | **Acidified foods** (high-risk, pH-controlled) |
| **Requires** | FDACS permit + ServSafe Manager + commercial kitchen | All of Tier 1 **plus** BPCS cert + FDA Food Facility Registration + Process Authority scheduled process + FDACS Special Process Approval |
| **Lift** | Light | Heavier (but fully doable) |

> Keep the bass-pie and PB&J ideas parked on the back burner — they're fine, just not the
> fastest path to legal-and-selling.

### Option B — The kitchen you already work in (commissary agreement)
Cheapest if the owner says yes. To make it legal you need:
1. A **written commissary / shared-use agreement** from the owner.
2. **Your own FDACS food permit** listing that kitchen as your production facility.
3. An **FDACS inspection** of the facility for your use.
- Negotiate off-hours access as flat monthly rent, hourly, or a small revenue cut.
- ⚠️ Verify the restaurant's license covers your use; FDACS confirms during inspection.

- [ ] Tour **KitchenShare**; ask Tier 1 vs Tier 2 requirements.

- [ ] Read UF/IFAS guides FS400, FS318, FS325 (links in §9).

### Phase 1 — Get legal on Tier 1 and start selling
- [ ] Register the business (DBA/LLC) + get **EIN** (free, irs.gov).
- [ ] Get **Local Business Tax Receipt** (City of [CITY] / Leon County).
- [ ] Earn **ServSafe Manager** certification.
- [ ] Lock kitchen (membership agreement or signed commissary letter).
- [ ] Apply for **FDACS Food Establishment Permit** + facility inspection.
- [ ] Register for **sales tax** with FL Dept. of Revenue (candy & some items are taxable).
- [ ] Build compliant **labels** (see §7).
- [ ] Get **product liability insurance**.
- [ ] Start selling: farmers markets, pop-ups, online (in FL), and now **wholesale to stores**.

### Phase 2 — Add acidified / fermented (hot sauce, pickles, ferments)
- [ ] Complete **Better Process Control School – Acidified** (online).
- [ ] **FDA Food Facility Registration** (Form 3537).
- [ ] Get recipes validated by a **Process Authority** (scheduled process).
- [ ] File **FDACS Special Process Approval**; pass acidified-process inspection.
- [ ] Launch the hot sauce / ferment line.

## 6. Permits & Registrations

- **FDACS Food Establishment Permit** — Wholesale/Manufactured (selling to stores) or
  Retail (selling direct). Apply + schedule facility inspection via FDACS. Fee varies by
  category — confirm current amount on the FDACS permit page. Renews annually; random
  inspections apply.
- **Local Business Tax Receipt** — City of [CITY] and/or Leon County.
- **DBA / business entity** — register the name (and form an LLC if you want liability
  separation).
- **EIN** — free from the IRS.
- **FL Dept. of Revenue (sales tax)** — register; candy and individual prepared servings
  are typically taxable.
- **Product liability insurance** — not legally required but strongly recommended (and
  often required by markets/retailers). ~$300–600/yr.
- **Self-employment tax** — if net earnings hit $400+, you file and pay SE tax.

## 7. Packaged-Food Labeling Requirements

Every package needs:
- Product name
- Your business name + address
- **Ingredients** listed in descending order by weight
- **Net weight / volume**
- **Allergen statement** (milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy)
- **Nutrition info** if you make a nutrition claim (avoid claims early — they trigger
  labeling rules and edge toward FDA-regulated territory)

> Commercial (FDACS-permitted) products do **not** carry the "made in a cottage food
> operation…" disclaimer — that line is cottage-only.

## 8. Acidified / Fermented Track (Tier 2 detail)

Hot sauce, pickles, and shelf-stable kraut/kimchi are **acidified foods** — a high-risk
process FDACS controls tightly because of botulism (C. botulinum) risk. To sell them
shelf-stable you need **all four**:

1. **BPCS – Acidified** certificate (a trained supervisor must be on hand during production).
2. **FDA Food Facility Registration** (Form 3537) — identify as an Acidified/LACF processor.
3. **Scheduled process from a Process Authority** — a qualified expert validates your
   recipe's pH (must end ≤ 4.6) and process. UF/IFAS Extension is your FL starting point
   for a referral. Guide: EDIS **FS318**.
4. **FDACS Special Process Approval** + acidified-process facility inspection.

> Plain probiotic *drinks* and kombucha are their own beverage/fermentation headache —
> treat those as a separate, later project, not part of this build.
