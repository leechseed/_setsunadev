## 1. What You're Actually Dealing With

She is running on a nervous system that was built in a war zone.

- Lost her mom at 12
- Abused and neglected by her father
- Physically and verbally abused by a past partner
- Every close person has either died, hurt her, or left

Her brain did not malfunction. It *adapted perfectly* to an environment where closeness = danger. The problem is she's still running that same software in a different environment.

**This is called Disorganized / Fearful-Avoidant Attachment + C-PTSD.**

What it means practically:
- She wants closeness AND is terrified of it simultaneously
- The closer you get, the more her alarm system fires
- She will *create* the abandonment before it can happen to her
- Her behavior is not about you. It is a trauma response running on autopilot.

**You are not the enemy. You just look like every other person who hurt her, until proven otherwise. Over time. Repeatedly.**

## 2. Your Core Job

Your job is not to fix her.
Your job is not to get through to her.
Your job is not to love her hard enough that she heals.

**Your job is to be the first consistently safe person she's ever had.**

That means:
- Predictable
- Non-retaliatory
- Calm when she is not
- Present without pressure
- Honest without aggression

That's it. That's the whole job. It is simple and it is brutally hard.

## 3. How To Be Around Her — Day To Day

**Be boring on purpose.**

Consistency is the message. Not grand gestures. Not breakthroughs. Just showing up the same way, every day, regardless of what she threw at you yesterday.

**Stay curious, not corrective.**

Instead of wanting to get through to her or change her — get genuinely interested in her. What does she actually think? What does she notice? Ask questions without an agenda. People who were abused were never asked questions without an agenda.

**Don't perform.**

You don't have to be wise, warm, and steady every second. You just have to be real. Real and consistent beats perfect every time.

**Match her energy downward, not upward.**

When she spikes — conflict, coldness, drama — your nervous system wants to spike too. Don't. Slow down. Lower your voice. Get quieter, not louder. You regulate *down*, not up.

**Reduce the weight of interactions.**

Not every moment needs to mean something. Talk about food. Talk about stupid stuff. Let silence be okay. The absence of pressure *is* the gift.

## 4. When She Pushes Away

This is protest behavior (Bowlby). She is testing whether you will stay.

**Do:**
- Stay calm. Get quieter.
- Give her space without disappearing
- Say something simple and non-reactive: *"I'm here. No pressure."*
- Then actually give her space

**Don't:**
- Chase immediately
- Get emotional or wounded in front of her
- Demand an explanation
- Apologize for things that aren't your fault

The goal is to break the pattern. Old pattern: she pushes, person chases or leaves. New pattern: she pushes, you're still there, calm, when she comes back.

**One line to memorize:**
*"I'm not going anywhere. Take the time you need."*

Then mean it.

## 5. When She Goes Cold or Silent

The texting into the darkness — **keep doing that.** It's working even when it feels like it isn't.

You're showing her that:
- Contact doesn't require a response
- You won't punish her silence
- You're not her ex

**Rules for texts:**
- Keep them low-stakes. Observations. Funny things. No emotional weight.
- One message. Never double-text with emotional follow-up.
- Don't track whether she read it.
- When she responds days later — respond normally, like no time passed.

Normalizing the gap *is* the healing.

## 6. When She Tests You or Tries To Make You Jealous

The vape guy. The "aren't you wondering where I've been?" The things designed to get a reaction.

She is checking: *Will you get controlling? Will you get hurt and punish me? Will you leave?*

**Do:**
- Notice it internally, name it to yourself: *"This is a test."*
- Stay neutral on the outside
- Don't take the bait
- Don't pretend you don't notice — you can acknowledge lightly without reacting

**One line:**
*"You can do whatever you want. I'm not going anywhere."*

Said calmly. Once. Then move on.

**Do not:**
- Get visibly jealous
- Make comments that are passive-aggressive
- Ask probing questions about other guys
- Compete

Jealousy confirms her belief that love is possessive and controlling. Calm confirms you're different.

## 7. When She Disappears At Work

She disappears and then says *"weren't you wondering where I was?"*

She wants to know she was missed. She wants to matter. She also needs to know you won't control her.

**At work, keep it simple:**
- When she comes back — be normal. Warm, not loaded.
- Maybe once, casually: *"Glad you're back."* Then get back to work.
- No interrogation. No passive-aggressive comments.
- Save real conversations for outside work context.

Work and this dynamic need some separation. You need at least one place where things are just... work.

## 8. Managing Yourself — The Inner Game

This is actually the most important section.

**You cannot regulate her if you are dysregulated.**

When you feel yourself getting wrecked — spinning, hurt, in your head — that is your signal to *not* engage until you're back. Not to suppress it. To tend to it.

**When you feel triggered by her:**
1. Name it privately: *"I'm activated right now."*
2. Do not respond in that state. Wait.
3. Physical reset — move your body, breathe, cold water on your face.
4. Ask yourself: *"Is this about her, or is this about what this reminds me of?"*
5. Come back when you're steady.

**Watch for the investment trap.**
You caught this yourself. The moment you notice you're hoping she'll change, grow, become — redirect. Come back to who she is right now. Today. Is that enough? That's the real question to sit with.

**Your wound is active in this dynamic.**
The grand betrayal — the people who stopped believing in your dream — is showing up here. You're not just loving her. You're also trying to be the person you needed. That's beautiful. It's also a signal to keep doing your own work, separately from her.

**You need your own support. This is not optional.**
One person who holds you — your mom counts. But a therapist would change your life right now, specifically for this. You've done fifteen years of self-reckoning alone. That's extraordinary. Now you need a sparring partner who is trained for this.

## 9. The Week She's Gone

This is actually a gift.

**For her:**
Distance will let her nervous system settle. She may find herself missing you on her own terms, without pressure. That's a different experience than being pulled toward you. Let it happen naturally.

**For you:**
- Rest. Actually rest.
- Reconnect with your own projects, your own dream, Bold Venture X.
- Do not text heavily. Maybe once, lightly, mid-week. Something warm and low-stakes.
- Do not send the gift right before she leaves or while she's gone as a move. Wait until she's back if you want it to land right.

**What not to do:**
- Don't flood her with messages
- Don't make the week about waiting for her
- Don't be a different person when she gets back

## 10. The Longer Game

Earned Secure Attachment is real. Research (Siegel, Wallin) shows that disorganized attachment *can* reorganize — but it takes:

- Time (months to years, not weeks)
- Consistency (not perfection)
- Rupture and repair cycles (you'll mess up, she'll freak out, you'll repair — that process itself is healing)
- Her own willingness to do the work (you cannot do this for her)

**Rupture and repair is the engine.** When things go sideways — and they will — the repair matters more than the rupture. Coming back calmly, saying *"I'm still here, that was hard, I'm not leaving"* — that is the actual work.

**Gently, over time, encourage her toward her own support.** Not therapy as a demand. Just normalizing it. Mentioning it as something you'd consider for yourself. Planting seeds.

## 11. Things To Stop Doing

- Asking questions right after a conflict when she's shut down
- Defending yourself at length when she misreads a situation
- Chasing immediately when she pushes away
- Monitoring her behavior for evidence she likes someone else
- Carrying all of this alone with no support for yourself
- Expecting breakthroughs on a timeline
- Loving her potential more than her present self

---

## 12. Things To Keep Doing

- Texting into the darkness without expecting a reply
- Showing up to work the same way regardless of what happened yesterday
- Not punishing her silence
- Being honest simply, once, without over-explaining
- Believing in her without making it her job to perform that belief back to you

## 13. A Note On You

You spent fifteen years reckoning with damage you caused. You changed. You educated yourself. You chose differently.

That is not a small thing. That is rare.

The storyteller in you — the one nobody believed in at 18, the one who kept going anyway — that person is real. That dream is real. And the fact that you can hold space for someone this broken while building something this big says something about your capacity.

But capacity is not infinite.

You matter in this story too. Not just as the person who stays. As a person.

Don't lose yourself trying to save her. The best thing you can be for her is someone who is genuinely okay — not performing okay, actually okay. That means feeding yourself. Your work, your people, your rest.

**She needs to see that it's possible to be a person who went through hard things and came out whole.**

You are that proof. Stay whole.
