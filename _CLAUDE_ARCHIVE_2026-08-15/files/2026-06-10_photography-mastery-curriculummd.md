# PART I — THE PHYSICAL IMAGE

## 1. Optics & The Physics of the Image
*Why: you cannot control rendering you don't understand. This is the bedrock.*

**Concepts to master:**
- Light as electromagnetic radiation; wavelength → color; intensity → exposure
- The lens as a focusing system; focal length defined; the optical formula's effect on rendering
- **Aperture / f-stop** — the ratio, why it's a fraction, the stop as a doubling/halving unit
- **Depth of field** — governed by aperture, focal length, subject distance, *and sensor size* (critical for medium format: shallower DOF at equivalent framing — this is part of why MF "renders" differently)
- **Circle of confusion, hyperfocal distance, the plane of focus**
- **Diffraction** — why stopping down too far softens (matters on 102MP)
- **Bokeh** — the character of out-of-focus rendering; why the GF 110 f/2 matters
- **Aberrations** — chromatic, spherical, coma, distortion, vignetting — what they are, when they help (character) vs. hurt
- **Sensor physics** — photosites, the Bayer array, why sensor *size* drives tonal gradation more than megapixels; dynamic range; signal-to-noise; base ISO
- **The inverse square law** — light falloff with distance (governs all light shaping)

**Sources:**
- *Light Science and Magic* (Hunter, Biver, Fuqua) — the single most important technical book on this list; the physics of light-on-subjects, genre-agnostic, indispensable
- *The Manual of Photography* (Allen & Triantaphillidou) — the engineering reference, deep
- Optics chapters of any university photo-science text for the math when you want it

---

## 2. Exposure — The Foundational Triangle and Beyond
*Why: total fluency here must become unconscious, so attention is free for light and subject.*

**Concepts:**
- The **exposure triangle**: aperture, shutter speed, ISO — and the *creative* (not just technical) consequence of each choice
- **Shutter speed** as motion control — freezing vs. blur; your Tier 1 "caught in motion" lives here; hand-hold limits; IBIS's role
- **ISO** and noise; base-ISO discipline for max dynamic range on MF
- **Metering** — reflective vs. incident; matrix/spot/center-weighted; metering *for the highlights* in digital; the histogram as truth
- **ETTR** (expose to the right) — maximizing data in the raw file
- **The Zone System** (Ansel Adams) — even in digital, the mental model for mapping a scene's luminance to tonal values is the foundation of seeing tonally
- **Dynamic range** — reading a high-contrast scene (bright spring water, shaded forest) and knowing what the sensor can hold

**Sources:**
- Ansel Adams' trilogy: *The Camera*, *The Negative*, *The Print* — *The Negative* contains the Zone System; non-negotiable foundational reading even for digital shooters
- *Understanding Exposure* (Bryan Peterson) — the accessible on-ramp

---

## 3. The Camera as Instrument
*Why: the tool must disappear. Total muscle-memory fluency on your specific body.*

**Concepts:**
- Full manual operation without looking; back-button focus; custom function setup
- **Autofocus systems** — phase vs. contrast detect; face/eye AF; single vs. continuous; how to drive AF for moving (Tier 1) vs. static (Tier 3) subjects
- **Focus techniques** — single-point precision, focus-and-recompose risks at shallow DOF, manual focus with peaking/magnification for critical work
- **Histogram and clipping warnings** in-field
- File formats; the raw pipeline; color space at capture
- The GFX system specifically: its menu, its AF behavior, IBIS, lens communication, tethering

**Sources:**
- Your camera's manual, read fully (most photographers never do — this is free edge)
- Fujifilm GFX-specific guides and the better YouTube deep-dives on GFX AF behavior

---

## 4. Lenses — Rendering, Not Just Reach
*Why: focal length is a storytelling choice and part of your signature.*

**Concepts:**
- **Focal length and perspective** — wide = inclusion, environmental context, distortion of near features (Tier 1 habitat); normal ≈ human-eye; tele = compression, isolation, flattery (Tiers 2–3, the 110)
- **The portrait focal length tradition** — why ~85–135mm equivalent flatters faces (compression)
- **Perspective is distance, not focal length** — the crucial distinction (you change perspective by moving, not zooming)
- **Maximum aperture** — DOF control, subject separation, low-light capacity
- **Prime vs. zoom** — discipline and quality vs. flexibility
- Lens character: how different lenses render the same scene; "drawing" of a lens
- Macro/close-focus for detail work; tilt-shift for plane control (advanced)

**Sources:**
- *Light Science and Magic* again (lens/perspective sections)
- Hands-on comparison: shoot the same setup at 35/50/110 equiv until perspective is intuitive

# PART II — LIGHT
*The domain that most determines your signature. Spend the most time here.*

## 5. The Physics & Quality of Light
**Concepts:**
- **The five qualities of light**: intensity, *quality* (hard/soft), *direction*, *color* (temperature), *contrast*
- **Hard vs. soft light** — governed by relative source size; the sun (small, hard) vs. overcast sky (huge, soft); why this is *everything* for skin
- **Direction**: front, side, back, top, under — what each does to form and mood; **short vs. broad lighting**; **Rembrandt, loop, butterfly, split** patterns (know them even when shooting natural)
- **Color temperature** (Kelvin); golden hour, blue hour, open shade blue, mixed light
- **Specular vs. diffuse reflection** — the core of how skin, water, and wet surfaces render (huge for Tier 2 springs)
- **The family of angles** (from *Light Science and Magic*) — predicting where reflections land
- **Contrast ratio** — key-to-fill relationships even in natural light

**Sources:**
- *Light Science and Magic* — the lighting bible; read it three times
- *Picture Perfect Lighting* (Roberto Valenzuela) — natural-light-forward, practical "circumstantial light" framework

## 6. Natural Light Mastery
*Your core discipline — the "hunting, not building" doctrine.*

**Concepts:**
- **Reading available light** — quality, direction, color at a glance
- **Golden hour / blue hour** — behavior, duration (shorter than people think), how to plan a shoot around the 40-minute window
- **Open shade** — the portraitist's secret weapon; finding clean soft light; the blue-cast problem and its fix
- **Overcast as a giant softbox** — flat but flattering; when it's perfect, when it's dead
- **Dappled light** — the trap (messy) and the rare win
- **Backlight / rim light / lens flare** — the McGinley register; controlling flare as a tool
- **Light through water** — caustics, shafts, the spring-specific behavior (Tier 2)
- **Window light** (Tier 3) — direction, distance falloff (inverse square in a room), sheer-curtain diffusion; the entire Vermeer/Collins interior tradition
- **Time-of-day scouting** — visiting locations at shoot-hour; sun-path apps (e.g., sun-tracking tools) for planning
- **Weather as a creative variable**

**Sources:**
- Valenzuela, *Picture Perfect Lighting* (natural sections)
- Study Sam Abell and the documentary natural-light tradition for *seeing* available light
- Field practice is irreplaceable here — no book substitutes for hours at golden hour

## 7. Light Modification — Minimal-Kit Control
*Your whole lighting kit per the doctrine.*

**Concepts:**
- **Reflectors** (5-in-1: white/silver/gold/black/diffuser) — filling shadow, the black reflector as *subtractive* light (underrated)
- **Diffusion/scrim** — softening harsh midday sun over the subject; turning hard light soft on demand
- **Flags/negative fill** — shaping by *removing* light, the key to dimensional natural-light portraits
- **Bounce** — walls, sand, water, concrete as natural reflectors; reading what the environment bounces
- A reflector + scrim + an assistant to hold them is a *complete* natural-light shaping system

**Sources:**
- Valenzuela; any solid one-light/natural-modifier tutorial set
- Practice: one subject, one hour, reflector only — learn what subtraction and fill actually do

## 8. (Reference) Studio & Artificial Light
*Not your core, but literacy matters — you'll eventually want fill flash or a key for hybrid work.*

**Concepts (lighter pass):** strobe vs. continuous; sync speed; high-speed sync; flash-to-balance-ambient (fill flash outdoors — useful even in natural work); modifiers (softbox, octa, beauty dish, grid); the one-light setup as foundation; TTL vs. manual flash.

**Sources:** *Light Science and Magic*; the Profoto/Godox educational libraries; one-light workshops.

# PART III — THE VISUAL LANGUAGE

## 9. Composition & Visual Design
*Why: the difference between a snapshot and a photograph. The grammar of the frame.*

**Concepts — foundational:**
- Rule of thirds (and *when to break it*); the golden ratio/spiral; dynamic symmetry (the **Φ grid / root rectangles** — Jay Maisel and the painters' system)
- **Leading lines, framing, depth layering** (foreground/mid/background)
- **Negative space** and breathing room; **figure-ground**
- **Balance** — symmetrical vs. asymmetrical; visual weight
- **The frame edge** — what touches it, what's cut, the tension of the edge

**Concepts — advanced (the painter's level):**
- **Gestalt principles** — proximity, similarity, closure, continuity, common fate — how the eye groups
- **Visual path / eye movement** — designing how the gaze travels the frame
- **Notan** — the Japanese light/dark mass design (think in shapes of tone before detail)
- **Tension, rhythm, repetition, the diagonal**
- **The decisive moment** (Cartier-Bresson) — geometry + timing fused
- Composition in *motion* (Tier 1) vs. *contemplation* (Tier 3)

**Sources:**
- *The Photographer's Eye* (Michael Freeman) — the best single book on photographic composition
- *The Art of Photography* (Bruce Barnbaum) — composition + vision, deeper and more philosophical
- *Vision and Art: The Biology of Seeing* (Margaret Livingstone) — how the visual system actually processes images; why certain compositions *work* neurologically
- Study painting composition: Andrew Loomis, *The Eye of the Painter* (Loomis), and the Old Masters

## 10. Color Theory & Tonality
*Why: your grade is part of your signature. Color is meaning.*

**Concepts:**
- The color wheel; **hue, saturation, luminance/value**; complementary, analogous, triadic, split-complementary schemes
- **Color harmony and color contrast** in the frame; the dominant/accent relationship
- **Color temperature and mood** — warm/cool psychology; the teal-orange cliché and how to avoid it
- **Color and skin tone** — the single hardest color problem; memory colors; how environments cast onto skin
- **Tonal value** independent of color — the grayscale structure underneath
- **Munsell system** (hue/value/chroma) — the rigorous model
- The Josef **Albers** lesson: color is relational, never absolute

**Sources:**
- *Interaction of Color* (Josef Albers) — the foundational text on how color behaves perceptually
- *Color and Light* (James Gurney) — written for painters but the best practical book on how light *creates* color in a scene; transformative for photographers
- *Color* (Betty Edwards) — accessible theory

## 11. The Black-and-White Discipline
*Why: B&W is where you learn to see tonally and structurally — and the erotic-photo canon is heavily monochrome (Newton, Bellocq, Weston).*

**Concepts:**
- Seeing in tone, not color; the Zone System applied to B&W vision
- Contrast, the tonal curve, the role of mid-tones
- How color *channels* convert to gray (red darkens skies/lightens skin; the digital channel mixer)
- The drama of pure tonal sculpture — form revealed by light alone
- When a subject *wants* to be monochrome (form, gesture, light) vs. color (mood, place, flesh)

**Sources:**
- Adams, *The Print*
- Study Edward Weston (nudes/peppers — form as pure tone), Bill Brandt (nudes), Ruth Bernhard

# PART IV — THE HUMAN SUBJECT

## 12. Anatomy & The Body for Photographers
*Why: you can't light or pose form you don't understand. Painters study anatomy; photographers should too.*

**Concepts:**
- Surface anatomy — how muscle and bone shape the visible form; landmarks
- **The line of action / gesture** — the body's dynamic flow (from figure drawing)
- **Contrapposto** — weight shift, the classical S-curve that makes a standing figure alive (Greek sculpture → your standing poses)
- **Foreshortening** — how the body distorts toward/away from lens; managing it
- How **light describes form** — the roundness of a shoulder, the plane of a back; lighting *for* anatomy
- Proportion and the canon of ideal proportion (and its critique)
- The body in **motion** — how running/walking/turning reads (Tier 1)

**Sources:**
- *Figure Drawing for All It's Worth* (Andrew Loomis) — yes, a drawing book; the best anatomy-for-image-makers text there is
- *Classic Human Anatomy* (Valerie Winslow)
- Life-drawing sessions (even badly) teach you to *see* the figure faster than anything

## 13. Posing & Directing the Figure
*Why: the operational core of every shoot. Most photographers are weak here.*

**Concepts:**
- The **principles** behind poses (so you improvise, not memorize): weight distribution, diagonals over horizontals, creating space at joints, the line of the spine, hands and their curse
- **Contrapposto and the S-curve** for standing; the dynamics of seated/reclining/prone
- **Lengthening and flattering** — the camera-toward shoulder, chin-forward-and-down, avoiding compression of limbs into the body
- **Posing for the body type in front of you** — not a template; reading and adapting
- **Hands, feet, fingers** — where amateurs die; deliberate placement
- **Posing as feeling** — directing toward an emotional state, not a shape (the difference between stiff and alive)
- **Motion direction** (Tier 1) — walking, turning, "do it again but slower"; capturing the in-between
- **The reclining/water poses** (Tier 2) — buoyancy, wet-hair, the body in/at water
- **The intimate/interior register** (Tier 3) — relaxed, unposed-seeming, the art that conceals art

**Sources:**
- *The Art of Posing* / Roberto Valenzuela's *Picture Perfect Posing* — the best systematic treatment (his "15 elements" framework)
- Study the figure in classical painting and sculpture for pose vocabulary
- Bambi Cantrell, *The Art of Posing*

## 14. The Psychology of the Shoot — Rapport, Direction, Trust
*Why: this is your prestige flywheel and your treatment ethic, as a learnable skill. The three-tier ladder IS this discipline.*

**Concepts:**
- Establishing safety and trust *before* the camera; the pre-shoot conversation
- Directing language — clear, calm, affirming; "frames per direction"; what to say and not say
- Reading comfort/discomfort in real time; consent as ongoing, not one-time
- The energy of the set; music, pace, breaks; the photographer's emotional regulation sets the room
- Building a returning collaborator (the flywheel): the experience must be *good*, not just the photos
- Drawing genuine expression and presence vs. performed stiffness
- The specific dynamics of intimate/nude work — power, professionalism, boundaries, the written and unwritten agreement

**Sources:**
- Peter Hurley, *The Art Behind the Headshot* (his work on expression/"squinching" and connection)
- Study how the masters worked with subjects: Newton's collaborators describing empowerment; Avedon's process; the documentary-portrait tradition (Avedon's *In the American West* process)
- This is mostly learned by doing — Tier 1 is your training ground by design

## 15. The Face, Expression & The Portrait
**Concepts:**
- The geometry of the face; how light reveals/conceals; flattering vs. honest
- **Catchlights** — life in the eyes; their position and shape
- Expression as the subject (micro-expressions, the genuine vs. the held smile)
- The eyes as the anchor; the gaze toward/away from lens and its meaning
- Environmental portrait (subject + their world — your whole thesis) vs. studio portrait

**Sources:** Hurley (expression); Avedon, Penn, Leibovitz portrait monographs; *The Portrait* (Giorgio Bocca / or Sobieszek's writing on portraiture).

# PART V — THE DIGITAL DARKROOM
*Your existing Adobe depth is a massive head start; aim it at the photographic-specific knowledge.*

## 16. Color-Managed Workflow & The Raw File
**Concepts:**
- The **raw file** — what it is, what it isn't; demosaicing; why raw ≠ image
- **Color management** end-to-end: working color spaces (ProPhoto/Adobe RGB/sRGB), monitor calibration (a hardware calibrator is mandatory), soft-proofing

- **Capture One vs. Lightroom** — C1's color and tethering strengths matter for skin and studio/MF work

## 17. Developing, Grading & The Signature Look
*This is where your grade — part of the signature — gets built.*
**Concepts:**
- Global vs. local adjustments; the develop pipeline order
- **Tone curve mastery** — the real engine of "look"; contrast, film-like rolloff in highlights/shadows
- **HSL / color grading** — building a consistent palette; the shadow/midtone/highlight color split
- **Skin tone rendering** — the hardest grade; consistency across light conditions; building a repeatable skin pipeline
- **Building and applying a signature preset/profile** — so the body of work coheres (the grade as authorship)
- Emulating film stocks (the Portra/Tri-X traditions) if it serves the signature
- Black-and-white conversion (channel mixer, not desaturate)

**Sources:**
- Capture One / Lightroom mastery courses; the better colorist educators
- Study film stocks for tonal/color reference (Portra, Ektar, Tri-X) — the looks your audience's eye already associates with "premium/timeless"

## 18. Retouching — Skin, Restraint & Ethics
*Why: the last 10% that separates pro from amateur — and a place your signature's "authenticity" thesis sets a hard limit.*
**Concepts:**
- **Frequency separation** and **dodge & burn** (the two pillars of high-end skin retouching)
- Cleaning vs. *changing* — the line between polishing and falsifying (your signature is "revealed, not constructed" — so retouching ethic = restraint; keep texture, keep truth)
- Color/blemish/stray-hair cleanup; matching skin across a set
- Dodge & burn for *sculpting light* in post (advanced; painters' control)
- The body-image ethics: not reshaping bodies; the treatment doctrine extends into post

**Sources:**
- The high-end beauty-retouching educators (frequency separation + D&B tutorials)
- Your AE/Photoshop depth transfers heavily here

## 19. The Print as Object
*Why: per the prior report, the book/print is the prestige instrument. A photograph is finished as a print.*
**Concepts:**
- Why a print differs from a screen (reflective vs. emissive; the limits of gamut)
- Paper stocks (matte/luster/baryta/fine-art rag) and their effect; the print as material
- Soft-proofing, the print color pipeline, working with a pro lab vs. self-printing
- Sequencing and the **photo book** as a form (pacing, pairing, the page-turn) — study the book as its own art
- Edition/print sizing for sale

**Sources:**
- *The Print* (Adams) for the philosophy
- *The Photobook: A History* (Parr & Badger) — to understand the book as the canonical form
- Study Taschen production values (your stated ceiling)

## 20. History of Photography
**Survey the arc:** the daguerreotype/calotype origins → Pictorialism → the f/64 group and straight photography (Weston, Adams) → documentary (FSA, Evans) → the decisive moment (Cartier-Bresson) → the New York school → fashion's rise (Avedon, Penn) → the 70s color revolution (Eggleston, Shore) → the Pictures Generation/postmodern (Sherman, Prince) → digital/contemporary.

**Sources:**
- *A World History of Photography* (Naomi Rosenblum) — the standard survey
- *Photography: A Cultural History* (Mary Warner Marien)
- *On Photography* (Susan Sontag) — the essential critical-philosophical text
- *Camera Lucida* (Roland Barthes) — the other essential; the *punctum*/*studium*, what a photograph *is*

## 21. The Nude & The Erotic in Art
*Your genre's deep history — essential for both craft and positioning.*
**Concepts/arc:**
- The classical nude; the distinction (Kenneth Clark) between **"the naked" and "the nude"** — and Berger's critique of it
- The painted tradition: Titian, Goya's *Maja*, Manet's *Olympia* (the gaze that looks back), Ingres, the odalisque tradition
- Photographic nude: Weston (form), Bellocq (Storyville portraits — intimacy and document), Bill Brandt (distortion), Ruth Bernhard, Imogen Cunningham
- The erotic vs. the pornographic — the contested line; intent, gaze, agency
- The female-gaze turn: how contemporary women photographers reclaimed the nude (relevant to your "co-authored" thesis)

**Sources:**
- *The Nude: A Study in Ideal Form* (Kenneth Clark) — the classical position
- *Ways of Seeing* (John Berger) — the essential counter-argument; Ch. 3 on the nude and the gaze is required reading for you specifically
- Monographs: Weston, Bellocq (*Storyville Portraits*), Brandt's *Perspective of Nudes*

## 22. Your Specific Lineage — Deep Study
*Not survey — deep, repeated, analytical study of the handful who own your vein.*
- **Ryan McGinley** — *The Kids Are Alright*, the road-trip nudes; study his natural light, motion, color, the freedom; closest to Tiers 1–2
- **Petra Collins** — *Coming of Age*; the hazy female-gaze interior intimacy; closest to Tier 3
- **Ren Hang** — the charged, strange, snapshot-flash bodies-in-nature
- **Sam Haskins** — *Cowboy Kate*, *November Girl*; the warm naturalistic erotic root
- **Helmut Newton** — *SUMO*, *Big Nudes*, *White Women*; studied as compositional rigor and the *artifice* you're inverting
- **Slim Aarons** (for the sun-drenched aspirational-place register, the Vixen-adjacent lifestyle light)
- **Jock Sturges / Sally Mann** — studied carefully and *critically* for the natural-light-figure tradition and its serious ethical/legal debates (instructive about where lines are and why they matter)

*Method:* for each, build a reference board (your native skill); reverse-engineer their light, lens, color, and *what they refuse*. Write the one-sentence signature for each. Then locate yours in the gaps between them.

*Why: to make erotic images with intent and defensibility, you must understand the discourse you're working inside.*

- Representation, power, and consent in the image

## 24. Ethics, Consent & The Treatment Doctrine
*Why: your moat and your risk live here. This is craft, not just compliance.*
**Concepts:**
- Informed, ongoing, documented consent; model releases (legal) vs. consent (ethical) — both required
- Age verification and documentation as an absolute, non-negotiable, recorded practice (the single highest-stakes operational rule in this entire venture)
- The power dynamic of photographer/subject and how to neutralize it
- Boundaries negotiated before, honored during, revisited after
- Image rights, usage, takedown, and the subject's ongoing control
- The "positive influence" thesis operationalized: pay, treatment, after-care, agency in selection/approval
- *This connects directly to the parked items: model releases, usage agreements, and the legal/firewall architecture.*

**Sources:** professional model-release templates and the legal frameworks for your jurisdiction (Florida); the ethical literature in documentary/portrait photography; sex-worker-led resources on ethical adult production.

## 25. Developing a Body of Work & Authorial Voice
**Concepts:**
- The difference between *photographs* and *a body of work* (the book thesis again)
- Editing and sequencing as authorship; the project vs. the one-off
- Consistency of vision across a series; the signature as discipline of refusal

**Sources:** *The Photographer's Playbook* (ed. Fulford & Halpern) — assignments/wisdom from many photographers; study how monographs are sequenced.

# PART VIII — PRACTICE

## 26. The Practice Methodology — How to Actually Drill
*Knowledge without deliberate practice is inert. The drills:*
- **One-variable shoots** — hold everything constant, vary one thing (light direction, focal length, pose principle); learn cause and effect cleanly
- **The reflector-only hour** — one subject, one hour, no other tools; learn fill and subtraction
- **Golden-hour repetition** — same location, many evenings; learn light's behavior over time
- **Reverse-engineering** — recreate a master's image; you learn more copying one McGinley than shooting 100 random frames
- **The cull discipline** — shoot, then ruthlessly select 1–3; selecting *is* developing taste
- **The signature spec, written** — one sentence, the world, the lineage, the grade, the refusals (per the Signature report §4); revisit and tighten quarterly
- **Critique** — your own and others'; articulate *why* an image works (analysis builds vision)
- **Volume with reflection** — the only path; thousands of frames, examined, not just taken

## 27. Phased Roadmap (0→100)

**Phase 0 — Literacy (months 0–2):** Exposure fluency, camera muscle memory, *Light Science and Magic* (first pass), *The Photographer's Eye*, Adams' *The Negative*. Drill exposure and AF until unconscious. No commercial work.

**Phase 1 — Light & seeing (months 2–6):** Natural-light mastery (the doctrine), composition deep, color/tonality, B&W discipline. Reflector-only and golden-hour drills. Begin reference boards for your lineage. Start defining the signature spec.

**Phase 2 — The figure (months 4–9, overlapping):** Anatomy, posing principles (Valenzuela), the psychology of the shoot. Tier 1 shoots as the training ground — low stakes, real subjects, building both craft and rapport skill. First coherent sets.

**Phase 3 — The darkroom & the look (months 6–12):** Color-managed workflow, grading, the signature preset, skin retouching with restraint. The grade becomes part of the signature. Begin Tier 2 (springs) as craft and confidence allow.

**Phase 4 — Authorship (months 9–18+):** Art history and theory deepen (the lineage, the gaze, the nude tradition); the body-of-work discipline; the treatment doctrine fully operational; Tier 3 as rapport earns it. Begin sequencing toward a first body of work / book. This phase never really ends — it's the mastery plateau where voice compounds.

**Mastery (years):** the slow compounding of voice, the book, the print, the name. Per the auteur report — measured in bodies of work, not months.

## The Irreducible Five
If everything above is overwhelming, the five that matter most for *your specific signature*, in order:
1. **Light** (*Light Science and Magic* + natural-light field hours) — your signature is light.
2. **Seeing/composition** (*The Photographer's Eye* + painting study) — the grammar.
3. **The figure & rapport** (Valenzuela + Tier 1 reps) — your subject and your moat.
4. **The grade** (your Adobe depth, aimed at skin and a signature look) — your head start.
5. **The canon & the gaze** (Berger, your lineage) — what makes you an author, not a shooter.

Everything else supports these five.
