## Dodge

The POV character. Name is already a verb, and the verb is evasion — a man who dodges is a man who will not say the thing.

**Core state:** limerence, not love. The distinction matters for the writing. Limerence is involuntary, intrusive, and feeds on ambiguity; it does not require reciprocation and is in fact sustained by its absence.

**The leash.** Bidirectional, and that's the trap:

> "She's on this big long leash that puts Dodge on a big long leash."

He has spent enormous amounts of money on her. That's not generosity in the scene — it's the leash paying out more line.

**Sensory wiring.** Dodge is acutely intuitive to physical stimulation and micro-mannerism. He reads bodies. This is the trait that makes him legible prey to Jane, who knows he reads bodies and leaves things for him to read.

## Carlie

*(rattlesnake · thriller)*

### Attachment

Trauma-based. Avoidant, disruptive.

### The Age Range

The central mechanic of the character. She is not one person across a scene:

> "Twenty-one in body and in some other utmost aspects, but at times in the worst of times, she's twelve. At the best of times, she's a fucking beast."

| State | Register | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Floor** | Twelve years old | regression under threat |
| **Baseline** | Twenty-one | volatile, combative |
| **Ceiling** | Command presence | Drummer, *The Expanse* |

**Reference triangulation:** Asuka Langley (*Evangelion*) — the armored abrasion covering a wound. Haruhi Suzumiya — the gravitational chaos everyone else orbits and accommodates. Drummer (*The Expanse*) — the peak state, total earned authority.

### Physicality

Reads tall. Long-legged. Thin. Outwardly, posturally venomous — the threat is carried in how she stands and moves, before she has said anything.

### Why Rattlesnake

> "She rattles her rattle before biting you."

She *announces*. The danger with Carlie is never a surprise — it's a countdown. Dodge always has warning and never takes it. That's the thriller register: dread of a known thing approaching.

## Jane

*(viper · terror)*

### Surface

Very petite. Subdued. Girl-next-door — **attainable**, which is the load-bearing word. Super cute. Mousey face.

> "She's a rat."

**Reference cluster:** Zooey Deschanel, wholesale. Ellen Page in *Juno*. The early-2010s self-aware meta manic-pixie-dream-girl — Reddit-literate, in on the joke, well before the e-girl turn. Lain, *Serial Experiments Lain*.

### The Mannerisms

Moe in the anime sense. Small, adorable, caricature-adjacent tics — the kind that read as love bait.

**The salad, canonical:**

> "She had a piece of salad in her mouth and was talking to me, but then someone showed up at the drive-through. Instead of picking the lettuce out of her mouth, she leaned all the way over and un-ate a piece of lettuce and put it back into the bowl. And this took about ten times longer than it would have to actually just —"

The excess time is the whole point. Ten times longer, for no reason, in front of him.

### The Trap Underneath

The mannerisms play as unintentional. Everyone reads them as unintentional.

> "It's all very unintentional, but it's very obvious to everyone else."

But Jane knows Dodge reads bodies. So the tells are *left out* — leaflets, deliberately dropped. The innocence is the delivery mechanism.

### Depth

Her depth is her whole thing. Layers, a lot of them. She can be surprising in a way that does not resolve comfortably.

### Why Viper — and Why Terror

No rattle. No countdown. She strikes, and the first information you get is that you've been struck. Terror rather than thriller because thriller requires anticipation, and Jane never grants any.

## The Inversion

The strongest structural asset in the cast, and worth building the scene on:

**Carlie looks dangerous and warns you. Jane looks safe and doesn't.**

Every visible signal is pointing the wrong direction. Dodge spends all his attention, money, and nervous system on the rattlesnake — the one who has been honest about what she is — while the actual strike comes from the petite one doing bits with lettuce.

Carlie's danger is loud and therefore survivable. Jane's is quiet and therefore not.
