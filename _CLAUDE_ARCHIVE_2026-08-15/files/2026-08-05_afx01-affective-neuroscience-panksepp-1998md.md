## CORE THESIS
Emotions are not learned, not constructed, and not cortical. They are seven genetically-built circuits sitting in the old subcortical brain — each with its own anatomy, its own chemistry, its own action pattern, and its own raw felt quality. All mammals run the same seven. What we call personality is nothing more than the individual tuning of those seven dials.

---

## INVARIANTS
*These are the unchanging principles — true regardless of context.*

1. **The circuits are built, not learned.** No one teaches a child to rage, to panic when a parent disappears in a crowd, or to play. Primary-process emotions are operational at birth and require zero conditioning.

2. **There are seven, and the list is closed by evidence, not preference.** SEEKING, RAGE, FEAR, LUST, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, PLAY. Panksepp explicitly refused to admit social dominance as a primary emotion — he classed it as acquired behavior. HUNGER got a full treatment but was filed as a homeostatic affect, not an emotional one.

3. **Emotion is subcortical and survives decortication.** Rats with the cortex surgically removed at birth still play, still fight, still raise litters. Humans born without a cerebral cortex still smile, still fuss, still show situationally appropriate pleasure and aversion. The cortex *regulates* emotion; it does not *generate* it.

4. **Every system carries a valence and a vector.** SEEKING, LUST, CARE and PLAY are pleasant and say *approach*. RAGE, FEAR and PANIC are aversive and say *avoid*. Animals will work to switch the pleasant circuits on and work to switch the aversive ones off. That's the empirical proof they're felt at all.

5. **The systems interact massively — they are not independent modules.** FEAR and RAGE both facilitate SEEKING. Nothing fires in isolation.

6. **The neocortex is close to a blank slate at birth and gets organized bottom-up.** Visual cortex in the early-blind takes on tactile and auditory work. Rewired ferrets grow vision in auditory cortex. Nothing up top is pre-specified the way the subcortical circuits are.

7. **Capitalization is a claim, not a typographic style.** SEEKING is a named neural system. "seeking" is an English word. Panksepp introduced the all-caps convention specifically so the two could never be confused again.

8. **Pathology is imbalance across the seven, not a generic chemical deficit.** Depression reads as diminished SEEKING plus an overactive PANIC/GRIEF — low positive affect matters as much as high negative affect.

9. **Discipline is not a circuit.** Big Five Conscientiousness shows no consistent association with any primary emotional system. It measures cortical inhibition — the lid over the drives, not a drive.

10. **Strong emotion shuts the cortex down.** During intense affect, cortical activation *decreases*. The thinking cap loses to the old brain when an evolutionarily significant cue lands.

## HEURISTICS
*Situational rules — true most of the time, in most contexts.*

| If... | Then... |
|---|---|
| You want to know which circuit is firing | Read the action pattern and the vocalization, not the label the person puts on it. The circuit is upstream of the story they tell about it. |
| A character is enraged | Find the thwarted want first. RAGE is what happens downstream when SEEKING gets blocked. Anger with no blocked appetite reads as noise. |
| A character is grieving vs. terrified | Use different chemistry. PANIC/GRIEF is separation distress and runs on opioids. FEAR is predator threat. They are separate circuits and should not share a performance. |
| A character stops playing | Something upstream is threatening them. PLAY requires safety, satiety, and no active FEAR. Loss of play is the earliest visible tell of a compromised environment. |
| You're writing addiction, obsession, or ambition | Aim at SEEKING, not pleasure. The circuit is about the pursuit, the anticipation, the forward lean — not the having. Satisfaction actually turns it *off*. |
| A character is over-controlled | You're stacking cortical inhibition on top of a hot circuit. Name the circuit underneath, or the control reads as blankness rather than tension. |
| An emotional beat feels generic | Check whether you've written the *tertiary* version (the thought about the feeling) instead of the *primary* one (the feeling). |
| Two characters won't strike sparks | Their dominant circuits are compatible. Put a high-RAGE baseline against a high-CARE baseline, or high-SEEKING against high-FEAR. |
| You need a memory to stick for a character | Attach it to a strong affect. All long-term memory carries an emotional component — but traumatic intensity can *reduce* retrieval and even produce amnesia. |
| You're writing a child or an animal | They have full primary affect without necessarily having the ability to reflect on it. Raw feeling, no commentary track. |

## CORE CONCEPTS

### 1. The Seven Primary-Process Systems
The spine of the entire framework. Each is a distinct circuit with distinct chemistry and a distinct behavioral signature.

| System | Common name | What it does | Anatomy (core) | Chemistry (core) | Signature behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SEEKING** | Expectancy / wanting | Drives exploration, foraging, anticipation, enthusiasm; the engine that powers the other six | Mesolimbic/mesocortical dopamine tract; VTA → nucleus accumbens; medial forebrain bundle | Dopamine (primary), glutamate, neuropeptides | Forward-leaning sniffing, investigating, pursuit |
| **RAGE** | Anger | Defends resources and body; responds to restraint and to frustration | PAG → medial hypothalamus → medial amygdala, in *decreasing* order of importance | Substance P, glutamate, acetylcholine | Affective attack, snarl, bite, lunge |
| **FEAR** | Anxiety | Escapes and avoids bodily danger | Central/lateral amygdala → anterior/medial hypothalamus → dorsal PAG | Glutamate, CRF, DBI; reduced by benzodiazepines | Freeze, flight, hiding |
| **LUST** | Sexual desire | Identifies mates, drives reproduction | Preoptic hypothalamus, amygdala, related hypothalamic zones | Testosterone, estrogen, vasopressin, oxytocin | Courtship, pursuit, mating patterns |
| **CARE** | Nurturance | Maternal/parental tending; the root of love | Anterior cingulate, BNST, preoptic area, VTA, PAG | Oxytocin, prolactin, dopamine, opioids | Grooming, retrieval, protection, tending |
| **PANIC/GRIEF** | Separation distress / sadness | Alarms on social loss; the engine of attachment | PAG → dorsomedial thalamus → ventral septum → anterior cingulate | Endogenous opioids, oxytocin, prolactin (all *reduce* it); CRF (raises it) | Distress vocalization, crying, searching, withdrawal |
| **PLAY** | Social joy | Builds social skill, tests limits, generates laughter | Parafascicular/intralaminar thalamus, PAG, dorsomedial diencephalon, striatum, frontal cortex | Opioids, dopamine, cannabinoids | Rough-and-tumble, chasing, pinning, laughter |

> **Note on the RAGE hierarchy.** Fernandez de Molina & Hunsperger (1962) showed the layering cleanly: lesion the hypothalamus or PAG and amygdala-evoked aggression disappears; lesion the PAG and hypothalamus-evoked aggression disappears; PAG-evoked aggression depends on neither of the higher two. The oldest layer is the load-bearing one. This is the general architectural pattern of the whole system.

### 2. The Three-Level Nested BrainMind
Panksepp's answer to "where does thought fit."

- **Primary process** — subcortical, innate, unconditioned. The seven circuits. Raw affect.
- **Secondary process** — learning and memory. Basal ganglia. Classical and operant conditioning wraps new stimuli onto the primary circuits. Each circuit carries its own reward/punishment learning system.
- **Tertiary process** — neocortical. Thought, language, planning, rumination, the story you tell about the feeling.

The levels are nested and bidirectional. The cortex builds a seesaw relationship with the circuits below it — weighing whether an event should trigger or inhibit a primary emotion. But when the circuit fires hard enough, it overrides the top.

### 3. SEEKING as the Master System
The most consequential single concept in the book for character work. SEEKING is *appetitive*, not *consummatory* — it's the wanting, the looking-forward, the enthusiasm, not the satisfaction. Panksepp's own dissertation caught it accidentally: stimulating one site produced "affective attack" the rat would work to switch **off**, while stimulating another produced a quiet predatory bite the rat would work to switch **on**. The second turned out to be SEEKING, not aggression at all. Deep brain stimulation of the medial forebrain bundle — the most rewarding site in the SEEKING system — produced recovery in six of seven treatment-resistant depression patients sustained across a 33-week study.

### 4. The Opioid Hypothesis of Social Attachment
Panksepp noticed that opiate withdrawal and heartbreak look the same, and that opioid addicts disproportionately came from thin social backgrounds. Low doses of morphine soothed separation-distress vocalizations in puppies. He published the hypothesis in 1980: brain opioids underlie the formation of social bonds. Social pain and physical pain share machinery. This is why loss *hurts* and why the metaphor isn't a metaphor.

### 5. PLAY, 50-kHz Chirps, and Rat Laughter
PLAY produces measurable, frequency-modulated 50-kHz ultrasonic vocalizations in juvenile rats — which Panksepp read as social joy. Two downstream results matter. First, right-frontal-lesioned rats became *more* playful and hyperactive (a rodent ADHD model), and seven days of one-hour "play therapy" damped both — implying PLAY builds frontal-lobe inhibition. Second, breeding rats for high 50-kHz chirping and screening their expressed genes led to rapastinel/GLYX-13, a fast-acting antidepressant. A drug derived from measuring how much rats laugh.

### 6. The Decortication Evidence
The hardest empirical leg of the argument. Neonatally decorticated rats: normal posture, normal grooming, normal weight, normal reproduction, most defensive behavior intact, play indistinguishable. Sixteen graduate students watched a decorticated pup and a sham-surgery control for thirty minutes and *most picked the intact control* as the decorticated one. Meanwhile, far smaller subcortical lesions were devastating — parafascicular thalamic lesions cut pinning by 73%.

### 7. The ANPS (Affective Neuroscience Personality Scales)
Panksepp's testable prediction: the primary emotions are the foundation of personality. The ANPS measures self-reported activation of six of the seven — **LUST is deliberately excluded** because it wrecks valid responding through social desirability. Validated in ten languages. The mapping onto Big Five:

| ANPS system | Big Five relationship |
|---|---|
| PLAY | Extraversion |
| SEEKING | Openness to Experience |
| CARE (high) + RAGE (low) | Agreeableness — effectively a fused "love–hate" scale |
| FEAR + RAGE + PANIC | All three collapsed onto low Emotional Stability |
| — | Conscientiousness maps to **no** primary emotion |

The implication cuts one direction only: Big Five traits are *higher-order configurations* of the more elemental circuits, not the other way around. Agreeableness is two circuits welded together. Emotional Stability is three.

### 8. Anoetic / Noetic / Autonoetic (Tulving's ladder)
Most mammals combine *anoetic* consciousness (feeling without knowing) and *noetic* (knowing), without reaching *autonoetic* (holding experience in memory well enough to review the past and anticipate the future). Panksepp's framing: most animals run on "intentions-in-action" rather than "intentions to act." Raw affect does not require a self to observe it.

## NOTABLE QUOTES

> "the neocortex is fundamentally tabula rasa at birth"
> — Panksepp & Biven, *The Archaeology of Mind* (2012), p. 427

*The most load-bearing claim in the framework. If true, everything specific about a mind was built from below.*

> "To feel is to do something."
> — Susanne Langer, *Mind: An Essay on Human Feeling* (1988), p. 7 — quoted approvingly by Davis & Montag as the framework's thesis in six words

*Emotion is motor before it is mental. "Emotion" derives from* emovere *— to move out.*

> "such debates may simply reflect investigators working at different levels of control"
> — Panksepp (2010), p. 536

*His late-career olive branch to the constructivists: basic-emotion theory holds subcortically, constructivism holds cortically, and the fight was a level-confusion.*

## WHAT DOESN'T WORK
Treating emotions as socially constructed or learned; treating the cortex as the seat of feeling; using fMRI to hunt primary affects (blood-flow imaging suits large, fast-firing cortical networks, not the small, slow-firing subcortical neurons that actually generate emotion — PET is the better instrument); admitting social dominance as a primary emotion; conflating FEAR with PANIC/GRIEF; conflating SEEKING with pleasure or consummation; treating Big Five factors as bedrock rather than as composites; expecting Conscientiousness to map to a circuit; assuming primary affect requires self-reflection; assuming the neocortex arrived with pre-installed specializations.

## QUICK SELF-CHECK
- [ ] For each character, can I name which of the seven systems runs hottest at baseline?
- [ ] Can I name which one runs coldest — the deficit, not just the excess?
- [ ] When a character rages, have I shown the blocked want that preceded it?
- [ ] Am I distinguishing FEAR (threat) from PANIC/GRIEF (loss) in performance, chemistry, and staging?
- [ ] Does anyone in this world still PLAY? If not, what shut it off — and do I know?
- [ ] Am I writing primary affect, or am I writing the tertiary commentary about the affect?
- [ ] Have I separated a character's *drive* (circuit) from their *control* (cortical lid)?
- [ ] Does any relationship in this story run on opioid-grade attachment — where separation reads as withdrawal?

## BVX EXTRACTION LAYER
*Proposed template extension — v2.1. Flagged for approval; not yet locked into the spec.*

**Model 1 — The Seven-Dial Sheet.** Every character gets seven values, high/mid/low. That's the whole instrument. It is pre-narrative, pre-astrological, pre-Dramatica — it sits underneath the LEECHSEED pipeline rather than competing with it, because it describes what the body wants before anything decides what the story wants. Seven dials, three positions each, gives 2,187 distinct baselines before you name a single character.

**Model 2 — Wound = a mistrained circuit.** Backstory stops being biography and becomes an answer to one question: *which circuit got wired wrong, and by what?* An overactive PANIC baseline reads as abandonment. A suppressed PLAY circuit reads as an environment that punished joy. A hijacked SEEKING reads as a character who cannot stop pursuing something that will never satisfy.

**Model 3 — Conflict by circuit collision.** Cast antagonism at the circuit level rather than the opinion level. High-RAGE against high-CARE. High-SEEKING against high-FEAR. High-PLAY against a system that has no PLAY at all — which is, structurally, the shape of a student body against an administration.

**Model 4 — Arc = a baseline that moves.** A character arc is one dial shifting position permanently. Not a lesson learned — a setting changed. This gives arcs a falsifiable definition and a way to check them across a long structure, which is the specific cohesion problem the mapping work exists to solve.

**Model 5 — Drive/lid separation.** Because Conscientiousness maps to no circuit, control and drive are independently settable. A character can be high-RAGE + high-lid (the still one everyone is afraid of) or low-RAGE + low-lid (loud, harmless). Four quadrants per circuit, free of charge.

**Model 6 — Scene-level circuit check.** In any given beat: which circuit is firing, and does the dialogue match it? Match = plain scene. Mismatch = subtext. Character says the CARE line while the RAGE circuit is lit — that's the whole engine of a certain kind of scene, and it's now specifiable rather than intuited.

**Model 7 — World-level PLAY reading.** PLAY shuts off under FEAR. If Year 1 of The Academy is a clean sync-cult and Year 2 is neon rot, the PLAY circuit is the diagnostic instrument for the transition — track *what stops being funny* across the Hardware Gate and the rot becomes measurable rather than atmospheric.

**Open question for the Quartermaster log:** whether the seven-dial sheet is a BVX methodology capture or an Outliers-specific tool. Per the car-and-cargo rule it stays parked until it's actually been used to build a character in two distinct instances.

## COMPREHENSIVE GLOSSARY
*Alphabetical. Section references in brackets.*

**Affective Neuroscience** — The research field Panksepp named (1991–92): the cross-species study of the neural constitution of raw emotional experience, using electrical brain stimulation, pharmacological challenge, and lesion work rather than self-report. *[Core Thesis]* — Usage: the field name, not a book title, though the 1998 book carries the same name.

**Anoetic consciousness** — Feeling without knowing. Raw affect with no self-reflective observer. Tulving's bottom rung. *[Concept 8]* — Usage: what an infant or a rat has when it's in distress.

**ANPS (Affective Neuroscience Personality Scales)** — Self-report instrument measuring six of the seven systems in humans; validated in ten languages. LUST omitted. *[Concept 7]* — Usage: the empirical bridge between Panksepp's circuits and mainstream personality psychology.

**Appetitive vs. consummatory** — The distinction that defines SEEKING. Appetitive = pursuit, anticipation, the forward lean. Consummatory = the having. SEEKING is entirely the former. *[Concept 3]* — Usage: why satisfaction ends the drive instead of fulfilling it.

**Autonoetic consciousness** — Tulving's top rung: holding experience in memory well enough to review the past and anticipate the future. Probably human-specific. *[Concept 8]*

**CARE** — The nurturance circuit. Oxytocin, prolactin, dopamine, opioids; anterior cingulate, BNST, preoptic area. The root of what humans call love. *[Concept 1]*

**Decortication** — Surgical removal of the neocortex. The central experimental evidence that primary emotion is subcortical: neonatally decorticated rats play, fight, groom and raise young essentially normally. *[Invariant 3, Concept 6]*

**DBS (Deep Brain Stimulation)** — Therapeutic application of the ESB method in humans. Medial forebrain bundle stimulation produced sustained recovery in 6 of 7 treatment-resistant depression patients. *[Concept 3]*

**ESB (Electrical Stimulation of the Brain)** — Panksepp's core method. Introduce a crude unstructured electrical stimulus; if it reliably evokes both (a) a coherent emotional action pattern and (b) a verifiably pleasant or aversive affect state, you've hit an innate evolutionarily-organized circuit. *[Concept 3, Invariant 4]* — Usage: the reason the seven-system list is an empirical finding rather than a taxonomy.

**FEAR** — The threat-avoidance circuit. Amygdala → hypothalamus → dorsal PAG; glutamate and CRF; damped by benzodiazepines. Distinct from PANIC/GRIEF. *[Concept 1, Heuristic 3]*

**Fifty-kHz chirps** — Frequency-modulated ultrasonic vocalizations emitted by juvenile rats during rough-and-tumble play; Panksepp's index of social joy, and the screening measure that eventually produced rapastinel. *[Concept 5]*

**Homeostatic affect** — Body-state feelings such as HUNGER and THIRST. Panksepp treated them in detail but kept them categorically separate from the seven emotional affects. *[Invariant 2]*

**LUST** — The sexual desire circuit. Steroid- and neuropeptide-driven (testosterone, estrogen, vasopressin, oxytocin); preoptic hypothalamus. Excluded from the ANPS on social-desirability grounds. *[Concept 1, Concept 7]*

**Medial forebrain bundle** — The most rewarding stimulation site in the SEEKING system; the DBS target that outperformed nucleus accumbens placement in treatment-resistant depression. *[Concept 3]*

**Noetic consciousness** — Tulving's middle rung: knowing. Between raw feeling and autobiographical self-awareness. *[Concept 8]*

**Opioid hypothesis** — Panksepp et al. (1980): endogenous brain opioids underlie the formation of social attachments and modulate social emotion. Origin of the claim that social loss and physical pain share machinery. *[Concept 4]* — Usage: why heartbreak and withdrawal present alike.

**PAG (Periaqueductal gray)** — Midbrain structure, the deepest and most load-bearing layer of RAGE, FEAR, PANIC/GRIEF and PLAY. Lesion it and the higher layers stop working; lesion the higher layers and it keeps working. *[Concept 1]*

**PANIC/GRIEF** — The separation-distress circuit. Runs on endogenous opioids, oxytocin and prolactin. Panksepp's model of depression sits here. Not to be confused with FEAR. *[Concept 1, Invariant 8]*

**PLAY** — The social-joy circuit. Rough-and-tumble; builds social competence and frontal-lobe inhibition. Shuts off under FEAR or hunger. *[Concept 1, Concept 5]*

**Primary process** — The bottom level: innate subcortical circuits, no learning required. Where the seven live. *[Concept 2]*

**RAGE** — The anger circuit. PAG → medial hypothalamus → medial amygdala. Triggered by restraint and by frustrated SEEKING. *[Concept 1, Heuristic 2]*

**Rapastinel (GLYX-13)** — Antidepressant derived from screening rats bred for high 50-kHz play vocalization. Elevated mood within two hours, effects lasting at least seven days. The framework's most concrete validation. *[Concept 5]*

**Secondary process** — The middle level: learning and memory, basal ganglia, conditioning that attaches new stimuli to the primary circuits. *[Concept 2]*

**SEEKING** — The master appetitive circuit. Mesolimbic dopamine, VTA → nucleus accumbens, medial forebrain bundle. Powers exploration, enthusiasm, anticipation — and the other six systems. Originally labeled "Expectancy." *[Concept 3]*

**Seesaw relationship** — Panksepp's term for the reciprocal cortical–subcortical dynamic: the cortex weighs whether an event should trigger or inhibit a primary emotion; imbalance in either direction becomes dysfunction. *[Concept 2]*

**Social dominance** — Explicitly *rejected* as a primary emotion. Panksepp judged the evidence insufficient and treated it as acquired behavior. *[Invariant 2]* — Usage: relevant when adjacent frameworks treat hierarchy as innate; Panksepp would not.

**Tabula rasa (of neocortex)** — The claim that the neocortex arrives essentially unprogrammed and is organized by experience and by subcortical guidance. *[Invariant 6, Notable Quotes]*

**Tertiary process** — The top level: neocortex, thought, language, rumination. The story about the feeling, not the feeling. *[Concept 2]*

**VTA (Ventral tegmental area)** — Dopaminergic origin of the mesolimbic tract; the source end of SEEKING. *[Concept 1, Concept 3]*
