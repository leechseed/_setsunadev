## TL;DR
- **Yes** — at least three mayfly species (Order Ephemeroptera) carry the species epithet *pescadori* in honor of Manuel L. Pescador: ***Jubabaetis pescadori*** Müller‑Liebenau, 1980 (Baetidae); ***Sulu pescadori*** Grant & Peters, 1993 (Leptophlebiidae); and ***Dudgeodes pescadori*** Sartori, 2008 (Teloganodidae). All three are Philippine mayflies, and at least the first and third were based on collections Pescador himself made on Luzon.
- **No genus** has been named after Pescador. The genera *Jubabaetis*, *Sulu*, and *Dudgeodes* honor others (the last is named for David Dudgeon, Emeritus Professor of Ecology & Biodiversity at the University of Hong Kong); Pescador is honored only at the species level.
- **No taxa outside Ephemeroptera** (e.g., Trichoptera, Plecoptera, Coleoptera) appear to be named after him. The patronyms cluster around his Philippine mayfly material and his lifelong specialization on mayflies.

## Details

### The Three Patronymic Mayflies

**1. *Jubabaetis pescadori* Müller‑Liebenau, 1980 (Baetidae).** Ingrid Müller‑Liebenau described both the genus *Jubabaetis* and its type species *J. pescadori* from a small but distinctive collection of baetid nymphs that Manuel L. Pescador had made on the island of North Luzon, Philippines. The original chapter explicitly states that "*Jubabaetis* gen.n. collected by M.L. Pescador from the Philippines represents a more or less isolated genus within the family" and that "the two new genera of Baetidae, *Jubabaetis* gen.n. and *Platybaetis* gen.n. discussed herein are contained in a small collection made by Dr. M.L. Pescador on the island of North Luzon (Philippines)." The species epithet honors the collector. *Jubabaetis* remains a small, distinctive Oriental baetid genus, with most subsequent records (e.g., from India, Borneo) reinforcing its scarcity.

**2. *Sulu pescadori* Grant & Peters, 1993 (Leptophlebiidae).** Paul M. Grant and William L. Peters described the genus *Sulu* (a reference to the Sulu region of the Philippines) as one of four new Thraulus‑group genera in a substantial revision of Eastern‑Hemisphere leptophlebiids. *Sulu pescadori* is the type species and is a Philippine endemic. Grant & Peters were long‑time collaborators of Pescador at the Florida A&M Laboratory of Aquatic Entomology, making the dedication a clear in‑group honor. The species and genus are catalogued today on Wikidata (Q14419977), GBIF (taxon key 6934069), and IRMNG (11569424). The species is still classified within Leptophlebiidae: Atalophlebiinae, and the genus *Sulu* (Grant & Peters, 1993) is recognized in current Leptophlebiidae checklists.

**3. *Dudgeodes pescadori* Sartori, 2008 (Teloganodidae).** Michel Sartori, with co‑authors Janice G. Peters and Michael D. Hubbard (both of the Florida A&M Laboratory of Aquatic Entomology — Pescador's own institution), erected the new genus *Dudgeodes* (named for David Dudgeon, the prominent Hong Kong freshwater ecologist now Emeritus Professor of Ecology & Biodiversity at the University of Hong Kong) to accommodate several Southeast Asian species, and designated *Dudgeodes pescadori* as the type species. The paratype material was collected in 1977 from Luzon, Philippines, and the Pescador‑led Luzon collections are the historical foundation of this taxon. A 2020 integrative revision (Garces, Sartori & Freitag, *ZooKeys* 910: 93–129, DOI 10.3897/zookeys.910.48659) re‑examined *D. pescadori* alongside six new Philippine species and confirmed that, until then, "only *Dudgeodes pescadori* from Luzon island has been reported" from the Philippines.

### Authoritative Source: Ephemeroptera Galactica

The most authoritative single source is the **"Patronyms of Mayfly Workers"** compendium compiled by Michel Sartori (Museum of Zoology, Lausanne) and Luke M. Jacobus (Indiana University–Purdue University Columbus), maintained on the Ephemeroptera Galactica web site hosted by Florida A&M University (ephemeroptera-galactica.com/patronyms.php; last modified 23 July 2020). Under the heading **"Manuel (Manny) Pescador"** the list reads:

> *Jubabaetis pescadori* Müller‑Liebenau, 1980 (Baetidae, Philippines)
> *Sulu pescadori* Grant & Peters, 1993 (Leptophlebiidae, Philippines)
> *Dudgeodes pescadori* Sartori, 2008 (Teloganodidae, Philippines)

These are the only three mayfly patronyms recognised for him by the compilers, who are themselves leading mayfly taxonomists.

### Distinguishing Honored Taxa from Pescador's Own Authored Taxa

It is important not to conflate species **honoring** Pescador with species **authored** by Pescador. Pescador, often in collaboration with William L. and Janice G. Peters, has described numerous mayfly taxa — for example *Homoeoneuria alleni* Pescador & Peters, 1980 (named for Richard Allen); *Demoulinellus* Pescador & Peters, 1982 (named for Georges Demoulin); *Gonserellus* Pescador, 1997 (named for Tom Gonser); and *Rianilaneuria diminuta* Pescador & Peters (a Madagascan oligoneuriid). None of these honor Pescador himself.

Three additional points clarify the picture:
- **No genus or family-group name** has been erected to honor Pescador. The generic names containing his species epithets (*Jubabaetis*, *Sulu*, *Dudgeodes*) honor other people or refer to geography.
- **All three patronyms are Philippine.** This reflects Pescador's pioneering 1970s field work on the Philippine mayfly fauna; he and Hubbard published a *Catalog of the Ephemeroptera of the Philippines* in 1978 (*Pacific Insects* 19: 91–99), and the resulting collections seeded subsequent descriptions by Müller‑Liebenau, Grant & Peters, and Sartori.
- **No South American taxon has been named for him**, despite his major contribution to South American Leptophlebiidae and to the 2006 monograph *Ephemeroptera of South America* (Pensoft Press, Sofia and Moscow, 2006, 650 pp.; series: Aquatic Biodiversity in Latin America, Vol. 2) co-authored with Domínguez, Molineri, Hubbard, and Nieto.

### Search for Non-Mayfly Patronyms

Targeted searches across Trichoptera (caddisflies), Plecoptera (stoneflies), Coleoptera, and the general "pescadori" / "named in honor of Manuel Pescador" literature returned **no non-mayfly taxa** named after him. The Ephemeroptera Galactica patronym list is taxonomically narrow, but the supplementary checks (Plazi TreatmentBank, Wikispecies, Wikidata) likewise found no matches outside Ephemeroptera. Given Pescador's research life devoted entirely to mayflies, this is consistent.

## Recommendations

1. **For citation and biographical use, treat the answer as three confirmed mayfly species:** *Jubabaetis pescadori* Müller‑Liebenau, 1980; *Sulu pescadori* Grant & Peters, 1993; *Dudgeodes pescadori* Sartori, 2008. Use the Ephemeroptera Galactica patronyms page as the primary tertiary source and cite the three original taxonomic papers (DOIs above where available) as the primary sources.
2. **Do not claim a genus** has been named after Pescador. The relevant generic names (*Jubabaetis*, *Sulu*, *Dudgeodes*) honor others; if a definitive answer about generic patronyms is required, retain that nuance.
3. **If verbatim etymology statements are needed** (for example, for a Wikipedia article or a biographical sketch), obtain PDFs of (a) Müller‑Liebenau 1980 (Springer chapter, DOI 10.1007/978‑1‑4613‑3066‑0_8), (b) Grant & Peters 1993 (*Trans. Am. Ent. Soc.* 119(2): 131–168, available through BioOne/JSTOR or interlibrary loan), and (c) Sartori, Peters & Hubbard 2008 (*Zootaxa* 1957, openly available at biotaxa.org / mapress.com). The Grant & Peters 1993 paper is the only one of the three not openly accessible online.
4. **Trigger for re-checking:** if a future taxonomic paper formally erects a *Pescadorina*, *Pescadorella*, or similar genus-level patronym (or names a species in his honor in another order), the answer above should be updated. As of May 2026, no such taxon has appeared in the indexed taxonomic literature.

## Caveats

- The verbatim "Etymology:" sentences from the three original descriptions could not all be retrieved through open online sources. The dedication is, however, unambiguous because the world's leading mayfly taxonomists (Sartori, Jacobus) explicitly list these three species under Manuel L. Pescador on the standard patronyms reference, and the Müller‑Liebenau 1980 chapter directly states that the type material was collected by Pescador.
- The *Sulu pescadori* original description (Grant & Peters 1993) is in a print journal that is not freely available online; the patronym attribution to Pescador rests on the Sartori–Jacobus compendium and the close professional collaboration between W.L. Peters and Pescador at Florida A&M.
- "Pescador" is a relatively common Spanish surname meaning "fisherman," so a casual web search for "pescadori" will also surface unrelated uses (e.g., a Louisiana beer named "Pescador"). The three taxa identified here are the only ones for which the epithet is documented as honoring **Manuel L. Pescador**.
- No subspecies, varieties, or fossil taxa named after Pescador were found.
