# The "28 Female Body Archetypes" System: A Knowledge-Base Investigation

## TL;DR
- **No documented system of 28 female body archetypes from any actual haute couture house, atelier, or bespoke-tailoring tradition exists in the published literature** — the "haute couture lineage" framing of the question is, as far as I can verify, a misattribution.
- The only system whose arithmetic lands cleanly on 28 is the **Kitchener/Truth-is-Beauty style-essence lineage**: 7 pure essences + the 21 named two-way blends enumerated by Rachel Nachmias on truth-is-beauty.com sum to exactly 28 archetypes — but this descends from American **image-consulting** (Belle Northrup, who published "Art and Personality in Clothing Selection" in *Art Education Today*, Teachers College, Columbia University, 1936 → Harriet McJimsey → John Kitchener → David Kibbe → Rachel Nachmias), not from couture.
- The closest neighbouring expanded typologies are **Kibbe's 13 image identities** (1987), **Trinny & Susannah's 12 shapes** (Body Shape Bible, 2007), **Dwyn Larson's 20 Types of Beauty**, and **Ellie-Jean Royden's Body Matrix of 27 types** (3 widths × 3 lengths × 3 shapes) — none of which are couture in origin.

## 1. The Verdict Up Front

After targeted searches in English and French for "28 morphotypes," "28 silhouettes haute couture," "morphologie 28 types," and "28 archetypes corps féminin," and after surveying the major couture houses (Worth, Dior, Balenciaga, Chanel, Schiaparelli, Madame Grès, Vionnet, Charles James, YSL, Givenchy), the Chambre Syndicale, and the principal pattern-drafting traditions (ESMOD, Bunka, École de la Chambre Syndicale, Helen Joseph-Armstrong, Aldrich, McCunn), **no haute-couture, atelier, corsetière, or essayeuse tradition is documented as classifying women into 28 distinct body archetypes**. None of the canonical pattern-drafting textbooks teach a 28-type taxonomy; French ateliers historically work with the 5–6 silhouette letters (A, H, V, X, O, 8); ESMOD and Bunka adjust block patterns using drop-types and posture inclination, not archetypes; and the Chambre Syndicale's haute couture regulations concern atelier staffing and seasonal collections, not body classification.

The "28" almost certainly arises from a different, parallel lineage — **personal-style and image-consulting theory** — that has, on TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, and Etsy, been increasingly (and inaccurately) marketed as if it came from couture. The single system whose arithmetic produces 28 is the **Kitchener seven-essence framework**, when one counts the 7 pure essences plus the 21 unordered two-way blends (7 + C(7,2) = 7 + 21 = 28). This is the form most fully developed by Rachel Nachmias on her Truth-is-Beauty site, whose "Blends of 2 Types" page lists exactly 21 named two-way archetypes.

## 2. Why the Couture Provenance Is Almost Certainly a Myth

Three observations support this:

**First, the couture houses' own measurement traditions are person-specific, not archetypal.** Bespoke ateliers — including those that survive on Savile Row's women's side, at Maison Cléone, Kathryn Sargent, and The Deck London — draft a pattern from one client's measurements rather than slotting her into a numbered type. As Cutting Room Bespoke puts it, "in any single size, there can be up to 15 different body types" — but this is a critique of ready-to-wear sizing, not a taxonomy of 15 (let alone 28) named archetypes.

**Second, the historic essayeuse/première d'atelier role classifies posture and corrections (erect, stooped, sloping shoulder, prominent bust, sway-back, etc.), not whole-body archetypes.** Modern 3D body-scanning ateliers such as The Sages Bespoke describe shoulder slope, back curvature, and chest projection as adjustable parameters, again on a per-person basis.

**Third, where archetypal thinking does appear in fashion, the numbers are 5, 7, 12, 13, 20, or 27 — not 28.** NOMA's "A Queen Within: Adorned Archetypes" exhibition (New Orleans Museum of Art, 21 February–28 May 2018, developed by Barrett Barrera Projects and MUSEEA) uses **seven archetypal personality types: Mother Earth, Sage, Magician, Enchantress, Explorer, Heroine, and Thespian**. Kibbe codified 13 image identities in *Metamorphosis* (1987); Trinny & Susannah codified 12; Dwyn Larson lists 20 for women; Ellie-Jean Royden's Body Matrix codifies 27 (3×3×3).

The likeliest explanation is that someone — probably an online style consultant or social-media educator — combined the **7 Kitchener essences** with their **21 two-way blends** to produce a 28-cell matrix, then dressed the marketing in the language of "haute couture lineage" because the underlying yin/yang vocabulary was originally seeded by textile professor Belle Northrup in her 1936 article "Art and Personality in Clothing Selection" in *Art Education Today* (Teachers College, Columbia University) and elaborated by Harriet Tilden McJimsey (a home-economics academic) first in *Art in Clothing Selection* (Harper & Row, 1963) and then in its retitled second edition *Art and Fashion in Clothing Selection* (Iowa State University Press, 1973). That lineage is real, but it is academic dress theory and image consulting — not couture.

## 3. The Most Probable "28": Kitchener Essences + Two-Way Blends (Truth-is-Beauty)

### 3.1 Lineage

- **Belle Northrup**, textile and clothing professor at Teachers College, Columbia University, introduced yin/yang as an analytical frame for dress in her 1936 article "Art and Personality in Clothing Selection" in *Art Education Today*.
- **Grace Margaret Morton** and **Harriet Tilden McJimsey** extended it; McJimsey's *Art in Clothing Selection* (1963), retitled *Art and Fashion in Clothing Selection* (1973), codified six archetypes — Dramatic, Athletic (Natural Yang), Classic, Romantic, Gamine, and Ingenue — and explicitly stated that "it is possible and more probable that an individual will possess some ingenue or gamin qualities than that she would be a typical gamin or ingenue."
- **Personal Style Counselors (PSC)** was founded in California in **1964 by Joan Songer**, who handed the firm to **John Kitchener** in 1999 (Kitchener had joined in 1978 and has run PSC's analyses for men and women since). It is the oldest continuously operating service of its kind, with over 200,000 clients internationally. In 1987, while working in the Washington, D.C. area, Kitchener "discovered the Angelic (or ethereal) Style Essence which was the missing link or overlooked facet in style identification," adding the seventh essence to the lineage. Kitchener also pioneered expressing a client's identity as a **percentage blend** of essences rather than a single label.
- **David Kibbe** (NYC, *Metamorphosis*, 1987) created a closed system of 13 archetypes drawn from McJimsey's six (he discarded Ingenue and reorganised the yin/yang spectrum).
- **Dwyn Larson**, an American artist who could not place herself in Kibbe, spent 14 years analysing women's faces and figures and published her own 20-type system for women (32 total including men).
- **Rachel Nachmias**, of Truth-is-Beauty (Philadelphia), built directly on Kitchener: she retained the seven essences, but to handle real human variation she enumerates 21 two-way blends (e.g., "The Vamp" = Romantic-Dramatic, "Aphrodite" = Romantic-Ethereal, "The Sorceress" = Ethereal-Dramatic) and 35 three-way blends, each given an evocative archetype name and a Pinterest mood board.

### 3.2 Why this produces 28

If one stops at two-way blends — which is where almost all popular style content stops — the arithmetic is clean:

$$\binom{7}{1} + \binom{7}{2} = 7 + 21 = 28$$

These 28 are the most commonly referenced level in online style discourse. Add the 35 three-way blends and the full taxonomy is 63 identities; Truth-is-Beauty does not itself headline "28," but the 7-pure-plus-21-paired tier is what almost every YouTube and TikTok creator extracts when illustrating "all the types." It is, in our judgement, the strongest single referent for the user's question.

## 4. The Underlying Axes Every Candidate System Shares

Every expanded "couture-adjacent" system in this lineage rests on the same three or four axes:

- **Yin / Yang balance** — the McJimsey/Northrup axis, with yang denoting sharp, angular, vertical, elongated, dignified qualities and yin denoting soft, round, small, gentle, flowing qualities. *Where* yin or yang predominates (bone structure, body flesh, facial flesh, facial features) is what produces the type.
- **Bone structure** — narrow vs. wide, sharp vs. blunt. Couture-influenced systems treat bone structure as the immovable foundation; flesh and weight are read against it.
- **Vertical line / scale** — does the figure read tall and elongated, compact and petite, or balanced? This is the height-equivalent axis Kibbe emphasised.
- **Flesh distribution and contour** — taut and lean (Dramatic), muscled and broad (Natural), smooth and balanced (Classic), small and snappy (Gamine), lush and curved (Romantic), youthful and rounded (Ingenue), waif-like and translucent (Ethereal).

Ellie-Jean Royden's Body Matrix collapses these into a three-axis 27-cell cube: **Width (Narrow/Medium/Wide) × Length (Short/Medium/Long) × Shape (Straight/Medium/Round)**. The Kitchener-based 28 is conceptually similar but its axis is the single yin/yang spectrum with seven named anchor points and pairwise blends.

## 5. Detailed Enumeration of the 28 (Truth-is-Beauty Reconstruction)

### The 7 Pure Essences (silhouette characteristics, neighbour distinctions, styling implications)

**1. Dramatic (extreme yang).** Tall vertical line; long narrow limbs; angular, sharp, narrow bone structure; hands and feet long and narrow; taut, lean flesh; sharp jaw, prominent cheekbones, thin lips, almond eyes. The Dramatic differs from the Natural in being *sharper, narrower, and more vertically elongated* (Natural is broader and blunter), and from the Ethereal in being *intense and commanding* rather than delicate. Styling: long unbroken vertical lines, monochrome, structured tailoring, geometric sharp-edged details, stiff and weighty fabrics.

**2. Natural (relaxed yang).** Broad, blunt, slightly angular bone structure; wide shoulders; athletic, even musculature; longer face, square or soft-square jaw, broader lips. Differs from Dramatic by being *blunter and wider*, from Classic by being *less symmetrical and more relaxed*. Styling: unconstructed silhouettes, soft tailoring, asymmetry, natural-fibre textures (linen, suede, wool), earth tones.

**3. Gamine (playful, contrasting yin-yang).** Short, compact frame; small bone structure but with sharp features; high contrast between sharpness of feature and smallness of scale; mischievous, boyish look. Differs from Ingenue (which is round-soft, not sharp-small) and from Dramatic (which is tall, not compact). Styling: short, snappy lines; mixed prints; menswear borrowings; whimsical accessories; high-contrast colour blocking.

**4. Classic (balanced yin-yang).** Symmetrical, even, "no feature outweighs another"; oval face; straight nose; even bust-to-hip ratio; medium height and bone structure. The "balance point" of the spectrum. Styling: timeless tailoring, clean lines, neutral palette, formal elegance, restrained details.

**5. Romantic (deep yin).** Curvy, voluptuous, hourglass body with double-curved silhouette; soft cheeks, full lips, alluring eyes; lush flesh on a moderate or small bone structure. Differs from Ingenue (which is *youthful* yin rather than *sensual* yin) and from Ethereal (which is yin but *waif-like and translucent*). Styling: draped, fitted, body-celebrating cuts; ruching; luxurious tactile fabrics (silk, satin, velvet); florals; ornate jewellery.

**6. Ingenue (cute, soft yin).** Petite or moderate height; round or heart-shaped face; large wide-set eyes; bowed lips; delicate rounded bone structure; youthful aura. Kibbe rejected this as redundant for adults; Kitchener restored it. Differs from Gamine (Gamine is sharp-small, Ingenue is round-small) and from Romantic (Romantic is mature-sensual, Ingenue is youthful-sweet). Styling: small-scale florals, lace, ruffles, bows, vintage silhouettes, ribbon-and-pearl ornamentation.

**7. Ethereal (most yin; Kitchener's 1987 addition).** Tall and slender; long graceful proportions; pale or translucent skin; hooded or almond eyes; delicate elongated jaw; long neck; "otherworldly" or "fairy-like" quality. Differs from Dramatic in being *delicate rather than commanding* despite shared verticality, and from Romantic in being *fragile rather than fleshly*. Styling: sheer layers, draped column dresses, opalescent and metallic fabrics, soft glimmering makeup, "goddess-wear."

### The 21 Two-Way Blends (Truth-is-Beauty names)

Each blend takes the silhouette of the dominant essence and softens or sharpens it with the secondary. The Truth-is-Beauty mood-board names below are the most widely used; differentiating each from its neighbours is a matter of percentage weight rather than category.

1. **Classic Gamine — "The Prep Schooler."** Balanced symmetrical proportions with a compact, snappy edge; styling is tailored but in petite, contrast-trimmed pieces.
2. **Classic Ingenue — "The Class President."** Symmetrical even proportions softened by rounded youthful features; styling is polished, decorous, decade-spanning.
3. **Dramatic Classic — "The Art Critic."** Long sharp lines tempered by symmetry; styling is elongated tailoring in restrained palette.
4. **Dramatic Gamine — "The Punk Rocker."** Sharp angular features in a compact frame; styling is high-contrast, asymmetric, snappy, edgy.
5. **Dramatic Ingenue — "The Childlike Czarina."** Sharp bone structure with soft rounded flesh and youthful face; styling juxtaposes structure with sweetness.
6. **Dramatic Natural — "The Amazon Queen."** Tall, broad, angular; long blunt lines; styling is unconstructed but powerful, long coats and column silhouettes.
7. **Ethereal Classic — "The Delicate Sophisticate."** Elongated delicacy with symmetry; styling is column gowns in muted palette.
8. **Ethereal Dramatic — "The Sorceress."** Sharp verticality with translucent fragility; styling is long, sweeping, otherworldly.
9. **Ethereal Gamine — "The Sprite."** Compact, sharp, fairy-like; styling is small-scale ethereal — sheer cropped layers, fae-like accessories.
10. **Ethereal Ingenue — "The Fairy."** Delicate, youthful, fragile; styling is gauze, lace, pastels, small floral prints.
11. **Ethereal Natural — "The Earth Goddess."** Broad relaxed frame with otherworldly delicacy; styling is flowing natural-fibre robes, draped column shapes.
12. **Gamine Ingenue — "The Girlish Mod."** Compact figure with rounded youthful features; styling is mod, snappy, sweet-spirited.
13. **Natural Classic — "The Prep."** Broad blunt structure tempered by symmetry; styling is country-classic, equestrian, polished.
14. **Natural Gamine — "The Tomboy."** Broad athletic frame in compact scale; styling is utilitarian, menswear-inspired, mixed textures.
15. **Natural Ingenue — "The Outdoorsy Sweetheart."** Broad athletic with soft round face; styling pairs casual outdoor pieces with feminine softening.
16. **Romantic Classic — "The Sexy Sophisticate."** Lush curves with even symmetry; styling is tailored but body-celebrating — wrap dresses, fitted suits.
17. **Romantic Dramatic — "The Vamp."** Sharp narrow bone structure clothed in lush flesh; the classic "soft dramatic" femme fatale.
18. **Romantic Ethereal — "Aphrodite."** Lush curves on a tall, fragile frame; styling is goddess-gown territory.
19. **Romantic Gamine — "The Firecracker."** Curvy in compact scale; styling is fitted, short-line, high-contrast, playfully sensual.
20. **Romantic Ingenue — "The Demure Seductress."** Lush curves with youthful sweetness; styling is fitted but covered, lace and florals over a curvy line.
21. **Romantic Natural — "The Babe Next Door."** Broad athletic frame softened by lush flesh; styling is body-celebrating but relaxed.

Together with the 7 pure essences, that is the **28**.

## 6. Neighbouring Expanded Systems Worth Knowing

**Kibbe (1987), 13 image identities.** Five families — Dramatic, Natural, Classic, Gamine, Romantic — with eight subtypes (Soft Dramatic, Flamboyant Natural, Soft Natural, Dramatic Classic, Soft Classic, Flamboyant Gamine, Soft Gamine, Theatrical Romantic). In 2024–2025 Kibbe retired the three "pure" balanced types (Natural, Classic, Gamine), leaving 10 in his current framework, and in his 2025 book *The Power of Style* he writes "NO BODY PARTS," emphasising *Personal Line* sketching rather than physical classification. Axes: yin/yang and contrast/blended. Not couture.

**Trinny & Susannah (2007), 12 shapes.** Apple, Brick, Goblet, Hourglass, Vase, Cello, Pear, Skittle, Bell, Column, Lollipop, Cornet. Grouped by whether shoulders/hips/waist define the silhouette. Not couture; British TV-styling.

**Dwyn Larson, 20 Types of Beauty (women) / 32 total.** A geometric expansion of Kibbe along proportions, angles, lines, and contrast. Often credited in Russophone styling circles as more accurate than Kibbe for analytical typing.

**Ellie-Jean Royden's Body Matrix, 27 types (3×3×3).** Width × Length × Shape, with each axis taking three values. The closest *single integer* to 28 but explicitly 27, and explicitly *not* a couture system — Royden positions it as a refinement of Kibbe for online clients.

**Aly Art's videos.** Have made Kibbe famous on YouTube but did not invent a new typology — she explains Kibbe's 13.

**Truth-is-Beauty's three-way blends.** If you extend the count to include three-essence mixes you get a further 35 identities (e.g., Romantic-Ethereal-Dramatic) and a total of 63 — too many to be the "28" the user has in mind.

## 7. What the Couture World Actually Does (and Why It Isn't 28)

Bespoke ateliers work from individual measurements and posture analysis, not typologies. The variables actually used in couture and made-to-measure fitting are:

- **Posture** — erect/military, sway-back, stooped, forward-shoulder, balanced;
- **Shoulder slope and symmetry** — sloping, square, uneven, forward-rolled;
- **Back shape** — straight, rounded, hollow;
- **Bust** — projection, fullness, asymmetry, position;
- **Waist definition** — defined, undefined, high, low;
- **Hip prominence and pelvic tilt**.

French ateliers also work from the **5–6 silhouette letters** (A, H, V, X, O, 8) as a first-pass categorisation before personalised drafting. ESMOD and Bunka use **drop-type charts** (difference between bust and hip circumferences) and **posture inclination N-/S-types** for block-pattern adjustment. The Chambre Syndicale of haute couture imposes no body taxonomy at all — its rules concern atelier staffing, the percentage of hand work, and the number of designs presented per season. The Worth–Dior–Balenciaga–Vionnet–Grès lineage is documented in extensive scholarship (Palmer, Steele, Pochna, Mendes & de la Haye) with no reference to a numbered female-body taxonomy.

In short: the couture world does individualised draping and pattern-cutting precisely *because* archetypal classification is too coarse to serve its purposes. The "28 archetypes" framing is therefore conceptually foreign to couture.

## 8. Recommendations

**If the user wants to *use* a 28-archetype system right now,** the best operational match is the **Truth-is-Beauty (Rachel Nachmias) framework** at truth-is-beauty.com. Begin with the **Style ID Calculator** and read the "Blends of 2 Types" page; this gives the 7 pure + 21 two-way = 28 named identities. Threshold to change recommendation: if percentage outputs show a third essence above ~20%, move to her three-way blend boards (then the framework is 63, not 28).

**If the user wants a system explicitly grounded in *body lines* rather than essence/vibe**, switch to **Ellie-Jean Royden's Body Matrix** (27 types, 3×3×3 grid at bodyandstyle.com). Use it in tandem with style essences rather than instead of them; Royden herself does this.

**If the user wants the historically deepest and most theoretically clean version**, read McJimsey's *Art in Clothing Selection* (1963) or *Art and Fashion in Clothing Selection* (1973) for the original six-archetype framework and Kitchener's writings/videos at Personal Style Counselors for the addition of the Ethereal and the percentage approach. These are the academic roots.

**If the user specifically wants a *couture* tradition**, abandon the 28 framing. The couture tradition is per-person draping. The best entry points are Helen Joseph-Armstrong's *Patternmaking for Fashion Design*, Winifred Aldrich's *Metric Pattern Cutting for Women's Wear*, and the Bunka Fashion College pattern-drafting series. For French atelier-specific morphology language, look at the 5–6 silhouette letters (A, H, V, X, O, 8) used in French styling guides.

**If the user is researching for content creation**, flag the misattribution explicitly. Marketing "28 couture archetypes" as if it were a Dior/Balenciaga tradition would propagate a falsehood; positioning it as an extension of the Northrup–McJimsey–Kitchener–Nachmias image-consulting lineage is accurate and defensible.

## 9. Caveats

- The "28 = 7 + 21" identification is our best inference. Neither Kitchener nor Nachmias headlines "28" as a canonical count; we constructed it from their published material. If the user encountered a specific source that says "28," that source may be either (a) miscounting, (b) doing the same combinatoric we did and labelling it "28," or (c) referring to a private or proprietary system we could not locate.
- A small number of niche Russophone, Korean, and Japanese personal-styling blogs and Etsy/Patreon offerings claim large numbered taxonomies; we could not verify any of them as descending from a real couture house.
- Kibbe's own framework changed materially in 2024–2025: he now states "NO BODY PARTS" in *The Power of Style* (2025) and emphasises *Personal Line* sketching rather than physical classification. So even the Kibbe baseline is in flux.
- Body shape can shift with hormonal change, weight, and age; even the best taxonomy is a moment-in-time read.
- We have flagged the couture provenance as unsupported by sources. If a user produces a specific book, course URL, or atelier name claiming a 28-type system, the analysis here should be re-run against that primary source.
