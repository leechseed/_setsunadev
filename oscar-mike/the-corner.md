# Chair Reference

Private shopping reference. Budget ceiling: ~$3,000. Indoor, corner of office room.

**Core requirements:** breathable (no sticking), wipeable/washable, no upholstery anxiety,
armless or open for spread/display posture, reads as design or equipment — not as sex furniture.

---

## The two big insights

**1. Don't shop "sex furniture." Shop bodywork and gym equipment.**
Massage and Pilates gear is built for bare skin, oils, and being wiped down between clients.
Non-porous by requirement, load-rated, infinitely adjustable, and 1/4 the price of the
purpose-branded equivalent. It also doesn't look like anything.

**2. Two layers beats one perfect material.**
Waterproof non-porous base (vinyl / mesh / coated fabric) + washable terry cover on top.
Strip, wash, swap. This is how every professional solves it. Stop hunting for one magic fabric.

## Materials cheat sheet

| Material | Breathes | Wipes | Sticky when hot | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Textilene / Batyline mesh** | ✅✅ | ✅ | No | **Best for a lounge chair.** Fluids pass through |
| Woven vinyl cord (Acapulco) | ✅✅ | ✅ | No | Great, but marks bare skin |
| Ultrafabrics **Brisa** / Ultraleather Pro | ✅ | ✅✅ | No | Only leather-alike that breathes. COM this |
| PU vinyl (massage / gym / Pilates) | ❌ | ✅✅ | Yes | Fine — *with* a terry cover over it |
| Sunbrella + reticulated quick-dry foam | ~ | ✅ | ~ | Water passes through foam, dries inside-out |
| Pigmented leather | ❌ | ✅ | **Very** | The "love seat" problem. Avoid |
| Aniline leather / nubuck | ❌ | ❌ | Yes | Stains permanently. Hard no |
| Normal upholstery fabric | ✅ | ❌ | No | Hard no |

**COM** = Customer's Own Material. Most makers will upholster in fabric you supply.
This is how you get a design-classic silhouette in a hospital-grade textile.

## A. The lounge chair — sit, recline, spread, display

Geometry that matters: **no arms** (or low enough to hook a knee over), **seat width 26"+**,
**recline 115–130°**, **seat height 14–17"**, a **front rail/edge** for heels,
and **no tufting, buttons, or piped seams** — crevices are where cleaning fails.

| Pick | Material | Why | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Knoll Schultz 1966 lounge chair** | Coated polyester mesh / aluminum | Breathes hard, wipes, no foam. MoMA collection | $1,800–2,400 |
| Knoll Schultz 1966 **chaise** | same | Adjustable recline. Bigger footprint | $2,800–3,500 |
| Kettal / Tribù / Dedon Batyline lounge | Batyline mesh | Higher-end sling, better tension | $1,500–3,000 |
| **Generic Textilene sling lounger** | Textilene mesh | Same physics, 1/5 the price | $200–600 |
| Acapulco chair | Woven vinyl cord | Fluids fall through. Sprawl in any direction | $300–700 |
| BKF / Butterfly chair | Canvas or leather sling | Covers removable + swappable ($100–200 ea) | $400–1,500 |
| Outdoor lounge — Gloster, Kettal, Tribù, RH Outdoor | Sunbrella + quick-dry foam | Most *comfortable* worry-free option. Filter for armless | $800–2,500 |

**Avoid:** Eames Lounge (arms + wings constrain), Womb chair (absorbent),
LC4 (only ~22" wide), anything tufted.

## C. The purpose-built sex-furniture category — honest read

| Product | Build | Real problems |
|---|---|---|
| **Liberator Esse** ($600–900) | Genuinely decent — high-density foam, moisture barrier under a removable washable cover | Microfiber runs **hot** and absorbs. Black Label vinyl is sticky. Instantly recognizable |
| **The Tantra Chair** ($1,200–2,500) | Wood frame, leather or microfiber, several sizes (Baby / Grand / Zen / Nuo) | Reads more as furniture — but leather is the exact warm-and-sticky failure. Still legible to anyone who's seen one |
| Amazon / generic wave chaises ($150–400) | Low-density foam, thin covers | Compress permanently. Actually are what you assumed |

**Verdict:** the *form* is real and ergonomically correct. The *materials* fail your criteria,
and the *legibility* fails your aesthetic. Buy the curve from Pilates instead.
