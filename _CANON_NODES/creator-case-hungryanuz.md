## 5 · Growth tactics (observed)

- **Hub-as-Official-Site.** After the ban, the tube profile's single outbound link became the Linktree, not any one store — the funnel survives the loss of any single platform.
- **Three stores at once, seeded in a burst.** ManyVids opened with 13 clips in three days and 21 in seven weeks; Fansly and unlockd were live within the same window. The catalog looked alive on day one.
- **Low entry price.** $1.99 is the modal clip price; nothing free, nothing on sale — volume of cheap SKUs over a few premium ones (a single $17.99 and $13.99 clip).
- **The ban as copy.** The Pornhub bio turns the deplatforming into a call to action ("DO NOT subscribe to my onlyfans… CLICK OFFICIAL SITE LINKS") — unchanged for four years, so it is the one line every visitor reads.
- **Customs in every bio** from the first capture to the last.
- **Passive tube tail.** Zero uploads for 14 months still returned +0.8K subs and +183K views — the verified page keeps working after the operator stops.
- **No social layer at all** — no X, Reddit or Instagram — which is also why growth is linear and slow (§8).

## 10 · TTP

**The one mechanic.** The single thing worth studying is the **ban-to-hub rebuild**: when the paid platform disappeared, the creator did not replace it with another single platform — they inserted a link hub between the free tube page and the paid layer, opened three stores at once behind it, seeded the first store with 21 clips in seven weeks at a $1.99 modal price, and rewrote the one piece of copy they control (the tube bio) to route everyone to the hub. The record proves the sequence to the day: OF still linked 2021-10-22 → Linktree 2021-11-03 → 13 ManyVids clips by 11-06 → Fansly by 12-19 → unlockd as the fourth link. The tube page then did what a verified tube page does — kept adding subscribers and views with no further work — while the stores went quiet. It is a small operator's platform-risk hedge, executed fast, and it is the only reason this handle has a funnel today.

**Transferable tips.**
- Put the hub, not a platform, in the "Official Site" slot on every free surface from day one — a ban should cost you one link edit, not the funnel.
- Read the paid platform's content rules against your actual category before the first upload; choose the store whose ToS fits, not the store with the most traffic.
- Seed a new store in a burst (a dozen SKUs in the first week) at a low modal price so the catalog reads as alive; add the premium SKUs after.
- Treat the free verified tube page as the durable asset and keep it verified — it returned +0.8K subscribers on zero uploads over 14 months.
- Write the platform change into your bio copy as a call to action and leave it there; the bio is the one ad you own on someone else's site.

**Does not transfer.** The reason the pivot was needed — content extreme enough to be banned by OnlyFans — is also the reason the stores (ManyVids, Fansly, unlockd) were the right ones, and a zero-budget operator running a disclosed rendered persona has the mirror-image problem: not extremity but synthetic-content rules, and a **Pornhub Verified Model page is not available to a rendered persona at all** (verification requires a real person), so the durable free asset at the centre of this case does not exist for that lane. The absence of any social layer here is a warning, not a model: the creator's growth was linear because discovery was tube-only, and a persona with no tube page and no social layer has no discovery at all. And nothing in this record prices the stores — 31 clips and 65 Fansly followers is a catalog, not a business, and no earnings exist to benchmark against.
