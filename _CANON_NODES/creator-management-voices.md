# CREATOR-MANAGEMENT VOICES

**Who this is for.** A solo operator going creator/studio-first who wants to grow a tiered agency out of a network. **What it is.** The people who actually became managers, agency owners, studio owners or house-builders for online adult creators, in their own words, from podcasts, forums, support communities and press — plus what the creators they signed say about them.

---

## 0 · Bottom line

- **Nobody credible started with a roster. They started with one relationship.** A friend ("The first time I started, I made $40 by helping out a friend"), a creator they already worked for (Vic, five months from chatter to first full client), a producer's network (Spiegler: "you know everybody, can you help her get work?"), or their own assistants (Amouranth).
- **Creator-first operators sell the thing they built for themselves first.** Amouranth sold their assistant team; Lucy Banks sold the PR muscle nobody would sell them; Lydia Love sells the training they lacked; MelRose Michaels sells "the room I wish I had"; Sophie Rain sold cross-promotion. The product existed before the first signee.
- **The commission number is the ethics map.** Licensed agents 10–15% (Spiegler; Hussie's contract 15%). Creators' own "standard" for a manager: 5–15% (Rothfield). OFM reality: 25% (Unruly) → "commonly 50%" (BBC 2026) → 60–70% (NJAC clients; BBC contracts).
- **Account control is the line.** Every exploitation story runs through logins, email or bank control — and the course-sellers teach exactly that ("you do have control over the bank accounts"). The operators who last say the opposite: no retainers, 30-day exits, creator keeps the keys.
- **Chatters are the engine and the exposure.** "50 to 60 percent of their earnings through messaging" (Kamal); AI chat now sits behind half of one agency's roster (NEO); class actions over undisclosed chatters are set for trial in 2027.
- **Houses break on control, not money.** Bop House co-founder left after seven months: "began to feel controlling".
- **Survivors diversified early.** "relying on one big creator nearly ruined us" (TDM); "No single-platform dependency" (OFagency).
- **Course-sellers outnumber operators.** The tell: they sell to would-be managers, not to creators.

## 2 · Creator-first → roster: the specific pattern

- **The cases:** Amouranth (assistants → Real Work) · Lucy Banks (creator → PR/talent agency) · Sophie Rain & Aishah Sofey (creators → house) · Lydia Love (cam model → studio) · MelRose Michaels (creator → community/mastermind) · Amberly Rothfield (creator → consultant) · Spiegler (producer network → agency; adjacent).
- **What had to exist before anyone signed:**
  - **Own proof of earnings.** Amouranth's $1.5M/month; Banks's $1M; Rain's $43M year; Love's decade on cam. Nobody signed with a peer who had not already won.
  - **A working asset, not a promise.** Amouranth's trained assistants ("the same services to other creators"), Banks's press contacts built for themselves, Love's onboarding stack (1:1 calls, mentors, chat channels), Michaels's community of 2.6k before The Boardroom, Rain's viral TikTok before the open call.
  - **A gap the creator felt personally.** "nobody would work with me because I was an OnlyFans creator" (Banks); "the knowledge I did not have access to" (Love); "the room I wish I had" (Michaels).
- **What they charged:**
  - Real Work — undisclosed; managers as PAs (Insider).
  - Million Billion Media — undisclosed; refuses "small deals".
  - Bop House — no commission on record; shared costs ($75k → $100k rent) and shared audience.
  - IGF Studios — split undisclosed; non-exclusive; weekly payouts; six-month post-exit restriction.
  - CEO Society free; Boardroom paid, capped, "pricing increases" per cohort.
  - Rothfield — hourly consults; no percentage.
- **What broke:**
  - Real Work restructured within four months: "staffing changes that reflect my renewed priorities and focus."
  - Bop House lost its co-founder in seven months: "certain members wanting to dictate how I should act, post, and live."
  - The creator-first model scales by turning people away (Spiegler: "We turn down over two-hundred girls a year"; Banks: no small deals) — growth is capped by the founder's attention.
- **Informal precedent (Prism, 2024-01-08):** "paid assisting work is a common practice in the field, where one creator aids other creators with the scheduling of posts" — the smallest creator-first roster is one friend paying you to schedule.

## 3 · Operator-first: the specific pattern

- **The cases:** Vic (chatter job) · Unruly (marketing + finance) · TDM (fitness → chat shifts) · OFagency (writer) · NEO · Oscar Gracie (YouTube) · Creators Inc (ad-tech) · NJAC/Lolly (Instagram growth) · reddit manager (friend) · liam900 (never launched).
- **Where the first client came from:**
  - **An existing job or network** — Vic was hired as a chatter, then signed a full client five months later; Unruly's first 10 clients came through the founders' network; Gracie's first adult client came through one UK agency owner.
  - **A friend** — "$40 by helping out a friend"; liam900's two creators they had already shot with.
  - **Cold outreach at volume** — Plummer's 100 messages/day; the method creators identify as spam ("three or four of these kinds of messages a day", Grace, Prism).
- **What they offered first:** a single function — chat (Vic, TDM), promotion (Gracie), scheduling (reddit manager), socials at 10% (liam900) — before "full management".
- **How they priced:** revenue share with no retainer (OFagency), 25% + one-year term (Unruly), 30% (reddit manager), sliding 5–30% plus fixed fee proposed (liam900), 60–70% reported (NJAC).
- **What broke:** one-creator dependence (TDM: "nearly ruined us"); recruits' expectations ("He promised that I would be in the top one percent in a few months" — Lora on NJAC); lock-in contracts turning into lawsuits (Unruly, NJAC); never starting (liam900: "changing jobs in 3 weeks which will enable me much more time").
- **Structural tell of a durable operator:** dedicated account manager per creator, 30-day exit, creator keeps logins, published thresholds for when the service pays for itself (TDM's "25+ paid subs per day").
