# THE STANDARDS BODY OF ASTRO7EX

**The one-line read (⧗).** The body that certifies what may be eaten aboard is a life-support authority before it is a kitchen, and it charges every person up there the price of proving they fed themselves.

## ⧗ The body: who issues, who enforces, who is bound

Two candidate readings.

**A. A life-support authority.** The standard is issued by whatever office already tracks air, water, and power reserves, because a bad seal or an uncontained ferment is a life-support failure, not a culinary one. Enforcement is mechanical: a batch fails a test or it does not. This reading treats cuisine as downstream of the same office that already has to say shall.

**B. A preservers' guild.** The standard is issued by the preservers themselves, a craft body that trains, vouches, and expels, closer to a trade guild than a directorate. Enforcement is social: your batch is trusted because your name is on it, not because an inspector signed off.

**Recommendation: A, with B nested under it.** The prompt's own framing, that a standard here is literally life support, points at the directorate. The guild survives inside it as the social layer, the belonging gradient (S8 HABIT) that decides whose jar you'll actually eat from once the directorate has cleared it.

## The S12 invariant — RULED KEEP 2026-09-15

**RULED 2026-09-15, Chief: KEEP.** ("Go ahead, keep all three.") This line is canon law of the place from this date: every scene card set here obeys it, and a scene that breaks it owes the story a reason.

**Ruled: no person aboard [place, once keyed] eats a ration they did not put up themselves or receive, sealed and named, from a member of their own community.**

It forbids two things at once: food of anonymous origin crossing a table, and food eaten from a seal with no name attached to it. A scene tests it the moment someone offers an unlogged jar, salvaged, traded, or unmarked, and a character eats from it on camera; the story can then charge them exactly what the table above says a preserver or a newcomer pays.
