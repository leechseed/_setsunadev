## 10 · TTP

**The one mechanic.** Brand-to-studio conversion, with distribution borrowed. The single thing worth studying is how she turned a performer brand that already had ~135K followers and a verified tube profile (2019) into the *name* of a studio-plus-agency (Nov 2020), then put the studio's output through channels she did not have to build: an aggregator-studio that assigns per-scene codes (AnalVids/LegalPorno, NT010 → NT082), a free tube channel with a paid tier (XVideos, 455 free + 474 RED), and a white-label fansite (Sheer). The roster's scenes carry her name in the title string, so every release the studio makes markets her brand, and her own on-camera volume (49 credits in 2021 → 8 in 2025) could fall without the audience falling — the studio channel added +91K subscribers and +269M views in the three years her personal output halved. The two-principal split (partner: direction / production / talent hunting; her: "vocational training") is stated in one sentence on Sheer, which is the closest thing to a business plan the whole record contains.

**Transferable tips.**
- Name the venture after the audience you already have; do not launch a second brand from zero when the first one is the asset.
- Do not build a paysite first — put output into an existing marketplace that assigns you a code and a page (the AnalVids studio page is the studio's catalogue), and run a free tube channel with a paid tier as the funnel.
- Open the venture's own social account and channel on day one, separate from the personal hub; keep bookings on the personal account by DM.
- Write the division of labour down in public in one sentence (who directs, who trains, who scouts) — it is the cheapest credibility signal an unknown studio can buy.
- Turn roster onboarding into product: introductions and "casting by" titles are releases, not overhead.

**Does not transfer.** Almost everything that made the launch cheap is pre-existing: two years of European studio bookings (Woodman → LetsDoeIt → Her Limit) that built the audience; a Medellín cost base and a local studio ecosystem (Yummy Estudio and the webcam-studio economy) that supplies talent; a partner who already had AnalVids and Evil Angel credits — that is the distribution relationship, and it is not available to a zero-budget operator with no roster and no partner inside a distributor. The hardcore-niche marketplace pays per scene at volumes a small studio can hit; a curated or masked lane has no equivalent buyer. And the compliance load of running other people's on-camera work (records, consent, payments across a roster of fourteen) is real overhead that nothing in this record prices.

**Feeds.** **The manager/agency path** — primary: the first roster case on the register with a documented division of labour and a measurable studio channel, and a caution that "agency" here means production-plus-training, with no public terms. **The studio launch (BOLO 24)** — the naming rule and the borrow-distribution-first sequence. **The masked-performer Lab venture** — her stated role ("vocational training") is a coaching seat that does not require being on camera, which is the seat a masked operator can hold. **The curation funnel (BOLO 27 / ULTRA DARK)** — weak; only the XVideos series is useful, as a benchmark for what a free channel with a paid tier does over three years.
